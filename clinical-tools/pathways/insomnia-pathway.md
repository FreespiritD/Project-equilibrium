# Insomnia Clinical Pathway - EQUILIBRIUM

**Version**: 2.0
**Last Updated**: October 2025
**Evidence Base**: CBT-I is first-line treatment (Grade A evidence)

## Quick Reference

| Presentation | First-Line | Second-Line | Avoid |
|--------------|------------|-------------|-------|
| **Chronic Insomnia** | CBT-I (digital or therapist-delivered) | Medication if CBT-I unavailable/failed | Long-term hypnotics |
| **Short-term Insomnia** | Sleep hygiene + stimulus control | CBT-I if persists >4 weeks | Routine hypnotic prescription |
| **Comorbid Depression** | Treat both (CBT-I + depression treatment) | Monitor closely | Hypnotics alone |

**Key Principle**: CBT-I > medication (better efficacy, no side effects, no dependency, effects persist)

---

## Why Insomnia Matters for Mental Health

### Bidirectional Relationship

**Insomnia → Depression/Anxiety**:
- Insomnia doubles risk of developing depression
- Persistent insomnia predicts depression onset 6-12 months later
- Mechanism: Sleep deprivation impairs emotion regulation, increases negative thinking

**Depression/Anxiety → Insomnia**:
- 80% of depression cases have sleep disturbance
- Rumination, anxiety prevent sleep onset
- Early morning awakening common in depression

**Clinical implication**: **Treat insomnia even when comorbid with depression**
- Improves depression outcomes
- Reduces relapse risk
- Often resolves residual depressive symptoms

## Step 1: Assessment

### A. Insomnia Diagnosis

**DSM-5 Criteria**:
- Difficulty initiating sleep, maintaining sleep, or early morning awakening
- Sleep difficulty ≥3 nights/week for ≥3 months
- Daytime impairment (fatigue, mood, concentration, performance)
- Opportunity for adequate sleep (not due to external constraints)

**Types**:
- **Sleep onset insomnia**: Difficulty falling asleep (>30 min)
- **Sleep maintenance insomnia**: Frequent awakenings or difficulty returning to sleep
- **Early morning awakening**: Waking 2+ hours before desired, unable to return to sleep
- **Mixed**: Combination

### B. Sleep History

**Use assessment tool**: `assessment-tools/sleep-quality-assessment.md`

**Quantify**:
- **Sleep onset latency**: Time from lights out to sleep (normal <30 min)
- **Wake after sleep onset (WASO)**: Total time awake during night (normal <30 min)
- **Total sleep time**: Actual sleep (not time in bed)
- **Sleep efficiency**: (Total sleep time / time in bed) × 100
  - Normal: ≥85%
  - Insomnia: <85%

**Example**:
- Time in bed: 11pm - 7am (8 hours)
- Sleep onset latency: 90 min (fell asleep 12:30am)
- Awakenings: 3× during night, 60 min total
- Total sleep time: 5.5 hours
- Sleep efficiency: 5.5 / 8 = 69% (poor)

**Sleep diary**: 2-week prospective log (more accurate than recall)

**Daytime impact**:
- Fatigue, sleepiness
- Mood (irritability, low mood, anxiety)
- Concentration, memory
- Physical symptoms (headaches, GI issues)
- Functional impairment (work, driving safety)

### C. Differential Diagnosis

**Rule out**:
1. **Sleep apnea**:
   - Loud snoring, witnessed apneas
   - Daytime sleepiness (Epworth Sleepiness Scale >10)
   - Obesity, large neck circumference, hypertension
   - **Refer**: Sleep clinic for polysomnography if suspected
   - **Don't**: Treat as insomnia with CBT-I (won't work, delays diagnosis)

2. **Restless legs syndrome**:
   - Urge to move legs, worse evening/night, relieved by movement
   - **Treatment**: Iron if deficient, dopamine agonists (specialist)

3. **Circadian rhythm disorders**:
   - Delayed sleep phase (can't fall asleep until 2-4am, can't wake until 10am-noon)
   - Shift work disorder
   - **Treatment**: Chronotherapy, light therapy

4. **Narcolepsy**: Excessive daytime sleepiness, cataplexy, sleep paralysis (rare)

5. **Medication/substance-induced**:
   - Stimulants (caffeine, nicotine, cocaine, amphetamines)
   - Medications (steroids, beta-blockers, SSRIs, decongestants)
   - Alcohol (sedating acutely, disrupts sleep architecture, rebound insomnia)

6. **Medical causes**:
   - Pain (arthritis, fibromyalgia)
   - Respiratory (COPD, asthma)
   - Gastrointestinal (reflux)
   - Endocrine (hyperthyroidism, diabetes with nocturia)
   - Neurological (Parkinson's, dementia)

### D. Perpetuating Factors (Target of CBT-I)

**Behavioral**:
- Irregular sleep schedule (varying bed/wake times)
- Excessive time in bed ("trying" to sleep)
- Daytime napping
- Using bed for non-sleep activities (watching TV, working, worrying)
- Clock-watching

**Cognitive**:
- Worry about sleep ("I must get 8 hours or I'll be a disaster tomorrow")
- Catastrophizing ("I'll never sleep again")
- Performance anxiety about sleep
- Attention bias to sleep-related threats

**Physiological**:
- Hyperarousal (conditioned arousal to bedroom)
- Caffeine, alcohol, nicotine
- Lack of wind-down routine
- Light exposure (screens, bright lights before bed)

**Environmental**:
- Bedroom too hot, cold, noisy, bright
- Uncomfortable bed
- Partner snoring

## Step 2: Treatment - Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I)

### Why CBT-I First-Line?

**Evidence**:
- **Efficacy**: 70-80% achieve clinically significant improvement
- **Effect size**: 0.98 (large, superior to medication)
- **Sustainability**: Effects persist >12 months (vs. medication effects stop when stopped)
- **No side effects**: (vs. medication: tolerance, dependency, falls, cognitive impairment)
- **Bonus**: Improves depression, anxiety, pain, QOL

**NICE, American Academy of Sleep Medicine, European Sleep Research Society**: All recommend CBT-I as first-line

### CBT-I Components

**Multicomponent intervention** (all components necessary, not à la carte):

#### 1. Sleep Hygiene Education

**Purpose**: Address environmental and lifestyle factors

**Recommendations**:

**Bedroom environment**:
- Cool (16-18°C)
- Dark (blackout curtains, eye mask)
- Quiet (ear plugs, white noise if needed)
- Comfortable bed

**Daytime**:
- ☕ Limit caffeine:
  - No caffeine after 2pm (half-life 5-6 hours)
  - Some need to eliminate entirely
  - Sources: Coffee, tea, energy drinks, cola, chocolate
- 🚭 Avoid nicotine close to bedtime (stimulant)
- 🍺 Limit alcohol:
  - Sedating acutely but disrupts sleep architecture
  - REM suppression, rebound awakening 3-4 hours later
  - Max 1-2 drinks, ≥3 hours before bed
- 🏃 Exercise regularly:
  - Improves sleep quality, sleep onset latency
  - Best: Morning or afternoon
  - Avoid vigorous exercise within 2-3 hours of bed (arousing)
- ☀️ Light exposure:
  - Bright light during day (regulates circadian rhythm)
  - Dim lights 1-2 hours before bed
  - Avoid screens (blue light suppresses melatonin)
    - If must use: Blue light filters, night mode

**Pre-sleep**:
- Wind-down routine (30-60 min):
  - Relaxing activities (reading, gentle music, bath)
  - NOT: TV, work, arguments, rumination
- Avoid large meals, spicy food close to bed (reflux)
- Limit fluids 2 hours before bed (nocturia)

**Note**: Sleep hygiene alone is INSUFFICIENT (weak effect size 0.3) - must combine with behavioral components below

#### 2. Stimulus Control

**Purpose**: Re-associate bed with sleep (break conditioned arousal to bed/bedroom)

**Instructions** (give as handout):
1. **Go to bed only when sleepy** (not just tired)
   - Sleepy = heavy eyelids, nodding off, yawning
2. **Use bed only for sleep and sex**
   - NOT: TV, reading, eating, worrying, phone, working
3. **If not asleep within 15-20 minutes** (don't clock-watch, estimate):
   - Get out of bed
   - Go to another room
   - Do something relaxing (dim light, boring book, gentle music)
   - Return to bed when sleepy again
   - Repeat as many times as needed
4. **Get up at the same time every morning**
   - Even weekends, even if slept poorly
   - Use alarm, don't sleep in
5. **No daytime napping**
   - If exhausted, limit to 20-30 min before 3pm

**Rationale** (explain to patient):
- Currently: Bed = struggle, frustration, arousal (conditioned response)
- Goal: Bed = sleep (re-condition)
- Lying awake in bed strengthens bed-arousal association
- Getting up breaks this association
- Consistency strengthens sleep drive

**Common concern**: "But I'll be exhausted getting up repeatedly!"
- **Answer**: Yes, short-term (1-2 weeks). But sleep drive builds, makes falling asleep easier. Lying in bed awake doesn't help anyway, just increases frustration.

#### 3. Sleep Restriction (Sleep Compression)

**Purpose**: Consolidate sleep, increase sleep drive, improve sleep efficiency

**Most powerful CBT-I component** (but hardest for patients)

**Protocol**:

**Week 1**: Calculate prescribed time in bed
1. Average total sleep time from 2-week sleep diary (e.g., 5.5 hours)
2. Add 30 min (= 6 hours prescribed time in bed)
3. **Minimum**: 5.5 hours (safety, don't go below)
4. Set fixed wake time (e.g., 7am)
5. Calculate bedtime: 7am - 6 hours = 1am bedtime
6. **Go to bed at 1am** (not before), wake 7am every day

**Subsequent weeks**: Adjust based on sleep efficiency
- **If sleep efficiency ≥85%**: Increase time in bed by 15-30 min (earlier bedtime)
- **If sleep efficiency 80-85%**: No change
- **If sleep efficiency <80%**: Decrease time in bed by 15-30 min (later bedtime)
- **Goal**: Sleep efficiency >90%, consolidated sleep

**Rationale**:
- Currently: 8 hours in bed, 5.5 hours asleep = 69% efficiency (fragmented)
- Sleep restriction: 6 hours in bed, 5.5 hours asleep = 92% efficiency (consolidated)
- Builds sleep pressure → fall asleep faster, fewer awakenings
- Gradually extend as sleep consolidates

**Safety warnings**:
- **Daytime sleepiness expected** first 1-2 weeks
- **Do not drive** if severely sleepy (Epworth >15)
- **Not suitable** if: Seizure disorder, bipolar (sleep deprivation trigger), safety-critical job (pilot, surgeon)

**Common concern**: "I can't function on 6 hours sleep!"
- **Answer**: You're already getting 5.5 hours (per diary). This makes it more consolidated and efficient. We'll gradually increase as sleep improves.

#### 4. Cognitive Therapy

**Purpose**: Challenge unhelpful beliefs and worries about sleep

**Common unhelpful thoughts**:
- "I must get 8 hours or I'll be a disaster"
  - **Challenge**: Sleep need varies (7-9 hours). One bad night has minimal impact. Chronic restriction is the problem, not occasional poor sleep.
- "I'll never sleep again"
  - **Challenge**: Catastrophizing. Insomnia is treatable. You've slept before, you will again.
- "If I don't fall asleep soon, tomorrow is ruined"
  - **Challenge**: Pressure to sleep makes it harder. Worrying about not sleeping IS the problem.
- "I can't function without perfect sleep"
  - **Challenge**: You function better than you think on poor sleep. Anxiety about performance is worse than actual impairment.

**Techniques**:
- **Thought records**: Identify automatic thoughts when can't sleep, challenge with evidence
- **Worry time**: Scheduled time (6pm) to write worries, not in bed
- **Mindfulness**: Acceptance of wakefulness reduces struggle and arousal

#### 5. Relaxation Training (Optional Adjunct)

**Progressive muscle relaxation**, **breathing exercises**, **guided imagery**

**Evidence**: Weak as stand-alone, helpful as adjunct to above

**NOT**: Primary treatment (sleep hygiene + relaxation alone insufficient)

### CBT-I Delivery Formats

**Options**:

1. **Face-to-face therapist-delivered**:
   - Gold standard
   - 4-8 sessions, weekly
   - IAPT services offer CBT-I (availability varies)
   - Specialist sleep clinics

2. **Digital CBT-I** (online, app-based):
   - Sleepio (NICE-approved, NHS prescription)
   - Effectiveness: 70-80% (similar to face-to-face)
   - Advantages: Immediate access, lower cost, scalable
   - Self-guided or therapist-supported

3. **Self-help workbooks**:
   - "Overcoming Insomnia" (Espie)
   - Effectiveness lower than guided, but accessible

4. **Group CBT-I**:
   - Cost-effective
   - 4-8 weeks, 6-12 participants

5. **GP/nurse-delivered**:
   - Brief version (2-4 sessions)
   - Focus on stimulus control + sleep restriction
   - Training available (GP can deliver basic CBT-I)

**Recommend**: Digital CBT-I first (Sleepio) - immediate, effective, low-cost

## Step 3: When Medication is Considered

### Indications

**Medication is SECOND-LINE** (after CBT-I tried or if unavailable/unsuitable)

**Appropriate scenarios**:
1. CBT-I unavailable or long wait (medication bridge)
2. CBT-I tried but inadequate response
3. Severe, acute distress (short-term, <2 weeks)
4. Patient unable to engage with CBT-I (cognitive impairment, severe mental illness)

### Medication Options

**No hypnotic is suitable for long-term use** (all have tolerance, dependency, side effects)

| Medication | Dose | Duration | Pros | Cons |
|------------|------|----------|------|------|
| **Zopiclone** | 3.75-7.5mg | <2 weeks | Short-acting, effective | Metallic taste, tolerance rapid |
| **Zolpidem** | 5-10mg | <2 weeks | Fast onset | Tolerance, complex behaviors (sleep-eating, driving) |
| **Temazepam** | 10-20mg | <2 weeks | Effective | Benzodiazepine, dependency, withdrawal |
| **Mirtazapine** | 7.5-15mg | Ongoing (if comorbid depression) | Sedating, treats depression | Weight gain, hangover effect |
| **Trazodone** | 25-100mg | Ongoing (off-license) | Low dependency risk | Priapism (rare), postural hypotension |
| **Melatonin** | 2mg MR | ≥13 weeks (≥55yo only) | Natural, low risk | Weak efficacy, expensive |

### What NOT to Prescribe

❌ **Long-term benzodiazepines/Z-drugs** (>2-4 weeks):
- Tolerance develops (lose efficacy)
- Dependency, withdrawal syndrome
- Cognitive impairment
- Fall risk (especially older adults)
- Rebound insomnia when stopped (worse than original)

❌ **Antihistamines** (diphenhydramine, promethazine):
- Tolerance rapid (3 days)
- Anticholinergic side effects (confusion, falls, urinary retention in older adults)
- Hangover effect
- NOT effective >1 week

❌ **Antipsychotics** (quetiapine off-license):
- Metabolic syndrome, weight gain, diabetes
- Not approved indication
- Reserve for severe mental illness

### Deprescribing Hypnotics

**Many patients on long-term hypnotics** (often started years ago, continued inappropriately)

**Approach**:
1. **Motivational interviewing**: Explore patient's views on medication
   - Benefits? (probably minimal now due to tolerance)
   - Harms? (dependency, worry about running out, side effects)
   - Concerns about stopping? (rebound insomnia)

2. **Offer CBT-I first**:
   - Start CBT-I while still on medication
   - Once sleep improves with CBT-I skills, taper medication
   - **Evidence**: CBT-I + gradual taper more successful than taper alone

3. **Taper slowly** (NOT abrupt):
   - Reduce dose by 25% every 1-2 weeks
   - Example: Zopiclone 7.5mg → 5mg × 2 weeks → 3.75mg × 2 weeks → 1.875mg × 2 weeks → stop
   - **Expect**: Rebound insomnia for 1-2 weeks (temporary, not return of original insomnia)
   - **Support**: CBT-I techniques during taper

4. **Monitor**: Sleep diary, support, troubleshoot difficulties

**Outcome**: 70-80% successfully discontinue with CBT-I support

## Step 4: Comorbid Insomnia + Depression/Anxiety

### Approach: Treat Both Simultaneously

**Old approach** (wrong):
- "Insomnia is symptom of depression, will resolve when depression treated"
- Treat depression only, ignore insomnia
- **Result**: Residual insomnia persists, increases relapse risk

**EQUILIBRIUM approach** (correct):
- **Treat insomnia AND depression/anxiety concurrently**
- **Evidence**:
  - CBT-I improves both insomnia AND depression symptoms
  - Treating insomnia reduces depression relapse risk
  - Faster recovery from depression

**Protocol**:
1. **Assess both** (PHQ-9, GAD-7, sleep diary)
2. **Offer**:
   - CBT-I (digital or therapist)
   - PLUS depression treatment (therapy, medication, or lifestyle per depression pathway)
3. **Monitor both**: PHQ-9 + sleep efficiency

**Medication considerations**:
- **If starting SSRI**: Warn may worsen insomnia first 1-2 weeks
  - Mirtazapine alternative if insomnia severe (sedating)
- **If on hypnotic**: Taper after CBT-I working, not before

## Monitoring and Follow-Up

**Sleep diary**: Continue throughout treatment (track progress)

**Metrics**:
- Sleep onset latency (target <30 min)
- Wake after sleep onset (target <30 min)
- Sleep efficiency (target >85%, ideally >90%)
- Daytime functioning (fatigue, mood, concentration)

**Timeline**:
- **Weeks 1-2**: Often worse (sleep restriction causes sleepiness, stimulus control disrupts routine)
- **Weeks 3-4**: Improvement begins (sleep consolidates)
- **Weeks 6-8**: Significant improvement (most achieve target sleep efficiency)
- **3+ months**: Maintain gains, adjust for setbacks

**Follow-up**:
- Week 2: Check tolerability, adherence, troubleshoot
- Week 4: Assess progress, adjust sleep restriction
- Week 8: Evaluate outcome, plan maintenance
- 3 months: Long-term outcome

## Special Considerations

### Older Adults

**Insomnia very common** (30-50% of older adults)

**Assessment**:
- Screen for sleep apnea (higher prevalence)
- Review medications (many are alerting or sedating)
- Address nocturia (BPH in men, overactive bladder)
- Pain conditions

**Treatment**:
- **CBT-I first-line** (safe, effective at all ages)
- **Avoid hypnotics** especially in older adults:
  - Fall risk (hip fracture = 33% mortality in elders)
  - Cognitive impairment (dementia risk)
  - Polypharmacy interactions

### Shift Workers

**Circadian misalignment** (trying to sleep when body clock says be awake)

**Strategies**:
- Light exposure timing (bright light during night shift, dark sunglasses driving home)
- Strategic napping (20-30 min before shift)
- Melatonin (3-5mg before day sleep)
- CBT-I adapted for shift work

### Pregnancy and Postpartum

**Pregnancy**: Insomnia common (hormonal, physical discomfort, nocturia)
- **Treatment**: CBT-I safe, no medication
- Sleep hygiene, sleep restriction modified (shorter restriction period)

**Postpartum**: Infant care disrupts sleep
- Realistic expectations (fragmented sleep inevitable first months)
- CBT-I techniques when able (stimulus control, sleep restriction NOT appropriate if feeding every 2-3 hours)
- Address postpartum depression if present

## Key Messages for Patients

**Use patient materials**: `patient-materials/sleep-hygiene-guide.pdf`

1. **"CBT-I works better than pills"** - 70-80% success, effects last, no side effects
2. **"Sleep restriction feels worse before better"** - First 1-2 weeks hard, but builds sleep drive
3. **"Get out of bed if not sleeping"** - Don't lie awake (breaks bed-arousal association)
4. **"Keep consistent wake time"** - Even if slept poorly, even weekends (stabilizes rhythm)
5. **"Cut caffeine after lunch"** - Major sleep disruptor, often overlooked
6. **"Worry time is not bed time"** - Deal with worries earlier, not when trying to sleep

## Clinical Pearls

✅ **DO**:
- Offer CBT-I first-line (better than medication, no downsides)
- Use digital CBT-I (Sleepio) if face-to-face unavailable
- Treat insomnia even when comorbid with depression (improves both)
- Deprescribe long-term hypnotics with CBT-I support
- Address caffeine and alcohol (common culprits)

❌ **DON'T**:
- Prescribe long-term hypnotics (tolerance, dependency, harms)
- Rely on sleep hygiene alone (weak, must add behavioral components)
- Miss sleep apnea (refer if suspected - CBT-I won't work)
- Tell patients insomnia is "just anxiety/depression" (it's a disorder that requires treatment)
