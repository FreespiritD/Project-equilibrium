# Anxiety Disorders Clinical Pathway - EQUILIBRIUM

**Version**: 2.0
**Last Updated**: October 2025
**Aligned with**: NICE CG113 (Generalised Anxiety Disorder and Panic Disorder, 2019)
**Evidence Base**: See `docs/01-evidence-base/systematic-reviews/`

## Quick Reference

| Condition | First-Line | Second-Line | Specialist Referral |
|-----------|------------|-------------|---------------------|
| **GAD (Generalized Anxiety)** | Psychoeducation + low-intensity CBT | High-intensity CBT OR SSRI | Not responding 12 weeks |
| **Panic Disorder** | Psychoeducation + self-help | CBT OR SSRI | Severe, not responding |
| **Social Anxiety** | CBT (individual best) | SSRI if CBT unavailable/declined | OCD-like severity |
| **Health Anxiety** | CBT (specialized) | SSRI if severe | Complex, not responding |

**❌ Avoid**: Benzodiazepines except short-term crisis (<2 weeks)

## Step 1: Assessment

### A. Screening and Recognition

**Common presentations**:
- Excessive worry, can't control worrying (GAD)
- Panic attacks (sudden surges of anxiety with physical symptoms)
- Fear of social situations, scrutiny (social anxiety)
- Fear of specific objects/situations (phobias)
- Obsessive thoughts + compulsive behaviors (OCD - see separate pathway)

**Screen with GAD-7**:
- 7 questions, score 0-21
- ≥8: Probable anxiety disorder → full assessment
- ≥15: Severe anxiety

### B. Diagnostic Assessment

**Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)**:
- Excessive worry about multiple issues (work, health, relationships)
- Difficult to control worrying
- Present most days ≥6 months
- Associated symptoms:
  - Restlessness, feeling on edge
  - Easily fatigued
  - Difficulty concentrating
  - Irritability
  - Muscle tension
  - Sleep disturbance

**Panic Disorder**:
- Recurrent unexpected panic attacks
- Worry about having more attacks OR consequences (heart attack, going crazy)
- Avoidance of situations where attacks occurred
- **Panic attack** = Abrupt surge of fear/discomfort, peak within 10 minutes, with ≥4 of:
  - Palpitations, racing heart
  - Sweating
  - Trembling
  - Shortness of breath, feeling smothered
  - Choking sensation
  - Chest pain
  - Nausea
  - Dizziness, feeling faint
  - Chills or heat sensations
  - Numbness, tingling (paresthesias)
  - Derealization (unreality) or depersonalization (detachment)
  - Fear of losing control or "going crazy"
  - Fear of dying

**Social Anxiety Disorder**:
- Marked fear of social situations where scrutinized by others
- Fear of humiliation, embarrassment, negative evaluation
- Social situations almost always provoke anxiety
- Avoidance OR endured with intense distress
- Impacts functioning (work, education, relationships)
- ≥6 months duration

**Specific Phobia**:
- Marked fear of specific object/situation
- Avoidance or intense distress
- Out of proportion to actual danger
- ≥6 months

### C. Differential Diagnosis

**Medical causes** (rule out):
- Hyperthyroidism (tremor, palpitations, weight loss)
- Cardiac arrhythmias (palpitations, especially if older)
- Hypoglycemia (diabetes)
- Respiratory (asthma, COPD)
- Pheochromocytoma (rare but serious)

**Medication/substance-induced**:
- Stimulants (caffeine, energy drinks, cocaine, amphetamines)
- Medication side effects (salbutamol, corticosteroids, levothyroxine)
- Alcohol/sedative withdrawal

**Psychiatric**:
- Depression (comorbid in 60%)
- PTSD (trauma-related, intrusive memories)
- OCD (obsessions + compulsions)
- Psychosis (persecutory delusions can present as anxiety)

### D. Biopsychosocial Assessment

**Use template**: `assessment-tools/biopsychosocial-assessment-template.md`

**Biological**:
- Caffeine intake (coffee, energy drinks - common trigger)
- Alcohol use (anxiolytic short-term, rebound anxiety)
- Sleep (insomnia perpetuates anxiety)
- Exercise (low activity = higher anxiety)
- Medical conditions

**Psychological**:
- Cognitive patterns:
  - Overestimation of threat ("something terrible will happen")
  - Catastrophizing
  - Intolerance of uncertainty
  - Perfectionism
- Safety behaviors (subtle avoidance that maintains anxiety)
- Coping strategies (helpful vs. unhelpful)

**Social**:
- Life stressors (work demands, relationship conflict, financial)
- Social support (isolation worsens anxiety)
- Trauma history (especially for panic, social anxiety)

**Functional impact**:
- Work/education affected?
- Social life restricted?
- Avoidance patterns?

## Step 2: Severity-Guided Treatment

### Mild GAD (GAD-7: 8-12)

**First-Line: Psychoeducation + Lifestyle + Self-Help**

**Approach**:

1. **Psychoeducation**:
   - Explain anxiety (adaptive in real danger, maladaptive when excessive/chronic)
   - Fight-or-flight response (physical symptoms are not dangerous)
   - Vicious cycle:
     - Threat perception → Anxiety → Physical symptoms → Misinterpretation as danger → More anxiety
   - Role of avoidance in maintaining anxiety (short-term relief, long-term worsening)

2. **Lifestyle modifications**:
   - **Reduce caffeine**: Major trigger (coffee, tea, energy drinks, cola)
     - Recommend <200mg/day (2 cups coffee) or eliminate
   - **Limit alcohol**: Rebound anxiety after acute anxiolytic effect
   - **Regular sleep**: Sleep deprivation lowers anxiety threshold
     - Use: `patient-materials/sleep-hygiene-guide.pdf`
   - **Exercise**: Anxiolytic effects, reduces physiological arousal
     - 150 min/week moderate activity
     - Yoga particularly effective for anxiety (mind-body component)
     - Use: `patient-materials/exercise-for-mental-health.pdf`
   - **Diet**: Blood sugar stability (avoid skipping meals, reduce refined sugars)

3. **Low-intensity psychological intervention**:
   - **Guided self-help**: Online CBT, workbooks
   - **Psychoeducation groups**: IAPT Step 2
   - **Applied relaxation**: Progressive muscle relaxation, breathing exercises
     - BUT: Not stand-alone (must combine with cognitive/exposure work)

4. **Monitor**: GAD-7 every 4 weeks
   - If improving: Continue, extend follow-up
   - If not improving 8 weeks: Step up

**Second-Line**: High-intensity CBT OR SSRI (patient choice)

---

### Moderate GAD (GAD-7: 13-15) / Severe GAD (GAD-7: ≥16)

**First-Line: High-Intensity CBT OR SSRI**

**NICE Recommendation**: Offer choice of CBT or medication

**Shared Decision-Making**:

**Option A: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)**
- **Efficacy**: 60-70% response rate
- **Format**: 12-16 sessions, weekly
- **Components**:
  - Cognitive restructuring (identify and challenge anxious thoughts)
  - Behavioral experiments (test anxious predictions)
  - Exposure (gradual confrontation of feared situations)
  - Relaxation techniques
- **Relapse prevention**: Skills persist
- **Availability**: IAPT referral (wait times variable)
- **Tolerability**: High (dropout 20%)
- **Patient effort**: Significant (homework, in vivo practice)

**Option B: SSRI Medication**
- **First-line**: Sertraline 50mg → 100-200mg
  - Alternatives: Escitalopram, paroxetine (license for GAD)
- **Efficacy**: 50-60% response
- **Onset**: 4-8 weeks (anxiety may worsen first 1-2 weeks)
- **Duration**: 12 months after remission (higher relapse than CBT)
- **Side effects**: Common (see depression pathway)
- **Availability**: Immediate
- **Patient effort**: Lower (daily pill, tolerate initial worsening and side effects)

**Option C: Combination** (for severe GAD):
- CBT + SSRI together
- Slightly better than either alone
- Most evidence-based for severe cases

**Treatment protocols**:

**If Patient Chooses CBT**:
- IAPT referral (specify GAD, request high-intensity)
- While waiting: Continue lifestyle, self-help, consider medication if severe/distressing
- Monitor GAD-7 every 4 weeks

**If Patient Chooses SSRI**:
- **Start**: Sertraline 50mg daily
- **Warn**: May feel more anxious first 1-2 weeks (starting effect)
- **Titrate**: If inadequate response at 4 weeks, increase to 100mg, then 150-200mg
- **Follow-up**:
  - 1-2 weeks: Tolerability, anxiety level (may be worse)
  - 4-6 weeks: Efficacy (GAD-7)
  - 8-12 weeks: Full assessment
- **Continue**: 12 months after remission
- **Taper**: Slowly over 4+ weeks when stopping

**Second-Line** (inadequate response 12 weeks):
- If CBT alone: Add medication
- If medication alone: Switch SSRI OR add CBT
- Consider SNRI (venlafaxine), pregabalin (if SSRIs failed)
- **Refer**: Mental health team if treatment-resistant

**❌ Benzodiazepines**:
- **Avoid** for GAD (NICE: do not offer)
- **Why**: Tolerance develops, dependency risk, rebound anxiety, cognitive impairment, withdrawal
- **Exception**: Short-term (<2 weeks) crisis only, with clear stop plan

### Panic Disorder

**First-Line: CBT (Superior to Medication for Panic)**

**NICE Recommendation**: CBT should be first-line; medication if CBT declined/unavailable

**CBT for Panic**:
- **Components**:
  - **Psychoeducation**: Panic attacks are not dangerous (no heart attacks, won't "go crazy")
  - **Cognitive restructuring**: Challenge catastrophic misinterpretations of bodily sensations
    - "Racing heart = heart attack" → "Racing heart = anxiety, uncomfortable but safe"
  - **Interoceptive exposure**: Deliberately induce feared sensations (run on spot to increase heart rate, spin to feel dizzy)
    - Teaches: Sensations are not dangerous, tolerance builds
  - **In vivo exposure**: Return to avoided situations (transport, crowded places)
  - **Breathing retraining**: (Evidence mixed - not stand-alone, can be adjunct)
- **Format**: 7-14 sessions
- **Efficacy**: 70-80% panic-free at end of treatment
- **Relapse prevention**: Excellent (skills-based)

**If Patient Declines or Long Wait for CBT**:
- **Self-help**: Guided online CBT for panic (effective, good alternative)
- **Medication**: SSRI (sertraline, paroxetine)
  - Efficacy 50-60% (lower than CBT, higher relapse)
  - Duration: 12 months minimum after remission

**Second-Line**: Combination (CBT + SSRI) if severe or CBT alone inadequate

**Lifestyle**:
- **Critical**: Reduce caffeine (major panic trigger, can provoke attacks)
- Sleep, exercise, alcohol reduction

**What NOT to do**:
- ❌ Benzodiazepines long-term (dependency, interferes with CBT exposure work)
- ❌ Avoid avoided situations (reinforces fear)
- ❌ Safety behaviors (holding onto walls, having someone with you always - prevents learning)

### Social Anxiety Disorder

**First-Line: Individual CBT (Group CBT also effective)**

**CBT for Social Anxiety**:
- **Components**:
  - **Cognitive restructuring**:
    - Challenge overestimation of scrutiny ("Everyone is watching me")
    - Challenge negative self-perception ("I look stupid")
    - Video feedback (how you appear vs. how you think you appear)
  - **Behavioral experiments**:
    - Test predictions (e.g., "If I speak up, people will laugh")
  - **Exposure hierarchy**:
    - Gradual confrontation of feared social situations
    - Start easy (eye contact with stranger), progress to harder (public speaking)
  - **Attention retraining**: Shift focus from self to external (conversation content, environment)
- **Format**:
  - **Individual CBT**: 14-16 sessions (most evidence)
  - **Group CBT**: Cost-effective, added benefit of peer support and in-group exposure
- **Efficacy**: 60-70% response

**Second-Line (if CBT declined, unavailable, or inadequate)**:
- **SSRI**: Sertraline, escitalopram
  - Efficacy 50% (lower than CBT, higher relapse when stopped)
  - Duration: 12 months minimum after remission
  - Consider long-term (relapse common when stopped)

**Combination**: CBT + SSRI for severe cases

**Lifestyle**:
- **Alcohol**: Common self-medication (liquid courage)
  - Address (rebound anxiety, prevents learning, dependency risk)
  - Motivational interviewing
- **Avoidance**: Central to maintenance (must address)

### Specific Phobias

**First-Line: Exposure Therapy (Brief, Highly Effective)**

**Graded Exposure**:
- Create fear hierarchy (0-100 scale)
- Example (spider phobia):
  - 20: Look at photo of spider
  - 40: Watch video of spider
  - 60: Be in room with spider in jar
  - 80: Spider in open container across room
  - 100: Let spider walk on hand
- **Protocol**:
  - Start at level 30-40 (manageable but anxiety-provoking)
  - Stay in situation until anxiety reduces by 50% (habituation)
  - Repeat until comfortable (tolerance builds)
  - Progress up hierarchy
- **Duration**: 4-10 sessions typically
- **Efficacy**: 80-90%

**Alternatives**:
- **Virtual reality exposure**: Effective for flying, heights
- **One-session treatment**: Intensive (3-hour) exposure for simple phobias
  - Example: Flying phobia cured in one flight with therapist

**Medication**: Generally NOT effective for specific phobias (CBT far superior)

## Step 3: Monitoring and Follow-Up

**Use GAD-7 every visit**:
- **Response**: ≥50% reduction OR score <7
- **Remission**: GAD-7 <5 sustained
- **Adjust treatment**: If no response 8-12 weeks

**Follow-up schedules**:
| Severity | Initial | Ongoing | Duration |
|----------|---------|---------|----------|
| **Mild** | 4 weeks | Every 4-6 weeks | 3-6 months |
| **Moderate/Severe** | 2 weeks | Every 2-4 weeks | 6-12 months |

**Monitor**:
- Symptom severity (GAD-7)
- Avoidance patterns (increasing or decreasing?)
- Functional impact (work, social, relationships)
- Side effects (if on medication)
- Substance use (alcohol, benzodiazepines)

---

## Step 4: Relapse Prevention

**After remission**:

**If on SSRI**:
- Continue 12 months minimum
- Consider longer if recurrent (multiple episodes)
- Taper slowly when stopping (4+ weeks)

**If had CBT**:
- Skills persist, but booster sessions helpful if symptoms re-emerge
- Practice ongoing (continue exposure, challenge anxious thoughts)

**Lifestyle maintenance**:
- Exercise regularly (anxiety buffer)
- Limit caffeine
- Adequate sleep
- Moderate alcohol

**Early warning signs**:
- Increased worry
- Avoidance creeping back
- Physical tension (muscle tightness, jaw clenching)

**Action**: Resume brief CBT, restart medication if needed

## Special Considerations

### Comorbid Depression

**Common** (60% overlap)

**Treatment**:
- SSRI treats both anxiety and depression
- CBT transdiagnostic protocols available
- Address both simultaneously

### Health Anxiety

**Specialized CBT**:
- Challenge health-related beliefs ("Headache = brain tumor")
- Reduce reassurance-seeking and checking behaviors
- Exposure to health uncertainty

**Do NOT**:
- Provide excessive reassurance (reinforces anxiety)
- Order unnecessary tests (reinforces belief that tests needed)

**Appropriate**:
- Single thorough examination to rule out medical cause
- Clear explanation of anxiety mechanism
- Redirect to CBT

### Older Adults

**Considerations**:
- GAD common in older adults (worry about health, finances)
- Medication interactions (polypharmacy)
- Fall risk (avoid sedating medications, benzodiazepines particularly dangerous)
- CBT effective at all ages

## Key Messages for Patients

**Use patient materials** (`patient-materials/anxiety-guide.pdf` - to be created):

1. **"Anxiety is treatable"** - 70-80% improve with CBT
2. **"Physical symptoms are not dangerous"** - Anxiety feels awful but won't harm you
3. **"Avoidance maintains anxiety"** - Short-term relief, long-term trap
4. **"Exposure is the antidote"** - Face fears gradually to build tolerance
5. **"Lifestyle matters"**: Cut caffeine, exercise, sleep, limit alcohol

## Clinical Pearls

✅ **DO**:
- Offer CBT first-line (superior long-term outcomes, no withdrawal)
- Address caffeine (often overlooked, major trigger)
- Assess for depression (60% comorbid)
- Explain avoidance trap (key to engagement with exposure)
- Monitor adherence to exposure homework (predicts outcome)

❌ **DON'T**:
- Prescribe benzodiazepines routinely (dependency, rebound, interferes with CBT)
- Offer relaxation as sole treatment (must combine with exposure/cognitive work)
- Order excessive tests for health anxiety (reinforces beliefs)
- Support safety behaviors (prevents learning)
- Accept "I've tried CBT and it didn't work" at face value (check: was it adequate dose? 12+ sessions? Did exposure component? Adequate therapist?)
