# Depression Clinical Pathway - EQUILIBRIUM

**Version**: 2.0
**Last Updated**: October 2025
**Aligned with**: NICE CG90 (Depression in Adults, 2022 update)
**Evidence Base**: See `docs/01-evidence-base/systematic-reviews/`

## Quick Reference

| Severity | First-Line Approach | Second-Line | When to Refer |
|----------|---------------------|-------------|---------------|
| **Subthreshold** | Watchful waiting, lifestyle advice | Low-intensity CBT if persists >2 weeks | Not improving at 4-6 weeks |
| **Mild** | Lifestyle interventions + low-intensity therapy | Medication if patient preference | Risk concerns, not improving 8 weeks |
| **Moderate** | **Patient choice**: Therapy OR medication OR lifestyle + therapy | Combination therapy | Treatment resistance, risk |
| **Severe** | Combination (medication + high-intensity therapy + lifestyle) | ECT, specialist input | Immediate if suicidal/psychotic |

## Step 1: Assessment

### A. Initial Presentation

**Presenting complaints suggestive of depression**:
- Low mood, loss of interest/pleasure (anhedonia)
- Fatigue, low energy
- Sleep disturbance
- Appetite/weight changes
- Cognitive symptoms (concentration, memory, decision-making)
- Somatic symptoms (pain, gastrointestinal)

**Consider depression screening** (PHQ-2):
1. "During the last 2 weeks, have you felt down, depressed, or hopeless?"
2. "During the last 2 weeks, have you had little interest or pleasure in doing things?"

**If YES to either** → Proceed to full assessment

### B. Diagnostic Assessment

**Use PHQ-9** (see `assessment-tools/phq-9-scoring.md`):
- 9 questions, score 0-27
- **Score interpretation**:
  - 0-4: Minimal/none
  - 5-9: Mild
  - 10-14: Moderate
  - 15-19: Moderately severe
  - 20-27: Severe

**Clinical interview** (confirm diagnosis, assess severity):
- Core symptoms (low mood, anhedonia) present most days for ≥2 weeks?
- Impact on functioning (work, relationships, self-care)?
- Previous episodes, treatments, response?
- Comorbid anxiety, substance use, medical conditions?

**Differential diagnosis**:
- Medical causes (hypothyroidism, anemia, vitamin D deficiency, sleep apnea)
- Medication-induced (beta-blockers, corticosteroids)
- Adjustment disorder vs. major depressive disorder
- Bipolar depression (screen for mania/hypomania history)
- Normal grief/sadness (context-appropriate, not pervasive)

### C. Biopsychosocial Assessment

**Use template**: `assessment-tools/biopsychosocial-assessment-template.md`

**Biological factors**:
- Sleep quality and quantity (use `sleep-quality-assessment.md`)
- Diet and nutrition (use `nutrition-screener.md`)
- Physical activity level (use `exercise-assessment.md`)
- Alcohol and substance use
- Chronic pain or medical comorbidities
- Medications (check for depressogenic drugs)

**Psychological factors**:
- Cognitive patterns (negative thinking, rumination, self-criticism)
- Coping strategies (adaptive vs. maladaptive)
- Trauma history
- Perfectionism, high self-expectations
- Meaning and purpose (work satisfaction, hobbies, values)

**Social factors**:
- Relationship quality (partner, family, friends)
- Social isolation vs. connection
- Work stress, unemployment, financial strain
- Housing security
- Caregiving responsibilities
- Discrimination, marginalization

### D. Risk Assessment

**Suicide risk** (ask directly):
- "Have you had thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life?"
- If YES:
  - Frequency, intensity, plans, intent
  - Protective factors (reasons to live, future plans)
  - Access to means
  - Previous attempts
- **Risk stratification**:
  - **Low**: Thoughts, no plan, good protective factors
  - **Moderate**: Plans but no immediate intent, some protective factors
  - **High**: Plan + intent + means, few protective factors, previous attempts
- **Action**:
  - Low: Safety plan, close follow-up
  - Moderate: Mental health team same-day assessment
  - High: Emergency referral (psychiatric liaison, A&E)

**Self-neglect risk**:
- Eating, hydration, personal care impaired?

**Safeguarding**:
- Vulnerable adults or children at risk?

## Step 2: Severity-Guided Treatment

### Subthreshold Depression (PHQ-9: 5-9, minimal functional impairment)

**First-Line: Watchful Waiting + Lifestyle Advice**

**Approach**:
1. **Psychoeducation**:
   - Explain subthreshold depression (symptoms present but below diagnostic threshold)
   - Natural course (often improves spontaneously, but can progress)
   - Role of lifestyle factors

2. **Lifestyle interventions**:
   - **Exercise**: Recommend 150 min/week moderate activity
     - Use patient material: `patient-materials/exercise-for-mental-health.pdf`
     - Discuss barriers, problem-solve, consider exercise on prescription
   - **Sleep hygiene**: Assess and provide guidance
     - Use: `patient-materials/sleep-hygiene-guide.pdf`
   - **Diet**: Brief nutrition advice, Mediterranean diet emphasis
     - Use: `patient-materials/mediterranean-diet-guide.pdf`
   - **Social connection**: Identify social activities, reduce isolation

3. **Self-help**:
   - Guided self-help materials (online CBT, books)
   - Mood monitoring (diary, PHQ-9 tracking)

4. **Follow-up**: 2 weeks
   - Re-assess PHQ-9
   - If improving: Continue, extend follow-up to 4 weeks
   - If worsening or not improving: Consider low-intensity intervention

**Second-Line (if persists or worsens at 2-4 weeks)**:
- Low-intensity CBT (IAPT Step 2: guided self-help, psychoeducation groups)
- Continue lifestyle interventions

**Do NOT**:
- ❌ Prescribe antidepressants (not recommended for subthreshold/mild depression - NICE)

---

### Mild Depression (PHQ-9: 10-14, some functional impairment)

**First-Line: Lifestyle + Low-Intensity Psychological Intervention**

**NICE Recommendation**: Antidepressants should NOT be used routinely for mild depression (not cost-effective, risk-benefit unfavorable)

**Approach**:

1. **Lifestyle interventions** (as above, intensified):
   - **Exercise prescription**: Specific, individualized plan
     - Type: Aerobic (walking, running, cycling, swimming)
     - Frequency: 3-5 times/week
     - Intensity: Moderate (can talk, not sing)
     - Duration: 30-45 min per session
     - Referral: Exercise on prescription, parkrun, gym membership subsidies
   - **Dietary intervention**:
     - Mediterranean diet (evidence from SMILES trial)
     - Consider referral to dietitian if resources available
   - **Sleep**:
     - CBT-I techniques (sleep restriction, stimulus control)
     - Avoid hypnotics (dependency risk, doesn't address underlying problem)

2. **Low-intensity psychological intervention** (IAPT Step 2):
   - **Guided self-help**: Online CBT programs (SilverCloud, Beating the Blues), workbooks
   - **Behavioral activation**: Activity scheduling, re-engage with pleasurable activities
   - **Problem-solving therapy**: Structured approach to life stressors
   - **Psychoeducation groups**: Understanding depression, coping strategies

3. **Social prescribing**:
   - Link worker referral for social activities, volunteering, community groups
   - Address loneliness and social isolation

4. **Monitor**: PHQ-9 every 2-4 weeks
   - **Response** (PHQ-9 ↓≥5 points or <10): Continue, extend intervals
   - **Partial response**: Intensify (add components, increase frequency)
   - **No response at 6-8 weeks**: Move to second-line

**Second-Line (if inadequate response at 8 weeks OR patient preference)**:
- **Option A**: Antidepressant medication (if patient prefers pharmacological approach)
  - Inform patient about alternatives, discuss evidence
  - Use shared decision-making (see `decision-aids/depression-treatment-decision-aid.pdf`)
- **Option B**: High-intensity therapy (CBT, if available and patient prefers)

**Third-Line**: Combination (medication + therapy)

---

### Moderate Depression (PHQ-9: 15-19, significant functional impairment)

**First-Line: Patient Choice of Therapy OR Medication (or both)**

**NICE Recommendation**: Offer psychological therapy AND antidepressant; patient decides preference

**Shared Decision-Making Process**:

1. **Present options** with balanced evidence:

   **Option A: High-Intensity Psychological Therapy (CBT, IPT, BA)**
   - **Efficacy**: Equivalent to medication acutely (50-60% response rate)
   - **Relapse prevention**: SUPERIOR to medication (50% relapse vs. 80% after stopping medication)
   - **Mechanism**: Teaches skills that persist after therapy ends
   - **Format**: 12-20 sessions, weekly, 50-60 min
   - **Availability**: IAPT referral (wait times vary, 6-18 weeks typically)
   - **Tolerability**: High (dropout 20-30%, usually early)
   - **Patient effort**: Significant (homework, practice between sessions)

   **Option B: Antidepressant Medication (SSRI first-line)**
   - **Efficacy**: 50-60% response rate (similar to therapy acutely)
   - **Relapse prevention**: 80% relapse after stopping (higher than therapy)
   - **Onset**: 4-6 weeks for full effect
   - **Duration**: Minimum 6 months after remission, often longer
   - **Side effects**: Common (nausea, sexual dysfunction, weight gain, withdrawal on stopping)
   - **Availability**: Immediate
   - **Patient effort**: Lower (take daily pill, tolerate side effects)

   **Option C: Combination (Medication + Therapy)**
   - **Efficacy**: Slightly better than either alone acutely (60-70% response)
   - **Relapse prevention**: Best outcomes (if therapy component retained)
   - **Cons**: More intensive, higher cost

   **Option D: Lifestyle Interventions**
   - **Efficacy**: Exercise alone comparable to medication (effect size 0.62)
   - **Onset**: 4-12 weeks
   - **Relapse prevention**: Good (if habits sustained)
   - **Side effects**: Beneficial (physical health improves)
   - **Patient effort**: Highest (requires sustained behavior change)
   - **Can combine**: With any of above options

2. **Use decision aid**: `decision-aids/depression-treatment-decision-aid.pdf`
   - Visual comparison of benefits, harms, patient experiences
   - Preference elicitation tool

3. **Elicit patient preferences**:
   - "What matters most to you?" (quick relief vs. long-term skills vs. avoiding medication)
   - Practical factors (wait for therapy? prefer pill? time for exercise?)
   - Previous experiences (if relapse, what worked before?)

4. **Document shared decision**:
   - Patient's choice and reasoning
   - Alternative options discussed
   - Plan for monitoring and adjusting

**First-Line Treatment Options** (patient selects):

**If Patient Chooses Therapy**:
- IAPT referral for high-intensity CBT, IPT, or BA
- While waiting: Start lifestyle interventions, low-intensity self-help
- Monitor PHQ-9 every 4 weeks
- If deteriorates while waiting: Consider medication bridge

**If Patient Chooses Medication**:
- **First-line SSRI**: Sertraline 50mg daily (better tolerated, lower cost)
- **Alternatives**: Fluoxetine, citalopram, escitalopram (if sertraline not tolerated)
- **Inform patient**:
  - May feel worse first 1-2 weeks (nausea, headache, anxiety)
  - Improvement gradual, 4-6 weeks for full effect
  - Continue for 6+ months after feeling better
  - Don't stop abruptly (withdrawal symptoms)
  - Sexual side effects common (30-40%)
- **Follow-up**:
  - 1-2 weeks: Tolerability, side effects, suicide risk (may increase transiently in first weeks)
  - 4-6 weeks: Efficacy (PHQ-9), adjust dose if partial response
  - 8-12 weeks: Full assessment of response
- **Monitoring**: PHQ-9 every 4 weeks

**If Patient Chooses Combination**:
- Start SSRI + IAPT referral
- Add lifestyle interventions
- Most intensive but best outcomes for moderate-severe

**If Patient Chooses Lifestyle**:
- Intensive lifestyle program:
  - Supervised exercise program (3-5×/week, 30-45 min)
  - Mediterranean diet (dietitian referral)
  - CBT-I for sleep
  - Social prescribing
- Close monitoring (PHQ-9 every 2 weeks)
- **Emphasize**: This is evidence-based choice, not "just lifestyle"
- If no response 8 weeks: Add therapy or medication

**Continue throughout**: Lifestyle interventions (exercise, sleep, diet, social) regardless of primary treatment choice

**Second-Line (inadequate response at 12 weeks)**:
- If therapy alone: Add medication
- If medication alone: Add therapy OR switch medication OR increase dose
- Reassess diagnosis, adherence, psychosocial stressors

---

### Moderately Severe/Severe Depression (PHQ-9: 20-27, major functional impairment)

**First-Line: Combination Treatment (Medication + Therapy + Lifestyle Support)**

**NICE Recommendation**: Combination therapy for severe depression

**Approach**:

1. **Antidepressant medication** (start promptly):
   - **SSRI first-line**: Sertraline 50mg, titrate to 100-200mg if needed
   - **Follow-up**: Weekly for first 4 weeks (safety, tolerability, risk monitoring)

2. **High-intensity psychological therapy**:
   - IAPT urgent referral (priority for severe cases, shorter wait)
   - **If long wait**: Interim crisis support, community mental health team input

3. **Crisis plan**:
   - Contact numbers (GP, mental health crisis line, Samaritans)
   - Coping strategies for acute distress
   - Safety plan if suicidal thoughts
   - Remove access to means (medications, ligatures)

4. **Intensive monitoring**:
   - **Weekly** contact first month (GP, practice nurse, mental health worker)
   - PHQ-9 every 1-2 weeks
   - Risk reassessment each contact

5. **Psychosocial support**:
   - Sick leave if working (functional impairment significant)
   - Carer support if family involved
   - Social services if self-neglect concerns

6. **Lifestyle support** (adapted to functional capacity):
   - Gentle movement (walking, gentle yoga) - avoid over-exertion
   - Sleep support (irregular sleep common in severe depression)
   - Nutritional support (appetite often poor)

**Second-Line (inadequate response at 4-6 weeks OR deterioration)**:
- **Specialist referral**: Community mental health team or psychiatry
- **Consider**:
  - Medication switch or augmentation
  - More intensive therapy (daily, inpatient if appropriate)
  - ECT (if psychotic features, catatonia, severe suicide risk, treatment-resistant)

**Third-Line**:
- Specialist management (psychiatry-led)

**Immediate Referral (same day)** if:
- High suicide risk (plan + intent + means)
- Psychotic symptoms (delusions, hallucinations)
- Catatonia
- Severe self-neglect (not eating, drinking)

## Step 3: Monitoring and Follow-Up

### Monitoring Tools

**Use PHQ-9 every visit** (track score over time):
- **Response**: ≥50% reduction OR score <10
- **Remission**: PHQ-9 <5 sustained
- **Partial response**: 25-49% reduction (consider treatment adjustment)
- **No response**: <25% reduction (change approach)

**Functional assessment**:
- Return to work/education?
- Social engagement?
- Self-care?
- Relationships?

**Side effects** (if on medication):
- Tolerability scale
- Sexual function (ask directly - underreported)
- Weight

### Follow-Up Schedule

| Severity | Initial Contact | Ongoing Monitoring | Duration |
|----------|----------------|-------------------|----------|
| **Subthreshold/Mild** | 2 weeks | Every 4 weeks until remission | 3-6 months |
| **Moderate** | 2-4 weeks | Every 4 weeks | 6-12 months |
| **Severe** | 1 week | Weekly × 4, then every 2 weeks | 12+ months |

### Treatment Adjustments

**If inadequate response at expected timeframe**:

1. **Check adherence**: Is patient taking medication, attending therapy, doing lifestyle interventions?

2. **Optimize current treatment**:
   - Medication: Increase dose to maximum tolerated
   - Therapy: Increase frequency, switch modality (CBT → IPT or BA)
   - Lifestyle: Intensify support (supervised exercise vs. self-directed)

3. **Reassess formulation**:
   - Missed diagnosis? (bipolar, PTSD, personality disorder)
   - Psychosocial stressors worsened? (relationship breakdown, job loss)
   - Substance use interfering?
   - Medical comorbidity?

4. **Add treatment component**:
   - Medication alone → Add therapy
   - Therapy alone → Add medication
   - Add lifestyle interventions if not tried

5. **Switch treatment**:
   - Different medication class (SSRI → SNRI, mirtazapine, bupropion)
   - Different therapy modality

6. **Refer**:
   - Mental health team for complex cases, treatment resistance

## Step 4: Relapse Prevention

**After achieving remission** (PHQ-9 <5 for 4-8 weeks):

### Continuation Phase (6-12 months)

**Goals**: Prevent relapse during vulnerable period

**Continue treatment**:
- **If on medication**: Continue same dose minimum 6 months
  - First episode: 6-12 months
  - Recurrent (≥2 episodes): 12-24 months or longer
  - Multiple risk factors (3+ episodes, severe, residual symptoms): Consider indefinite
- **If had therapy**: Booster sessions optional (evidence mixed)
- **Lifestyle habits**: Emphasize sustaining exercise, sleep, diet, social routines

**Monitoring**:
- PHQ-9 every 8-12 weeks
- Educate on early warning signs (sleep disturbance, loss of interest often first)

### Maintenance Phase (>12 months)

**For recurrent depression or high risk**:
- **Medication**: Taper slowly if stopping (over 4 weeks minimum, longer if >1 year use)
  - Monitor closely during taper (20-30% relapse)
  - Restart if symptoms re-emerge
- **Psychological**: Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT) effective for relapse prevention
  - 8-week group program
  - Reduces relapse 40-50% in recurrent depression
- **Lifestyle**: Ongoing habits critical (exercise most evidence for relapse prevention)

### Stopping Medication (when appropriate)

**Consider stopping if**:
- In remission ≥6-12 months
- Psychosocial stressors resolved
- Lifestyle habits established
- Patient preference (balanced with relapse risk)

**Tapering protocol**:
- Reduce dose by 25% every 4 weeks (minimum)
- Monitor PHQ-9 every 2 weeks during taper
- If symptoms return: Resume previous dose, consider longer maintenance

**Common withdrawal symptoms** (not relapse):
- Dizziness, electric shock sensations, flu-like symptoms, anxiety, insomnia
- Onset: 2-5 days after dose reduction
- Duration: 1-2 weeks usually
- **Distinguish from relapse**: Withdrawal is somatic, transient; relapse is mood/cognitive, progressive

## Step 5: Special Considerations

### Treatment-Resistant Depression

**Definition**: Inadequate response to ≥2 adequate trials of antidepressants (adequate dose, duration 6-8 weeks)

**Primary care actions**:
1. Confirm diagnosis (not bipolar, PTSD, personality disorder misdiagnosed)
2. Ensure adherence (therapeutic drug monitoring if available)
3. Optimize psychosocial (therapy, lifestyle, social support)
4. Review perpetuating factors (chronic stress, substance use, medical comorbidity)
5. **Refer to psychiatry** for specialist management:
   - Augmentation strategies (lithium, antipsychotics, thyroid)
   - ECT if severe
   - Specialist therapies (DBT if personality traits, trauma therapy if PTSD)

### Comorbid Anxiety

**Common** (60% of depression cases have comorbid anxiety)

**Treatment**:
- **SSRIs**: Effective for both depression and anxiety
- **CBT**: Transdiagnostic protocols address both
- **Exercise**: Benefits both conditions
- **Benzodiazepines**: Avoid (dependency risk, rebound anxiety, doesn't treat underlying problem)
  - **Exception**: Short-term (<2 weeks) for acute crisis only, with clear stop date

### Comorbid Substance Use

**Prevalence**: 20-30% of depression cases

**Treatment**:
- Address simultaneously (not sequentially)
- Motivational interviewing for substance use
- SSRI safe (avoid tricyclics due to overdose risk)
- Abstinence improves depression outcomes

### Older Adults (>65)

**Considerations**:
- Drug interactions (polypharmacy)
- Lower starting doses (sertraline 25mg)
- Fall risk (monitor, avoid sedating medications)
- Cognitive impairment (distinguish from dementia)
- **Bereavement**: Common trigger, consider psychological support

### Perinatal Depression

**Screening**: Routine at booking, 28 weeks, postpartum

**Treatment**:
- **Mild-moderate**: Psychological therapy first-line (avoid medication if possible, but don't withhold if needed)
- **Severe**: Medication + therapy (SSRI compatible with breastfeeding: sertraline preferred)
- **Refer**: Perinatal mental health team if available
- **Safeguarding**: Assess mother-infant bonding, risk to baby

## Key Messages for Patients

**Use patient materials** (`patient-materials/`):

1. **"Depression is treatable"** - 70-80% recover with appropriate treatment
2. **"You have options"** - Therapy, medication, lifestyle all effective; choose what fits you
3. **"Treatment takes time"** - 4-8 weeks to see benefit, continue even when feeling better
4. **"Lifestyle matters"** - Exercise, sleep, diet, social connection are treatments, not just "nice to have"
5. **"Relapse is preventable"** - Continue treatment 6+ months, recognize early warning signs

## Clinical Pearls

✅ **DO**:
- Use shared decision-making (improves adherence, outcomes)
- Address lifestyle in every case (evidence-based, patient-acceptable)
- Monitor PHQ-9 systematically (quantify response, guide adjustments)
- Continue treatment after remission (prevent relapse)
- Ask about suicide risk directly (doesn't increase risk, identifies need)

❌ **DON'T**:
- Prescribe antidepressants for mild depression routinely (NICE discourages)
- Use benzodiazepines for depression/anxiety long-term (dependency)
- Stop monitoring after starting treatment (many don't respond)
- Dismiss lifestyle as "alternative" (it's evidence-based mainstream)
- Assume patient prefers medication (many prefer therapy or lifestyle if offered)

### C. Evidence Summary

**Exercise vs. Antidepressants**:
- Meta-analysis (Schuch 2016): Effect size 0.62 vs. 0.3 for SSRIs
- Cochrane review: Exercise effective, comparable to medication

**CBT vs. Medication**:
- Acute: Equivalent efficacy (~55% response both)
- Relapse: CBT superior (50% relapse vs. 80% medication)
- Mechanism: Skills persist after therapy ends

**Mediterranean Diet**:
- SMILES trial (2017): 32% remission vs. 8% social support control
- HELFIMED (2019): Confirmed benefit

**Sleep (CBT-I)**:
- Treats insomnia AND improves depression
- Effect size 0.38 on depressive symptoms

**Social Prescribing**:
- Emerging evidence, promising for mild-moderate depression
- Low-cost, patient-acceptable
