# Antidepressant Medication Comparison Guide

**Comparing common antidepressants: efficacy, side effects, and practical considerations**

---

## Overview

This guide helps doctors and patients choose between antidepressant medications by comparing:
- Effectiveness for depression and anxiety
- Common side effects
- Drug interactions
- Special considerations
- Cost

**All SSRIs and SNRIs have similar overall efficacy** (~50-60% response rate). Choice is based on:
- Side effect profile
- Patient preference
- Previous response
- Drug interactions
- Cost
- Specific indications (e.g., anxiety, OCD)

## Detailed Comparison: First-Line SSRIs

### 1. Sertraline (Lustral)

**Recommended as first-line SSRI** by most guidelines

**Indications**:
- Depression (all severities)
- GAD (Generalized Anxiety Disorder)
- Panic disorder
- Social anxiety disorder
- PTSD
- OCD

**Dosing**:
- Start: 50mg daily
- Usual: 100mg daily
- Range: 50-200mg
- Time to full effect: 4-6 weeks

**Efficacy**: ★★★★ (equivalent to other SSRIs)

**Side Effects**:

**Common (>10%)**:
- Nausea, diarrhea (★★★) - usually first 1-2 weeks, often improves
- Sexual dysfunction (★★★) - 30-40% (delayed orgasm, reduced libido)
- Insomnia or somnolence (★★)
- Headache (★★)
- Dry mouth (★★)

**Less Common (1-10%)**:
- Weight gain (★★) - average 2-5 lbs
- Sweating, tremor
- Dizziness

**Withdrawal severity**: ★★ (moderate - taper over 4 weeks minimum)

**Drug Interactions**:
- NSAIDs: Increased GI bleed risk (use PPI if needed)
- Anticoagulants (warfarin): Monitor INR closely
- Tramadol, triptans: Serotonin syndrome risk (avoid if possible)
- Other serotonergic drugs: Additive effect

**Special Considerations**:
- Safe in breastfeeding (preferred SSRI)
- Safe post-MI (cardiac safety data)
- Good tolerability profile
- **First-line choice for most patients**

**Cost**: £ (generic, inexpensive)

---

### 2. Fluoxetine (Prozac)

**Advantages**: Long half-life (easier withdrawal)

**Indications**:
- Depression
- OCD (higher doses)
- Bulimia nervosa (60mg)
- Premenstrual dysphoric disorder

**Dosing**:
- Start: 20mg daily
- Usual: 20-40mg
- Range: 20-60mg
- Can give once daily (morning) or alternate days due to long half-life

**Efficacy**: ★★★★ (equivalent to other SSRIs)

**Side Effects**:

**Common**:
- GI upset (★★★) - nausea, diarrhea (early, improves)
- Sexual dysfunction (★★★) - 30-40%
- Insomnia, agitation (★★★) - more activating than other SSRIs
- Headache (★★)

**Less Common**:
- Weight loss initially, weight gain long-term (★★)
- Anxiety, restlessness (especially first weeks)

**Withdrawal severity**: ★ (LOWEST) - long half-life (~4-6 days) means gradual tapering naturally

**Special Considerations**:
- **Best for patients concerned about withdrawal** (easiest to stop)
- More activating → good if low energy depression, avoid if insomnia/anxiety prominent
- Inhibits CYP2D6 (many drug interactions - check before prescribing)
- Safe in breastfeeding

**Cost**: £ (generic)

---

### 3. Citalopram (Cipramil)

**Advantages**: Generally well-tolerated, less drug interactions than fluoxetine

**Indications**:
- Depression
- Panic disorder

**Dosing**:
- Start: 20mg daily
- Usual: 20mg (often sufficient)
- Maximum: **40mg** (20mg if >60 years old, due to QTc concerns)

**Efficacy**: ★★★★

**Side Effects**:

**Common**:
- Somnolence (★★) - more sedating than sertraline/fluoxetine
- Sexual dysfunction (★★★) - 30-40%
- GI upset (★★) - less than sertraline
- Dry mouth (★★)

**Less Common**:
- Weight gain (★★★) - more than sertraline
- Sweating

**Withdrawal severity**: ★★★ (moderate-high - taper slowly)

**Special Considerations**:
- **QTc prolongation concern** (dose-dependent)
  - Get baseline ECG if cardiac risk factors
  - Maximum 20mg if >60 years old
  - Avoid if known long QT, recent MI, heart failure
- Fewer drug interactions than fluoxetine (doesn't inhibit CYP2D6)
- More sedating → may help if insomnia prominent

**Cost**: £ (generic)

---

### 4. Escitalopram (Cipralex)

**Enantiomer of citalopram** (active component only)

**Indications**:
- Depression
- GAD
- Social anxiety disorder
- Panic disorder

**Dosing**:
- Start: 10mg daily
- Usual: 10-20mg
- Maximum: 20mg

**Efficacy**: ★★★★ (some studies suggest slightly more effective than citalopram, but marginal)

**Side Effects**:
- **Similar to citalopram** but potentially better tolerated
- Sexual dysfunction (★★★)
- Somnolence (★★)
- GI upset (★★)
- Weight gain (★★★)

**Withdrawal severity**: ★★★ (moderate-high)

**Special Considerations**:
- Same QTc concerns as citalopram (though lower doses used)
- Fewer drug interactions
- **Consider if citalopram not tolerated** (cleaner formulation)
- Slightly more expensive than citalopram but still affordable

**Cost**: ££ (generic now, moderate cost)

---

### 5. Paroxetine (Seroxat)

**Least preferred SSRI** due to withdrawal and weight gain

**Indications**:
- Depression
- All anxiety disorders (GAD, panic, social anxiety, PTSD, OCD)

**Dosing**:
- Start: 20mg daily
- Usual: 20-40mg
- Range: 20-50mg

**Efficacy**: ★★★★ (equivalent to other SSRIs)

**Side Effects**:

**Common**:
- Sedation (★★★) - most sedating SSRI
- Sexual dysfunction (★★★★) - highest rate (~50%)
- Weight gain (★★★★) - most weight gain of SSRIs (average 5-10 lbs)
- GI upset (★★)
- Dry mouth, sweating (★★★)

**Withdrawal severity**: ★★★★★ (WORST of all SSRIs)
- Withdrawal symptoms very common and severe
- Must taper very slowly (reduce by 10mg every 2-4 weeks)
- "Electric shock" sensations, dizziness, flu-like symptoms

**Special Considerations**:
- **Avoid as first-line** (unless specific reason, e.g., previous good response)
- Most sedating → may help severe insomnia, but mirtazapine better choice
- Significant CYP2D6 inhibition (many drug interactions)
- **Avoid in pregnancy** (risk of cardiac malformations)

**Cost**: £ (generic)

**Why prescribe at all?**
- Some patients respond very well and tolerate it
- May be useful if others failed
- Broad anxiety indication

## Second-Line Antidepressants

### SNRIs (Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors)

#### Venlafaxine (Effexor XL)

**When to use**: SSRI inadequate response, treatment-resistant depression

**Indications**:
- Depression (especially if SSRIs failed)
- GAD

**Dosing**:
- Start: 75mg XL daily
- Usual: 150-225mg
- Range: 75-375mg

**Efficacy**: ★★★★★ (may be slightly more effective than SSRIs for severe depression)

**Side Effects**:

**Common**:
- Nausea (★★★★) - worse than SSRIs, often limits tolerability
- Sexual dysfunction (★★★★) - similar or worse than SSRIs
- Blood pressure elevation (★★★) - **dose-dependent**
  - Monitor BP at baseline and dose increases
  - Significant hypertension in ~10% at high doses
- Sweating (★★★★) - more than SSRIs
- Dry mouth (★★★)

**Withdrawal severity**: ★★★★ (severe - must taper slowly)

**Special Considerations**:
- **Check BP before starting and at dose increases**
- More effective than SSRIs? (some evidence, not conclusive)
- Higher side effect burden than SSRIs
- Reserve for SSRI non-responders or severe depression
- **Not first-line**

**Cost**: £ (generic)

---

#### Duloxetine (Cymbalta)

**When to use**: Depression + chronic pain

**Indications**:
- Depression
- GAD
- Diabetic neuropathy
- Fibromyalgia
- Chronic musculoskeletal pain

**Dosing**:
- Start: 60mg daily (or 30mg if frail/elderly)
- Usual: 60mg
- Range: 60-120mg

**Efficacy**: ★★★★ (similar to SSRIs and venlafaxine)

**Side Effects**:
- Nausea (★★★★) - very common initially
- Sexual dysfunction (★★★)
- Dry mouth (★★★)
- Constipation (★★)
- Dizziness (★★★)

**Withdrawal severity**: ★★★★ (severe)

**Special Considerations**:
- **Excellent for depression + chronic pain** (dual indication)
- Useful for fibromyalgia, diabetic neuropathy
- Avoid if liver disease
- Less BP elevation than venlafaxine

**Cost**: ££ (moderate)

---

### Others

#### Mirtazapine (Zispin)

**Unique mechanism**: Noradrenergic and specific serotonergic antidepressant (NaSSA)

**When to use**: Depression with insomnia, poor appetite/weight loss, or if SSRIs not tolerated

**Indications**:
- Depression (especially with insomnia, weight loss)

**Dosing**:
- Start: 15mg at night (or 7.5mg if very sedating)
- Usual: 30mg
- Range: 15-45mg
- **Paradox**: Lower doses (15mg) more sedating than higher doses (30-45mg)

**Efficacy**: ★★★★ (equivalent to SSRIs)

**Side Effects**:

**Very Common**:
- **Sedation** (★★★★★) - profound, especially at 15mg
  - **Advantage if severe insomnia**
  - Disadvantage: daytime grogginess
- **Weight gain** (★★★★★) - most of any antidepressant (average 10-20 lbs)
  - Increases appetite significantly

**Common**:
- Dry mouth (★★★)
- Constipation (★★)

**Rare but Serious**:
- **Agranulocytosis** (very rare, <0.1%, but serious)
  - Warn patient: fever, sore throat, mouth ulcers → seek medical attention

**Withdrawal severity**: ★★ (moderate, less than SSRIs)

**Special Considerations**:
- **Best antidepressant for insomnia** (very sedating)
- **Best for poor appetite/weight loss** (increases appetite)
- **Sexual dysfunction rare** (★) - advantage over SSRIs
- **Disadvantage**: Weight gain, sedation problematic for many
- Give at night (due to sedation)

**Cost**: £ (generic)

---

#### Bupropion (Zyban/Wellbutrin) - Off-license in UK for depression

**Mechanism**: Norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitor (NDRI)

**When to use**: Depression + smoking cessation, or if sexual dysfunction on SSRIs

**Indications**:
- Smoking cessation (licensed)
- Depression (off-license in UK, licensed in US)

**Dosing**:
- Start: 150mg daily
- Usual: 150mg twice daily or 300mg XL once daily
- Maximum: 300mg/day

**Efficacy**: ★★★★ (equivalent to SSRIs)

**Side Effects**:
- Insomnia, agitation (★★★) - activating
- Headache (★★)
- Dry mouth (★★)
- Nausea (★★)
- **Sexual dysfunction** (★) - VERY LOW (major advantage)
- Weight: neutral or slight loss

**Serious**:
- **Seizure risk** (0.1-0.4%, dose-dependent)
  - **Contraindicated** if seizure disorder, eating disorder, alcohol withdrawal

**Special Considerations**:
- **Best antidepressant for avoiding sexual side effects**
- Useful for smoking cessation + depression
- More activating (good for low energy, bad for anxiety/insomnia)
- Off-license in UK (can prescribe but need to document reason)

**Cost**: ££ (moderate)

## Choosing the Right Antidepressant

### Decision Algorithm

**Step 1: First-line SSRI choice**

**For most patients**: **Sertraline 50mg**
- Best tolerated
- Broad indications
- Low cost
- Extensive safety data

**If insomnia prominent**: **Mirtazapine 15mg** (very sedating)

**If concerned about withdrawal**: **Fluoxetine 20mg** (long half-life)

**If cardiac concerns (post-MI)**: **Sertraline** (safety data)

**If chronic pain + depression**: **Duloxetine 60mg**

---

**Step 2: If SSRI not tolerated**

**Switch to different SSRI**:
- Sertraline → Fluoxetine or Citalopram (different side effect profiles)

**Or consider**:
- Mirtazapine (if sedation acceptable)
- Bupropion (if avoiding sexual side effects priority, off-license)

---

**Step 3: If SSRI inadequate response** (after 8-12 weeks at adequate dose)

**Options**:
- Increase SSRI dose (if tolerated, only at 4-6 weeks if partial response)
- Switch to different SSRI
- Switch to SNRI (venlafaxine 150mg)
- Add therapy (if not already)
- Augmentation strategies (refer to psychiatry)

---

### Specific Clinical Scenarios

**Depression + Anxiety (GAD, panic)**:
- **Sertraline** or **Escitalopram** (both licensed for anxiety + depression)

**Depression + OCD**:
- **Sertraline** or **Fluoxetine** (often need higher doses for OCD)

**Depression + PTSD**:
- **Sertraline** or **Paroxetine** (both licensed for PTSD)

**Depression + Insomnia**:
- **Mirtazapine 15mg** (very sedating) - best option
- Or SSRI + sleep hygiene/CBT-I (better than adding hypnotic)

**Depression + Weight loss/Poor appetite**:
- **Mirtazapine** (increases appetite, weight gain)

**Depression + Sexual dysfunction concerns**:
- **Bupropion** (very low sexual side effects, off-license) or
- **Mirtazapine** (lower sexual side effects than SSRIs)

**Depression + Chronic pain**:
- **Duloxetine** (licensed for pain + depression)

**Depression + Smoking**:
- **Bupropion** (licensed for smoking cessation)

**Depression in breastfeeding**:
- **Sertraline** (preferred, lowest levels in breast milk)

**Depression in elderly**:
- **Sertraline** or **Citalopram 20mg max** (lower doses, monitor QTc)
- **Avoid paroxetine** (anticholinergic, falls risk, withdrawal)

**Treatment-resistant depression** (2+ SSRIs failed):
- **Venlafaxine** 150-225mg
- Or refer to psychiatry (augmentation, tricyclics, MAOIs, ECT)

## Managing Side Effects

### Sexual Dysfunction (30-40% of patients)

**Strategies**:
1. **Wait** (4-6 weeks) - may improve
2. **Reduce dose** (if effective at lower dose)
3. **Switch antidepressant**:
   - Bupropion (best, minimal sexual side effects)
   - Mirtazapine (lower than SSRIs)
4. **Drug holidays** (not recommended - rebound depression risk)
5. **Add medication** (sildenafil for erectile dysfunction) - specialist input

**Don't ignore**: Causes medication non-adherence (30-50% stop due to side effects, often don't tell doctor)

**ASK directly**: "Any problems with sexual function?" (patients won't volunteer)

---

### Nausea (very common first 1-2 weeks)

**Strategies**:
1. **Take with food** (reduces nausea)
2. **Start low dose** (e.g., sertraline 25mg × 1 week, then 50mg)
3. **Reassure**: Usually improves by week 2-3
4. **Short-term anti-emetic** (domperidone, metoclopramide) if severe
5. **Switch** if intolerable after 2 weeks (try different SSRI or mirtazapine)

---

### Weight Gain (long-term concern)

**Strategies**:
1. **Lifestyle**: Diet and exercise (address early, don't wait for significant gain)
2. **Switch**: Bupropion (weight neutral or loss) or fluoxetine (less weight gain)
3. **Monitor**: Regular weight checks

**Highest risk**: Mirtazapine, paroxetine
**Lowest risk**: Fluoxetine, sertraline, bupropion

---

### Insomnia/Activation (especially fluoxetine, bupropion)

**Strategies**:
1. **Take in morning** (reduces nighttime insomnia)
2. **Sleep hygiene** (see sleep hygiene guide)
3. **Switch**: Mirtazapine or paroxetine (sedating)

**Avoid**: Adding hypnotics long-term (dependency risk)

---

### Withdrawal Symptoms (when stopping)

**Prevention**:
- **Taper slowly**: Reduce by 25% every 2-4 weeks (longer if on >1 year)
- **Slower for paroxetine, venlafaxine** (worst withdrawal) - may need 10% reductions

**Symptoms**: Dizziness, "electric shocks," flu-like, anxiety, insomnia

**Treatment**:
- Reinstate previous dose if severe
- Taper more slowly
- Fluoxetine has easiest withdrawal (long half-life)

## Drug Interactions

### High-Risk Interactions (Avoid or Monitor Closely)

**All SSRIs/SNRIs**:
- **NSAIDs** (ibuprofen, naproxen): Increased GI bleed risk
  - If needed: Add PPI (omeprazole)
- **Anticoagulants** (warfarin): Increased bleeding risk
  - Monitor INR closely
- **Antiplatelets** (aspirin, clopidogrel): Increased bleeding
- **Tramadol, triptans** (sumatriptan): Serotonin syndrome risk
  - Avoid if possible; if essential, low doses and monitor

**Fluoxetine, Paroxetine** (CYP2D6 inhibitors):
- **Tamoxifen**: Reduced effectiveness (avoid fluoxetine/paroxetine if on tamoxifen - use sertraline)
- **Codeine, tramadol**: Reduced analgesic effect
- **Beta-blockers** (metoprolol, carvedilol): Increased beta-blocker levels

**Citalopram, Escitalopram**:
- **QT-prolonging drugs** (antipsychotics, macrolides): Additive QTc prolongation
  - Check ECG if combining

## Summary: First-Line Recommendations

**For most patients**: **Sertraline 50mg**

**Special situations**:
- **Insomnia prominent**: Mirtazapine 15mg
- **Anxiety + depression**: Sertraline or escitalopram
- **Chronic pain + depression**: Duloxetine 60mg
- **Concerned about withdrawal**: Fluoxetine 20mg
- **Sexual dysfunction concerns**: Bupropion (off-license) or mirtazapine
- **Post-MI/cardiac**: Sertraline
- **Breastfeeding**: Sertraline

**Avoid first-line**: Paroxetine (withdrawal, weight gain)

**Second-line** (if SSRI inadequate): Venlafaxine, different SSRI, mirtazapine

**Always combine with**: Psychoeducation, lifestyle interventions (exercise, sleep, diet), consider therapy
