# Depression Treatment Decision Aid

**A tool to help you and your doctor choose the right treatment**

---

## How to Use This Tool

This decision aid helps you compare treatment options for depression. Use it **with your doctor** during your consultation to make a shared decision.

**Steps**:
1. Review your depression severity (your doctor will assess this)
2. Compare the treatment options (pages 2-4)
3. Consider what matters most to you (page 5)
4. Make a decision together with your doctor (page 6)

## Step 2: Compare Your Treatment Options

### Option A: Psychological Therapy (CBT, IPT, or Behavioral Activation)

**What is it?**
- Working with a therapist to change thinking patterns and behaviors
- 12-20 weekly sessions, 50-60 minutes each
- Homework between sessions (practicing skills, tracking mood)

**How well does it work?**
- ✅ **50-60%** of people significantly improve
- ✅ **Same effectiveness as medication** in the short term
- ✅ **BETTER than medication for preventing relapse** (depression returns in 50% with therapy vs. 80% with medication)

**Time to see benefits**: 6-8 weeks

**How long do I need it?**: 12-20 weeks for treatment; skills last forever

**What's involved?**
- Weekly appointments (may need time off work/adjust schedule)
- Homework (1-2 hours per week)
- Active participation (talking about difficult feelings, trying new behaviors)
- Waiting time: 6-18 weeks for NHS (IAPT); immediate if private

**Side effects**: None (though may feel uncomfortable facing difficult emotions)

**Cost**:
- NHS: Free (but waiting list)
- Private: £50-100 per session (£600-2000 total)

**Pros**:
✅ No medication side effects
✅ Lower relapse rate (50% vs. 80%)
✅ Teaches skills you keep forever
✅ Addresses root causes
✅ Can prevent future episodes

**Cons**:
❌ Waiting lists (NHS)
❌ Time commitment (weekly sessions + homework)
❌ Requires active effort
❌ Doesn't work for everyone (40% don't respond)
❌ Can be emotionally challenging

**Best for**:
- People who want to avoid medication
- People wanting long-term skills
- People who can commit to weekly sessions
- People who prefer active approach

---

### Option B: Antidepressant Medication (Usually SSRI)

**What is it?**
- Medication (usually sertraline, fluoxetine, citalopram, or escitalopram)
- Take one pill daily
- Works by affecting brain chemistry and connections

**How well does it work?**
- ✅ **50-60%** of people significantly improve
- ✅ **Same effectiveness as therapy** in the short term
- ❌ **Higher relapse rate** (depression returns in 80% when medication stopped)

**Time to see benefits**: 4-6 weeks (may feel worse in first 1-2 weeks)

**How long do I need it?**:
- Minimum 6 months after feeling better (first episode)
- 12+ months if recurrent depression
- Some people stay on long-term

**What's involved?**
- Take pill daily (at same time)
- Regular follow-ups with GP (weekly initially, then monthly)
- Tolerate side effects (especially first few weeks)
- When stopping: Must taper slowly over 4+ weeks

**Side effects** (30-50% of people experience):
- ❌ Nausea, upset stomach (first 1-2 weeks, usually improves)
- ❌ Sexual problems (difficulty achieving orgasm, reduced desire) - often persist
- ❌ Weight gain (average 5-10 lbs)
- ❌ Emotional numbness ("feel flat, neither happy nor sad")
- ❌ Vivid dreams, increased sweating
- ❌ Withdrawal symptoms when stopping (dizziness, "brain zaps," flu-like symptoms)

**Cost**:
- NHS: £9.90 per month (or free if prescription exempt)
- Private: Similar

**Pros**:
✅ Available immediately (no waiting list)
✅ Less active effort (just take pill)
✅ Well-studied and generally safe
✅ Can be life-saving for severe depression

**Cons**:
❌ Common side effects (see above)
❌ Takes 4-6 weeks to work; may worsen first 1-2 weeks
❌ Higher relapse rate (80% when stopped)
❌ Doesn't teach coping skills
❌ Withdrawal symptoms when stopping
❌ Some people on long-term (years)

**Best for**:
- People needing immediate treatment (can't wait for therapy)
- People who prefer medication to talking therapy
- Moderate-severe depression
- People who've responded well to medication before

---

### Option C: Exercise Prescription

**What is it?**
- Structured exercise program: 150 minutes per week of moderate aerobic activity
- Usually brisk walking, running, cycling, swimming, or dancing
- 30 minutes, 5 days per week

**How well does it work?**
- ✅ **As effective as medication** for moderate depression (effect size 0.62 vs. 0.3 for SSRIs)
- ✅ **50-60%** of people significantly improve
- ✅ Low relapse if exercise habit maintained

**Time to see benefits**: 4-8 weeks

**How long do I need it?**: Ongoing (lifestyle change)

**What's involved?**
- 150 min/week moderate exercise (e.g., 30 min brisk walking, 5 days/week)
- Build gradually if currently inactive
- Schedule exercise like a medical appointment

**Side effects**:
✅ Only beneficial side effects (physical health improves, better sleep, more energy)

**Cost**:
- Free (walking, running, home workouts)
- Moderate (gym membership £20-50/month, exercise classes)
- Some GP "exercise on prescription" schemes available

**Pros**:
✅ As effective as medication (research-proven)
✅ No side effects - only benefits
✅ Improves physical health (heart, weight, energy)
✅ Improves sleep and anxiety too
✅ Low relapse if maintained
✅ Immediate access (no waiting list)
✅ Patient control (no prescriptions needed)

**Cons**:
❌ Requires sustained motivation and effort (hardest when depressed)
❌ Time commitment (150 min/week)
❌ Takes 4-8 weeks to see mood benefits
❌ Barriers (weather, cost, energy, physical limitations)
❌ Doesn't work for everyone (40% don't respond or can't sustain)

**Best for**:
- Mild-moderate depression
- People who want to avoid medication
- People who can sustain regular exercise
- People motivated by physical health benefits

---

### Option D: Combination Treatment

**What is it?**
- **Medication + Therapy** (most common)
- OR Medication + Exercise + Therapy
- OR Therapy + Exercise

**How well does it work?**
- ✅ **60-70%** of people significantly improve (slightly better than single treatments)
- ✅ **Best outcomes for severe depression**
- ✅ Therapy component reduces relapse risk

**Time to see benefits**: 4-6 weeks (medication faster, therapy builds over time)

**What's involved?**
- All components of chosen treatments
- Coordinating multiple approaches

**Pros**:
✅ Highest response rate (60-70%)
✅ Medication provides quicker relief while therapy teaches skills
✅ Covers multiple mechanisms (biology + psychology + behavior)
✅ Best evidence for severe depression

**Cons**:
❌ Most intensive (time, effort, multiple appointments)
❌ Medication side effects still present
❌ Higher cost (if paying for therapy)
❌ Can feel overwhelming managing multiple treatments

**Best for**:
- Severe depression (PHQ-9 ≥20)
- Treatment-resistant depression (single approach didn't work)
- People needing rapid response plus long-term skills

---

### Option E: Lifestyle Approach (Exercise + Diet + Sleep)

**What is it?**
- Comprehensive lifestyle modification
- Exercise 150 min/week + Mediterranean diet + sleep hygiene

**How well does it work?**
- ✅ Each component has evidence (exercise = medication, diet shows 32% remission in SMILES trial, sleep treatment improves depression)
- ✅ Combined effects may be additive

**Time to see benefits**: 6-12 weeks

**Pros**:
✅ No medication side effects
✅ Multiple health benefits
✅ Patient control

**Cons**:
❌ Requires sustained behavior change in multiple areas (challenging)
❌ Takes longer to see benefits (6-12 weeks)
❌ May not be sufficient for moderate-severe depression alone

**Best for**:
- Mild depression
- People highly motivated for lifestyle change
- Combining with therapy or medication for moderate-severe

### If Treatment Doesn't Work

**If no improvement after 8-12 weeks**:
- We will review your treatment
- Options: Increase dose (medication), add another treatment (e.g., therapy + medication), switch to different treatment

**This is normal** - about 40% of people need to try more than one approach before finding what works.
