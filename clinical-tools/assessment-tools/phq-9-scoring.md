# PHQ-9: Patient Health Questionnaire-9

**Purpose**: Screen for and monitor depression severity

**Validated**: Yes (extensively validated, gold standard in primary care)

**Administration time**: 2-3 minutes

**Scoring time**: <1 minute

---

## PHQ-9 Questionnaire

**Instructions to patient**: "Over the last 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by any of the following problems?"

**Response options**:
- 0 = Not at all
- 1 = Several days
- 2 = More than half the days
- 3 = Nearly every day

| # | Item | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|------|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Little interest or pleasure in doing things | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 2 | Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 3 | Trouble falling/staying asleep, or sleeping too much | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 4 | Feeling tired or having little energy | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 5 | Poor appetite or overeating | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 6 | Feeling bad about yourself - or that you are a failure or have let yourself or your family down | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 7 | Trouble concentrating on things, such as reading the newspaper or watching television | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 8 | Moving or speaking so slowly that other people could have noticed. Or the opposite - being so fidgety or restless that you have been moving around a lot more than usual | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 9 | Thoughts that you would be better off dead, or of hurting yourself in some way | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |

**Total Score**: _____ / 27

**Functional impairment question** (optional, not scored):
"If you checked off any problems, how difficult have these problems made it for you to do your work, take care of things at home, or get along with other people?"
- Not difficult at all
- Somewhat difficult
- Very difficult
- Extremely difficult

---

## Scoring and Interpretation

**Total score**: Sum of all 9 items (range 0-27)

| Score | Severity | Clinical Action |
|-------|----------|-----------------|
| **0-4** | Minimal/none | No depression. Monitor if subthreshold symptoms. |
| **5-9** | Mild | Subthreshold or mild depression. Watchful waiting + lifestyle interventions. |
| **10-14** | Moderate | Moderate depression. Offer therapy OR medication (patient choice). |
| **15-19** | Moderately severe | Moderately severe depression. Combination therapy (therapy + medication) recommended. |
| **20-27** | Severe | Severe depression. Combination therapy + close monitoring. Consider crisis team. |

## Clinical Use

### 1. Screening

**PHQ-2** (brief screen) - Ask questions 1 & 2 only:
- If **both = 0**: Very unlikely depression (98% negative predictive value)
- If **either ≥1**: Complete full PHQ-9

**When to screen**:
- Patients presenting with somatic symptoms (fatigue, pain, insomnia, weight change)
- Chronic disease (diabetes, COPD, post-MI, cancer)
- High-risk groups (postpartum, caregivers, bereaved)
- Previous history of depression

### 2. Diagnosis

**PHQ-9 supports depression diagnosis**:
- **Major Depressive Disorder criteria**: Score ≥10 AND ≥5 symptoms scored ≥2 (more than half the days), including #1 or #2
- **Other specified/unspecified depressive disorder**: Score 5-9 with functional impairment

**PHQ-9 is NOT a substitute for clinical judgment**:
- Interview to confirm diagnosis
- Rule out medical causes (hypothyroidism, anemia, sleep apnea)
- Rule out bipolar depression (screen for mania/hypomania)
- Consider context (bereavement, adjustment disorder)

### 3. Severity Assessment

**Use score to guide treatment intensity** (see `pathways/depression-pathway.md`):

**PHQ-9: 5-9** (Mild)
- **First-line**: Lifestyle (exercise, sleep, diet) + low-intensity CBT
- **NOT recommended**: Antidepressants (NICE: not cost-effective, risk>benefit for mild)

**PHQ-9: 10-14** (Moderate)
- **First-line**: Offer **patient choice** of therapy OR medication OR combination
- Use shared decision-making (see `decision-aids/depression-treatment-decision-aid.pdf`)
- Continue lifestyle interventions alongside primary treatment

**PHQ-9: 15-19** (Moderately Severe)
- **First-line**: Combination (medication + therapy) recommended
- Close monitoring (weekly contact first month)

**PHQ-9: 20-27** (Severe)
- **First-line**: Combination therapy + intensive monitoring
- **Consider**: Crisis team involvement, psychiatry referral
- **Assess**: Suicide risk (question #9), psychotic symptoms, self-neglect

### 4. Monitoring Treatment Response

**Repeat PHQ-9 regularly**:
- **Frequency**:
  - Mild-moderate: Every 4 weeks
  - Moderately severe-severe: Every 2 weeks
  - If on medication: 2 weeks (tolerability), 4-6 weeks (efficacy), 8-12 weeks (full assessment)

**Response criteria**:
- **Response**: ≥50% reduction in score OR score <10
- **Remission**: Score <5 sustained for 2+ assessments
- **Partial response**: 25-49% reduction
- **No response**: <25% reduction

**Clinical decisions based on monitoring**:
- **Response or remission**: Continue treatment, plan for continuation/maintenance phase
- **Partial response**: Optimize (increase dose, add therapy, intensify lifestyle)
- **No response at 8-12 weeks**: Change approach (switch medication, add therapy, reassess diagnosis)

**Example**:
- Baseline: PHQ-9 = 16 (moderately severe)
- 6 weeks later: PHQ-9 = 10
- **Calculation**: (16-10)/16 = 37.5% reduction (partial response)
- **Action**: Optimize treatment (increase medication dose, add therapy component, intensify lifestyle support)

## Question #9: Suicidal Ideation

**If Question #9 scored ≥1** (any suicidal thoughts):

**Always assess further** (PHQ-9 is screen, not risk assessment):
1. **Frequency**: How often? (several days vs. nearly every day)
2. **Intent**: "Do you intend to act on these thoughts?"
3. **Plan**: "Do you have a plan for how you would do this?"
4. **Means**: Access to methods (medications, ligatures)?
5. **Previous attempts**: History of self-harm or suicide attempts?
6. **Protective factors**: Reasons to live? Future plans? Relationships?

**Risk stratification**:
- **Low risk**: Fleeting thoughts, no plan, strong protective factors
  - **Action**: Safety plan, crisis numbers, remove means, close follow-up (within 1 week)
- **Moderate risk**: Frequent thoughts, vague plan, some protective factors
  - **Action**: Same-day mental health assessment (crisis team, CMHT)
- **High risk**: Constant thoughts, detailed plan, intent, access to means, previous attempts
  - **Action**: Emergency referral (psychiatric liaison, A&E, Section if refuses)

**Document**:
- PHQ-9 #9 score: ___
- Further assessment conducted: Yes / No
- Risk level: Low / Moderate / High
- Action taken: ______________________

## Advantages of PHQ-9

✅ **Brief**: 2-3 minutes (acceptable to patients and clinicians)

✅ **Validated**: Extensively studied, reliable, sensitive, specific

✅ **Free**: Public domain, no licensing fees

✅ **DSM-aligned**: Questions map directly to DSM-5 criteria for major depression

✅ **Quantitative**: Numerical score tracks change over time

✅ **Multipurpose**: Screening, diagnosis support, severity assessment, monitoring

✅ **Widely used**: Standard in primary care, allows comparison across settings

---

## Limitations

❌ **Not diagnostic**: Supports but doesn't replace clinical diagnosis

❌ **Self-report**: Relies on patient honesty and insight

❌ **Timeframe**: Only assesses last 2 weeks (may miss fluctuations)

❌ **Cultural sensitivity**: May perform differently across cultures/languages

❌ **Response bias**: Social desirability (underreporting), cry for help (over-reporting)

❌ **Question #9 is not a comprehensive suicide assessment**: Must follow up if positive

---

## Clinical Pearls

✅ **DO**:
- Use PHQ-9 systematically (screen, baseline, monitor)
- Repeat at every follow-up (track trajectory)
- Always follow up Question #9 if scored ≥1
- Use score to guide treatment intensity (severity-stratified care)
- Share score with patient ("You scored 16, which is in the moderately severe range. Let's talk about treatment options.")

❌ **DON'T**:
- Use PHQ-9 in isolation (clinical context matters)
- Ignore functional impairment (some patients score high but function well, others score moderate but very impaired)
- Rely only on total score (individual items give useful info - e.g., sleep, appetite, concentration)
- Prescribe medication based solely on score without discussion (patient values matter)
- Forget to reassess (common error: start treatment, never re-check PHQ-9)

## PHQ-9 in Special Populations

### Older Adults

- **Valid** in older adults (65+)
- **Caution**: Somatic symptoms (#3-4-5-7-8) may be due to medical comorbidities
  - Consider weighting mood/cognitive symptoms (#1-2-6-9) more heavily
- Screen for dementia if concentration issues (#7) prominent

### Pregnancy and Postpartum

- **Valid** in perinatal period
- **Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS)** alternative (designed for postpartum, less somatic focus)
- PHQ-9 acceptable if EPDS unavailable

### Chronic Medical Illness

- **Valid** but somatic symptoms may overlap with illness
- **Consider**: Use PHQ-9 but interpret in context (fatigue may be from illness, not depression)

### Adolescents

- **PHQ-9 modified for adolescents (PHQ-A)** available
- Valid in ages 13-17

## Integration into Practice

### Electronic Health Records

**Ideal setup**:
- PHQ-9 questionnaire in EHR (auto-calculates score)
- Can be completed:
  - In waiting room (tablet, paper)
  - Electronically before appointment (patient portal)
  - During consultation (clinician-administered)

**Alerts**:
- If PHQ-9 ≥10 → Suggest treatment options per pathway
- If Question #9 ≥1 → Alert to assess suicide risk
- If no follow-up PHQ-9 in 4-8 weeks → Prompt to re-assess

### Workflow

1. **Screening**: PHQ-2 or full PHQ-9 for at-risk patients
2. **Baseline**: PHQ-9 if screen positive or depression suspected
3. **Treatment planning**: Use score to guide severity-stratified treatment
4. **Monitoring**: Repeat PHQ-9 every 2-4 weeks
5. **Review**: Adjust treatment based on response
6. **Remission**: Continue monitoring every 8-12 weeks during continuation phase
7. **Relapse prevention**: Annual PHQ-9 for patients with history of depression
