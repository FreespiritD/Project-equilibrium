# Treatment Options for Depression: Quick Comparison

**What works? How do they compare? How do you choose?**

---

## You Have Choices

**There are several evidence-based treatments for depression**. Your doctor should explain all of them so you can make an informed choice based on what matters to you.

---

## The Options: Side-by-Side

| | **Psychological Therapy (CBT/IPT)** | **Antidepressant Medication (SSRI)** | **Exercise** | **Combination** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **How well does it work?** | 50-60% respond | 50-60% respond | 50-60% respond | 60-70% respond |
| **Time to work** | 6-8 weeks | 4-6 weeks | 4-8 weeks | 4-6 weeks |
| **What's involved** | 12-20 sessions, weekly, 50-60 min, homework between | Daily pill | 150 min/week moderate exercise (30 min × 5 days) | Therapy + medication + exercise |
| **Effort required** | High (active participation, practice) | Low (take pill daily) | High (sustained behavior change) | High |
| **Waiting time** | 6-18 weeks (NHS IAPT) | Immediate | Immediate | Varies |
| **Duration of treatment** | 12-20 weeks | 6-12 months minimum | Ongoing (lifestyle change) | 12+ months |
| **Relapse after stopping** | **50%** (lower) | **80%** (higher) | Low if maintained | Lower if therapy component |
| **Why prevents relapse better** | Teaches skills that persist | No skills learned; when stopped, back to baseline | Maintained benefits if habit continues | Skills + biology |
| **Side effects** | None (psychological discomfort during exposure work) | Common (see below) | Beneficial (physical health improves) | Medication side effects |
| **Cost** | Free (NHS) but waiting list; private £50-100/session | Free (NHS prescription £9.90) | Free (walking, running) to moderate (gym) | Varies |
| **Availability** | Limited (waiting lists, therapist availability) | High (any GP can prescribe) | High (universally accessible) | High |
| **Patient satisfaction** | High (80%+ satisfied) | Moderate (side effects common) | High (if habit established) | High |
| **Best for...** | Moderate-severe depression, patients wanting skills, long-term solution | Moderate-severe depression, need quick relief, patient preference | Mild-moderate depression, those able to sustain exercise, want side-effect-free option | Severe depression, treatment-resistant, rapid response needed |

## Psychological Therapy (Talking Therapy)

### Types
- **CBT** (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy): Change thinking patterns and behaviors
- **IPT** (Interpersonal Therapy): Improve relationships and social functioning
- **Behavioral Activation**: Re-engage with pleasurable activities
- All are evidence-based and equally effective

### How It Works
- Identify negative thought patterns → challenge them → develop healthier thinking
- Change behaviors that maintain depression (withdrawal, avoidance)
- Develop coping skills, problem-solving strategies
- Practice between sessions (homework)

### Pros
✅ No medication side effects
✅ **Lower relapse rate** (50% vs. 80% with medication)
✅ Teaches skills you keep forever
✅ Addresses root causes (thought patterns, behaviors)
✅ Can be used preventatively (future episodes)

### Cons
❌ Waiting lists (6-18 weeks for NHS)
❌ Requires active participation and effort
❌ Time commitment (12-20 weeks)
❌ Doesn't work for everyone (~40% don't respond)
❌ Uncomfortable at times (facing difficult emotions, changing habits)

### Access
- **NHS**: IAPT services (free, self-referral or GP referral)
- **Private**: £50-120 per session
- **Digital**: Online CBT programs (some free, some paid)

## Antidepressant Medication (Usually SSRIs)

### Common Medications
- Sertraline, fluoxetine, citalopram, escitalopram (SSRIs—first-line)
- Mirtazapine, venlafaxine (second-line if SSRIs don't work)

### How It Works
- Affects brain chemistry (serotonin, norepinephrine)
- Changes brain connections over weeks (neuroplasticity)
- **NOT "topping up" a chemical—it's more complex**

### Pros
✅ Available immediately (no waiting list)
✅ Less active effort than therapy (just take pill)
✅ Well-studied, generally safe
✅ Can be life-saving for severe depression

### Cons
❌ Side effects common (see below)
❌ Takes 4-6 weeks to work; may feel worse first 1-2 weeks
❌ **Higher relapse rate** (80% when stopped)
❌ Doesn't teach coping skills
❌ Withdrawal symptoms when stopping (must taper slowly)
❌ Long-term use concerns (some people on for years)

### Common Side Effects (30-50% experience)
- **Nausea** (first 1-2 weeks, usually improves)
- **Sexual problems** (difficulty achieving orgasm, reduced desire)—often persist
- **Weight gain** (5-10 lbs common)
- **Emotional numbness** ("I don't feel sad, but I don't feel happy either")
- **Vivid dreams**
- **Sweating, dry mouth**

### Duration
- Minimum 6 months after feeling better (first episode)
- 12+ months if recurrent depression
- Some people stay on indefinitely (if multiple relapses)

### Stopping Medication
- **Must taper slowly** (over 4+ weeks, longer if on >1 year)
- Sudden stop → withdrawal symptoms (dizziness, "brain zaps," flu-like, anxiety)
- Expect 50-80% chance of relapse (why therapy has lower relapse)

## Exercise as Treatment

### The Prescription
- **Type**: Aerobic exercise (walking, running, cycling, swimming, dancing)
- **Frequency**: 5 days per week
- **Intensity**: Moderate (can talk but not sing)
- **Duration**: 30 minutes per session
- **Total**: 150 min/week

### How It Works
- Increases BDNF (brain growth protein)—**same mechanism as antidepressants**
- Reduces stress hormones (cortisol)
- Boosts dopamine (motivation, pleasure)
- Promotes new brain cell growth
- Behavioral activation (structured activity, sense of accomplishment)

### Pros
✅ **As effective as medication** for moderate depression (effect size 0.62)
✅ No side effects—**only benefits** (physical health improves)
✅ Low relapse if habit maintained
✅ Free or low-cost
✅ Immediate access
✅ Treats anxiety and insomnia too
✅ Patient control (no prescriptions, no doctor visits)

### Cons
❌ Requires sustained effort and motivation (hardest when depressed)
❌ Time commitment (150 min/week)
❌ Takes 4-8 weeks to see mood benefits
❌ Barriers (weather, cost, energy, physical limitations, self-consciousness)
❌ Doesn't work for everyone (~40% don't respond or can't sustain)

### Support Available
- **Exercise on prescription** schemes (GP referral)
- **parkrun** (free, weekly, community running/walking)
- **Walking groups, exercise classes** (social support)
- **Apps**: Couch to 5K (NHS, free, beginner-friendly)

## Diet (Mediterranean Diet)

### The Approach
- **Eat more**: Vegetables, fruits, whole grains, fish, legumes, nuts, olive oil
- **Eat less**: Processed foods, red meat, sugar, refined grains

### Evidence
- **SMILES trial**: 32% remission with dietary support vs. 8% in control
- Mediterranean diet linked to 30% lower depression risk
- Takes 6-12 weeks to see mental health benefits

### Pros
✅ Evidence-based
✅ No side effects—improves physical health
✅ Can combine with any other treatment
✅ Accessible (can start immediately)

### Cons
❌ Requires sustained behavior change (meal planning, cooking)
❌ May be more expensive (fresh produce, fish)
❌ Takes time (6-12 weeks for benefits)
❌ Cultural adaptation needed (if not familiar with Mediterranean cuisine)

### Not a Stand-Alone Treatment
- Diet is **supportive**, not a replacement for therapy or medication in moderate-severe depression
- Best combined with other treatments

**See separate guide**: "Mediterranean Diet for Mental Health"

## Combination Treatment

### What It Is
- **Medication + Therapy** (most common combination)
- OR Medication + Exercise + Therapy
- OR Therapy + Exercise + Diet

### Evidence
- **Slightly better than either alone** for moderate-severe depression (60-70% response)
- **Best outcomes for severe depression**
- Therapy component reduces relapse risk even when medication stopped

### When Recommended
- **Severe depression** (PHQ-9 ≥20)
- **Treatment-resistant depression** (didn't respond to single approach)
- **Rapid response needed** (medication faster onset, therapy for long-term)

### Pros
✅ Highest response rate (60-70%)
✅ Covers multiple mechanisms (biology + psychology + behavior)
✅ Medication provides quicker relief while therapy teaches skills

### Cons
❌ Most intensive (time, effort, side effects)
❌ Higher cost (medication + therapy sessions)
❌ May feel overwhelming (managing multiple treatments)

## How to Choose: What Matters to YOU

### Your Preferences

**If you want to avoid medication**:
- Try therapy + exercise + diet first (if mild-moderate)
- Medication still an option if these don't work

**If you want something now** (can't wait for therapy):
- Medication available immediately
- Start lifestyle changes while waiting for therapy

**If you prefer non-pharmaceutical approach**:
- Exercise and therapy are evidence-based alternatives (not just "alternative medicine")

**If you've tried before**:
- What worked? What didn't? (Guide current choice)

---

### Severity of Depression

**Mild (PHQ-9: 5-9)**:
- **NICE recommends**: Lifestyle (exercise, sleep, diet) + low-intensity therapy
- **NOT recommended**: Medication (not cost-effective, risk>benefit)

**Moderate (PHQ-9: 10-14)**:
- **NICE recommends**: Offer choice of therapy OR medication OR both
- Patient decides based on preferences, practicality

**Moderately Severe / Severe (PHQ-9: 15-27)**:
- **NICE recommends**: Combination (therapy + medication)
- Close monitoring, consider crisis team if very severe

---

### Practical Factors

**Therapy waiting time in your area**:
- If 6-month wait → medication or digital therapy while waiting

**Cost** (if private):
- Therapy: £50-100/session × 12-20 = £600-2000
- Medication: £9.90/month (NHS) or free if exempt
- Exercise: Free to moderate

**Time commitment**:
- Therapy: 1 hour/week + homework
- Medication: <5 min/day
- Exercise: 150 min/week

**Physical health**:
- Exercise may need adaptation if physical limitations (but still possible)

**Previous episodes**:
- Recurrent depression → therapy has better relapse prevention

## The Decision Is Yours

**Your doctor should**:
✅ Explain ALL options (not just medication)
✅ Discuss pros and cons of each
✅ Support your choice (within clinical appropriateness)
✅ Use shared decision-making (your values matter)

**Your doctor should NOT**:
❌ Prescribe medication without discussing alternatives (if mild-moderate)
❌ Dismiss therapy or lifestyle as "not real treatment"
❌ Make the decision for you

**You have the right to**:
✅ Ask questions
✅ Take time to decide
✅ Try one approach first, add others if needed
✅ Change your mind (e.g., start medication, add therapy later)

---

## Questions to Ask Your Doctor

1. **"What are all my treatment options for my severity of depression?"**

2. **"What are the pros and cons of each?"**

3. **"What's the waiting time for therapy in my area?"**

4. **"If I choose medication, what are the side effects, and how long will I need to take it?"**

5. **"If I choose therapy, what type, and how do I access it?"**

6. **"Can I try exercise/lifestyle changes first before medication?"** (if mild-moderate)

7. **"What if my first choice doesn't work?"**

8. **"How will we monitor my progress?"**

9. **"What support is available while I wait for therapy?"** (if applicable)

10. **"Can I have time to think about it?"**

## Remember

✅ **Depression is treatable**—70-80% of people recover

✅ **Multiple evidence-based options exist**—not just medication

✅ **Therapy has better long-term outcomes** (lower relapse)

✅ **Medication is appropriate for moderate-severe** (not weakness to take it)

✅ **Exercise and lifestyle are treatments**, not just "common sense"

✅ **Combination may be best** for severe depression

✅ **You choose** based on your values, preferences, and circumstances

✅ **Treatment takes time**—6-12 weeks minimum; don't give up early

✅ **Asking for help is strength**, not weakness

---

## Next Steps

**After discussing with your doctor**:

1. **Decide on initial approach**:
   - Therapy
   - Medication
   - Exercise/lifestyle
   - Combination

2. **Create a plan**:
   - If therapy: How to access (IAPT referral, private)
   - If medication: Which one, when to start, follow-up schedule
   - If exercise: Specific plan (type, frequency, intensity)
   - If combination: Coordinate all components

3. **Set review date**: 4-8 weeks (check progress, adjust if needed)

4. **Start supportive measures** (regardless of primary treatment):
   - Sleep hygiene
   - Social connection
   - Reduce alcohol, manage caffeine

5. **Track your progress**:
   - PHQ-9 scores
   - Functional improvements (work, relationships, self-care)
