# Project Equilibrium Governance

## Overview

Project Equilibrium operates as an open, collaborative initiative with distributed governance. This document outlines the organizational structure, decision-making processes, and roles within the project.

## Governance Principles

1. **Openness**: Transparent processes and accessible participation
2. **Inclusivity**: Diverse perspectives from multiple stakeholders
3. **Meritocracy**: Recognition based on contribution quality and commitment
4. **Evidence-Based**: Decisions grounded in data and research
5. **Accountability**: Clear responsibilities and mechanisms for oversight
6. **Sustainability**: Long-term viability and continuity
7. **Collaboration**: Partnership across institutions and disciplines
8. **Mission-Driven**: Focused on advancing medical education and patient care

## Organizational Structure

### 1. Governance Committee

**Purpose**: Strategic oversight and high-level decision-making

**Composition**:
- 7-11 members representing diverse stakeholder groups
- Rotating chair (2-year terms)
- Representatives from:
  - Medical educators (2-3)
  - Clinicians (2-3)
  - Researchers (1-2)
  - Implementation specialists (1-2)
  - Community contributors (1-2)

**Responsibilities**:
- Define strategic direction and priorities
- Approve major initiatives and resource allocations
- Resolve escalated disputes
- Ensure financial sustainability
- Oversee working groups
- Review and update governance policies
- Approve significant changes to project scope

**Meetings**: Quarterly, with special meetings as needed

**Terms**: 3-year staggered terms, renewable once

### 2. Steering Group

**Purpose**: Day-to-day operational leadership

**Composition**:
- Project Director
- Technical Lead
- 3-5 Domain Leads (Curriculum, Clinical, Research, Implementation, Community)
- Working Group Chairs (advisory)

**Responsibilities**:
- Coordinate project activities
- Review and prioritize contributions
- Manage releases and updates
- Facilitate communication across working groups
- Monitor project health and progress
- Support contributor development
- Implement Governance Committee decisions

**Meetings**: Monthly, with ad-hoc coordination as needed

### 3. Working Groups

**Purpose**: Focused development and content creation

**Types**:
- **Curriculum Working Group**: Educational content and assessment
- **Clinical Pathways Working Group**: Clinical tools and protocols
- **Research Working Group**: Study design and evaluation
- **Implementation Working Group**: Scaling and adoption
- **Technical Working Group**: Tools, apps, and infrastructure
- **Communications Working Group**: Dissemination and outreach
- **Quality Working Group**: Standards and review processes

**Structure**:
- Chair and Co-Chair (2-year terms)
- 5-15 active members
- Open participation from community

**Responsibilities**:
- Develop content in their domain
- Review contributions
- Maintain domain-specific standards
- Coordinate with other working groups
- Report to Steering Group
- Engage community contributors

**Meetings**: Monthly or as needed

### 4. Contributors

**Who**: Anyone contributing to the project

**Levels**:
- **Community Contributors**: Occasional contributions
- **Regular Contributors**: Sustained participation (3+ contributions)
- **Core Contributors**: Significant sustained contributions (recognized by Steering Group)

**Rights**:
- Submit contributions via pull requests
- Participate in working groups
- Engage in community discussions
- Vote on working group matters (active members)
- Nominate candidates for leadership roles

**Responsibilities**:
- Follow Code of Conduct
- Adhere to contribution guidelines
- Participate constructively
- Maintain quality standards

## Decision-Making Processes

### Consensus-Based Approach

Project Equilibrium uses a tiered consensus model:

1. **Working Group Decisions** (Content and domain-specific)
   - Process: Rough consensus among active working group members
   - Timeline: 2 weeks for discussion, objections addressed
   - Approval: Chair confirms consensus or calls for vote

2. **Steering Group Decisions** (Operational and cross-cutting)
   - Process: Discussion and consensus seeking
   - Timeline: 1-2 meetings for significant decisions
   - Approval: Majority agreement, with dissenting views recorded

3. **Governance Committee Decisions** (Strategic and policy)
   - Process: Formal deliberation with stakeholder input
   - Timeline: At least one meeting cycle for major decisions
   - Approval: 2/3 majority for significant changes

### Voting Procedures

When consensus cannot be reached:

- **Simple Majority**: Routine operational decisions (50% + 1)
- **Supermajority**: Significant changes to scope, governance, or standards (2/3)
- **Unanimous**: Changes to core mission or dissolution (all voting members)

**Voting Rights**:
- Working Groups: Active members (attended 50%+ meetings in past 3 months)
- Steering Group: All members
- Governance Committee: All members

**Abstentions**: Count toward quorum but not toward majority

### Conflict of Interest

Members must:
- Disclose relevant financial interests or affiliations
- Recuse themselves from decisions where conflicts exist
- Document conflicts in meeting records

## Leadership Selection

### Nominations

- Open nomination period (4 weeks)
- Self-nominations accepted
- Nominators provide supporting statement
- Nominees confirm willingness to serve

### Election Process

**Working Group Chairs**:
- Elected by working group members
- Requires simple majority
- Annual elections

**Steering Group**:
- Domain Leads: Elected by relevant working groups
- Technical Lead: Selected by Steering Group
- Project Director: Selected by Governance Committee

**Governance Committee**:
- Nominated by stakeholder groups
- Approved by existing committee
- Elections every 3 years (staggered)

### Removal Process

Leadership can be removed for:
- Violation of Code of Conduct
- Failure to fulfill responsibilities
- Extended inactivity

Process:
1. Concerns raised with appropriate body
2. Discussion with individual
3. Formal review if issues persist
4. Vote by relevant body (2/3 majority required)

## Contribution Recognition

### Attribution System

1. **Contributor List**: All contributors acknowledged in repository
2. **Working Group Membership**: Listed in group documentation
3. **Leadership Roles**: Prominently featured in project materials
4. **Publication Authorship**: Based on contribution level and journal guidelines
5. **Letters of Recognition**: Available for institutional purposes

### Criteria for Recognition

- Quality and impact of contributions
- Sustained participation over time
- Leadership and mentorship
- Advancement of project mission
- Collaboration and community building

## Financial Governance

### Funding Sources

- Institutional partners
- Grants and awards
- Individual donations
- Service agreements

### Financial Oversight

- Transparent accounting and reporting
- Governance Committee approval for major expenditures
- Annual financial report to community
- Independent audit (when applicable)

### Resource Allocation

Priorities (in order):
1. Essential infrastructure and maintenance
2. Working group support
3. Community events and engagement
4. New initiatives and pilots
5. Recognition and contributor support

## Communication Channels

### Public Channels

- GitHub repository: Primary collaboration platform
- Community discussions: Open forums
- Mailing lists: Announcements and updates
- Website: Public-facing information
- Social media: Outreach and engagement

### Internal Channels

- Working group meetings: Domain-specific coordination
- Steering Group calls: Operational planning
- Governance Committee meetings: Strategic oversight
- Private channels: Sensitive discussions (e.g., conduct issues)

### Transparency Requirements

Public:
- Meeting agendas (1 week advance notice)
- Meeting summaries (within 2 weeks)
- Decision rationales
- Strategic plans and roadmaps
- Financial reports

Confidential:
- Personnel matters
- Code of Conduct investigations
- Sensitive institutional information
- Unpublished research data

## Amendment Process

### Proposal

- Any contributor can propose governance changes
- Proposal submitted to Governance Committee
- Include rationale and impact assessment

### Review

- Public comment period (4 weeks minimum)
- Stakeholder feedback solicited
- Working group input
- Revised based on feedback

### Approval

- Governance Committee discussion
- Vote required: 2/3 majority
- Implementation plan developed
- Community notification

### Implementation

- Phase-in period for major changes
- Documentation updates
- Training and support as needed
- Review after 6-12 months

## Conflict Resolution

### Informal Resolution

- Direct communication between parties
- Working group chair mediation
- Steering Group facilitation

### Formal Process

1. Written complaint submitted
2. Review by appropriate body
3. Investigation if warranted
4. Decision and remediation plan
5. Follow-up and monitoring

### Escalation Path

1. Working Group Chair
2. Steering Group
3. Governance Committee
4. External mediation (if needed)

## Intellectual Property

### Copyright

- Contributors retain copyright on their work
- License contributions under project license
- Derivative works follow same license

### Institutional Contributions

- Institutional contributors ensure proper authorization
- Clarify IP rights before contribution
- Document any restrictions or requirements

### Patents

- Patent-free zone for core project materials
- Patent pledges required for significant contributions
- Good-faith commitment not to assert patents

## Project Continuity

### Succession Planning

- Overlap in leadership transitions
- Documentation of key processes
- Knowledge transfer requirements
- Backup maintainers for critical functions

### Archival and Preservation

- Version control of all materials
- Regular backups
- Long-term archival plan
- DOI assignment for releases

### Project Sunset

If project concludes:
- Community notification (6 months advance)
- Archive materials in permanent repository
- Transfer to successor organization (if applicable)
- Final documentation and lessons learned
