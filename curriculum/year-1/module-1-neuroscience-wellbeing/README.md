# Module 1: Neuroscience of Wellbeing

**Year**: 1 (Pre-Clinical)
**Duration**: 10 weeks
**Credits**: Integrated into Year 1 Neuroscience block
**Contact Hours**: 30 hours (20 lectures, 10 small group)

## Module Overview

This module establishes the neuroscience foundations for understanding how psychological and behavioral interventions affect brain function and mental health. Students learn about neuroplasticity, social neuroscience, stress biology, and reward systems—providing the biological basis for holistic interventions taught throughout the EQUILIBRIUM curriculum.

### Philosophy

**"Understand the biology of holistic interventions as deeply as drug mechanisms"**

By learning that exercise increases BDNF and promotes neurogenesis with the same rigor as learning how SSRIs increase synaptic serotonin, students develop parity between pharmacological and non-pharmacological approaches.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, students will be able to:

### Knowledge (Bloom's: Remember, Understand)
1. Describe the molecular mechanisms of synaptic plasticity (LTP/LTD)
2. Explain how experience shapes brain structure throughout the lifespan
3. Describe the neurobiology of social connection and isolation
4. Explain the HPA axis and its role in stress response
5. Describe the mesolimbic dopamine pathway and its role in motivation

### Analysis (Bloom's: Analyze, Evaluate)
6. Analyze neuroimaging evidence showing brain changes from exercise, meditation, and therapy
7. Compare neuroplastic changes induced by antidepressants vs. behavioral interventions
8. Evaluate the evidence that social support buffers stress effects on the brain

### Application (Bloom's: Apply)
9. Apply knowledge of stress biology to explain mechanisms of exercise and mindfulness
10. Explain to a patient (in lay terms) how exercise changes the brain [GMC 13, 14]

## Weekly Content Overview

### Week 1: Neuroplasticity Foundations

**Key Question**: *"How does the brain change in response to experience?"*

**Content**:
- Definition of neuroplasticity
- Critical vs. sensitive periods
- Adult neuroplasticity (hippocampal neurogenesis, synaptic remodeling)
- Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)
- Clinical relevance: therapy and exercise change the brain

**Readings**:
- Duman & Aghajanian (2012). Synaptic dysfunction in depression: Potential therapeutic targets. *Science*, 338, 68-72.
- Erickson et al. (2011). Exercise training increases size of hippocampus and improves memory. *PNAS*, 108, 3017-3022.

**Case**: "Sarah, 35, has depression. Her doctor recommends exercise. Sarah asks: 'How can running change my mood? Isn't depression a chemical imbalance?' Explain using neuroplasticity concepts."

---

### Week 2: Synaptic Plasticity Mechanisms

**Key Question**: *"What are the molecular mechanisms by which synapses strengthen or weaken?"*

**Content**:
- Long-term potentiation (LTP) and long-term depression (LTD)
- NMDA receptors, calcium signaling, AMPA receptor trafficking
- Protein synthesis and structural changes
- Relationship to learning and memory
- How antidepressants and behavioral interventions both promote synaptic plasticity

**Practical**: Analyze LTP experiments (data interpretation exercise)

---

### Week 3: Social Neuroscience

**Key Question**: *"What is the neurobiology of social connection?"*

**Content**:
- Oxytocin system (release, receptors, effects on trust and bonding)
- Mirror neuron system (understanding others' actions and emotions)
- Mentalizing network (theory of mind, perspective-taking)
- Social pain and physical pain overlap (anterior cingulate cortex)

**Journal Club**: Cacioppo & Patrick (2008). Loneliness: Human nature and the need for social connection.

---

### Week 4: Loneliness and the Brain

**Key Question**: *"How does social isolation affect brain structure and function?"*

**Content**:
- Epidemiology of loneliness and depression
- Brain changes from chronic loneliness (prefrontal cortex, amygdala, hippocampus)
- Social buffering of stress (social support reduces HPA activation)
- Evolutionary perspective (loneliness as adaptive signal)

**Case**: "John, 72, widowed, isolated. His GP notes low mood. Blood tests normal. Discuss the neurobiology of John's presentation and non-pharmacological interventions."

---

### Week 5: Stress and HPA Axis

**Key Question**: *"What is the neurobiology of the stress response?"*

**Content**:
- HPA axis anatomy and physiology (hypothalamus → pituitary → adrenal)
- Cortisol: acute stress (adaptive) vs. chronic stress (maladaptive)
- Effects of chronic cortisol on brain (hippocampal atrophy, prefrontal dysfunction, amygdala hyperactivity)
- Link to depression (hypercortisolemia in subset of patients)

**Practical**: Interpret HPA axis lab data (dexamethasone suppression test)

---

### Week 6: Resilience and Coping

**Key Question**: *"Why do some people cope well with stress while others develop depression?"*

**Content**:
- Individual differences in stress reactivity (genetics, early life experience)
- Stress inoculation (moderate stress builds resilience)
- Neurobiological correlates of resilience (prefrontal regulation of amygdala)
- How exercise and mindfulness enhance stress resilience (reduce HPA reactivity, increase prefrontal control)

**Journal Club**: Review on psychological resilience and brain mechanisms

---

### Week 7: Reward Systems

**Key Question**: *"What is the neurobiology of motivation and pleasure?"*

**Content**:
- Mesolimbic dopamine pathway (VTA → nucleus accumbens)
- Dopamine and reward prediction error
- Distinction: "wanting" (motivation) vs. "liking" (pleasure)
- Role in learning, goal-directed behavior
- Natural rewards (food, social, exercise) vs. drugs of abuse

**Practical**: Analyze reward system fMRI data

---

### Week 8: Motivation and Anhedonia

**Key Question**: *"How do reward systems malfunction in depression?"*

**Content**:
- Anhedonia (loss of pleasure/motivation) as core depressive symptom
- Reduced striatal dopamine response to rewards in depression
- Learned helplessness models (reduced reward sensitivity)
- How behavioral activation and exercise restore reward function

**Case**: "Emma, 28, depressed, describes 'nothing feels good anymore.' Explain the neurobiology of anhedonia and rationale for behavioral activation + exercise."

---

### Week 9: Integration Workshop

**Key Question**: *"How do all these systems interact to produce mental health or illness?"*

**Content**:
- Synthesis of weeks 1-8
- Integrative model: stress → neuroplasticity changes → social withdrawal → reward dysfunction → depression
- How interventions work at multiple levels (exercise: ↑BDNF, ↓HPA, ↑dopamine, ↑social)
- Case discussions (biopsychosocial formulations with neurobiological mechanisms)

**Activity**: Group presentations—construct integrated neurobiological model of depression and recovery

---

### Week 10: Assessment Week

**Content**:
- Revision lecture (key concepts review)
- Practice questions
- Written exam
- OSCE station ("Explain to patient how exercise changes the brain")

## Assessment

### Formative (Learning, Not Graded)

1. **Weekly quizzes** (online, 10 questions each)
   - Immediate feedback
   - Identify knowledge gaps

2. **Case discussions** (weeks 1, 4, 8, 9)
   - Small group analysis
   - Peer teaching

3. **Practice OSCE** (week 9)
   - Explain neuroplasticity to mock patient
   - Feedback from facilitator

### Summative (Graded)

1. **Written Exam** (week 10)
   - **Duration**: 90 minutes
   - **Format**: 40 MCQs + 2 short answer questions (SAQs)
   - **Content**: 15% of Year 1 neuroscience exam
   - **Pass mark**: 60%
   - **Sample questions**:
     - MCQ: "Which of the following best explains how chronic stress leads to hippocampal atrophy? A) Cortisol inhibits BDNF synthesis..."
     - SAQ: "Explain how exercise promotes neuroplasticity, citing specific molecular mechanisms (8 marks)"

2. **OSCE Station** (end of Year 1)
   - **Station**: "Explain to this patient why exercise might help their depression"
   - **Duration**: 10 minutes
   - **Assessed**: Communication, accuracy of explanation, use of lay language
   - **Marking scheme**: Global rating scale (1-5) + checklist
   - **Pass mark**: Borderline performance (standard setting)

3. **Contribution to Portfolio** (ongoing)
   - Reflective essay: "How has learning about neuroplasticity changed your understanding of mental health treatment?"
   - **Word limit**: 500 words
   - **Assessed**: Pass/Fail (reflective depth)
