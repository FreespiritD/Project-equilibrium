By the end of this week, students will be able to:

2. **Describe** the concept of critical vs. sensitive periods in brain development [GMC 8a]
3. **Explain** evidence for adult neuroplasticity, including hippocampal neurogenesis [GMC 8a]
4. **Describe** the role of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) in synaptic plasticity [GMC 8a]

7. **Explain to a patient** in lay terms how the brain can change (communication skill) [GMC 13, 14]

## Key Concepts

### 1. Definition of Neuroplasticity

**Neuroplasticity**: The brain's ability to reorganize itself by forming new neural connections throughout life.

**Types**:
- **Structural**: Changes in brain anatomy (volume, connections)
- **Functional**: Changes in how brain regions operate
- **Synaptic**: Changes in strength of connections between neurons

**Time scales**:
- **Rapid** (seconds-minutes): Synaptic strengthening (LTP)
- **Intermediate** (hours-days): Protein synthesis, dendritic spine growth
- **Long-term** (weeks-months): Neurogenesis, myelination, cortical reorganization

### 2. Critical vs. Sensitive Periods

**Critical period**:
- Specific window when brain MUST receive certain input
- If missed, function permanently impaired
- Example: Monocular deprivation (visual cortex)

**Sensitive period**:
- Window when brain is especially responsive to input
- Can still learn after, but less efficiently
- Example: Language acquisition

**Clinical relevance**:
- Importance of early intervention for neurodevelopmental conditions
- BUT: Adult brain also plastic (depression treatment works at any age)

### 3. Adult Neuroplasticity

**Evidence for adult plasticity**:

1. **London taxi drivers** (Maguire et al., 2000):
   - Larger posterior hippocampus (spatial navigation)
   - Correlation with years of experience
   - **Implication**: Adult human brain changes structure with intensive use

2. **Hippocampal neurogenesis** (Eriksson et al., 1998):
   - New neurons generated in adult human hippocampus
   - ~700 new neurons/day
   - **Function**: Memory, mood regulation, stress response

3. **Skill learning** (e.g., juggling, musical instruments):
   - Structural changes in motor cortex, cerebellum
   - **Implication**: "Use it or lose it" principle

**Implications for mental health**:
- Depression NOT fixed "chemical imbalance"
- Brain CAN change in response to therapy, exercise, environmental enrichment
- **Plasticity = hope** (recovery possible at any age)

### 4. Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF)

**What is BDNF?**
- Protein that promotes neuron survival, growth, differentiation
- High concentrations in hippocampus, cortex
- **Function**: Supports synaptic plasticity, neurogenesis

**BDNF in depression**:
- **Lower BDNF** in depressed individuals (blood, postmortem brain)
- Stress reduces BDNF expression
- Hippocampal atrophy in chronic depression (may relate to low BDNF)

**How treatments increase BDNF**:
1. **Antidepressants** (SSRIs): Increase BDNF after weeks of treatment
2. **Exercise**: Increases BDNF acutely and chronically (dose-dependent)
3. **Cognitive therapy**: Increases BDNF (emerging evidence)
4. **Enriched environment**: Increases BDNF in animals (social, novelty)

**Mechanism**:
- BDNF binds TrkB receptor → signaling cascades → gene expression → protein synthesis → synaptic strengthening and new synapse formation

**Clinical relevance**:
- **Common mechanism**: Antidepressants and behavioral interventions may work via overlapping pathways (BDNF upregulation)
- **Time course**: Both take weeks (time for structural brain changes)
- **Rationale for exercise**: Not just "distraction" but biological mechanism

### 5. Neuroimaging Evidence for Brain Changes

**Key Study: Erickson et al. (2011) - Exercise Increases Hippocampus Size**

**Design**:
- RCT: Aerobic exercise vs. stretching control (120 older adults, 1 year)
- MRI: Measure hippocampal volume before and after
- Cognition: Memory tests

**Results**:
- **Exercise group**: +2% hippocampal volume
- **Control group**: -1.4% volume (age-related atrophy)
- Exercise group had **better memory**
- **Mechanism**: Increased BDNF (blood levels correlated with volume increase)

**Interpretation**:
- Exercise doesn't just "prevent decline"—it REVERSES age-related atrophy
- Effect size comparable to years of aging
- **Implication**: Exercise is neuroplasticity intervention

**Other neuroimaging evidence**:

1. **Meditation** (8-week MBSR):
   - Increased gray matter in hippocampus, posterior cingulate, temporo-parietal junction
   - Decreased in amygdala (stress response)

2. **Cognitive therapy** for depression:
   - Normalized prefrontal cortex and amygdala activity
   - Similar changes to antidepressant medication

3. **Musical training**:
   - Enlarged auditory cortex, motor cortex, corpus callosum
   - Demonstrates domain-specific plasticity

### 6. Clinical Implications

**Old model**:
- Depression = "chemical imbalance"
- Treatment = restore chemical balance with medication
- Brain structure fixed

**New model (neuroplasticity-informed)**:
- Depression = maladaptive neural circuits (stress → synaptic changes)
- Treatment = promote adaptive neuroplasticity (strengthen resilience circuits)
- Brain structure changeable

**Treatment implications**:
- **Exercise**: Not adjunct, but mechanistic treatment (increases BDNF, neurogenesis, circuit remodeling)
- **Therapy**: Not "talking cure" but brain-changing intervention (rewires circuits)
- **Medication**: One way to promote plasticity, but not the only way
- **Time course**: All take weeks because structural change takes time

**Patient education**:
- "Your brain is not broken, it's stuck in a pattern"
- "Exercise literally grows your hippocampus"
- "Therapy rewires circuits, just like medication does"
- **Empowerment**: You can change your brain (agency, not passive recipient)

**Part 4: Preview Next Week** (15 min)

### Small Group Tutorial (1 hour)

**Activity**: Case-based discussion

**Case**:
> "David, 42, software engineer, diagnosed with major depressive disorder. PHQ-9 = 16 (moderate). His GP recommends sertraline. David has read that 'antidepressants only work by correcting a chemical imbalance' and asks: 'If I take medication, will my brain go back to normal? Will I need it forever?'"

**Discussion questions**:
1. How would you explain the neuroplasticity model of depression to David?
2. Compare how sertraline and exercise both increase BDNF—what does this tell us about mechanisms?
3. David is worried about taking medication long-term. How does neuroplasticity inform duration of treatment?
4. If David prefers not to take medication, what evidence-based alternatives could you discuss?

**Learning points**:
- Depression not "chemical imbalance" but circuit dysfunction
- Multiple pathways to promote neuroplasticity (medication, exercise, therapy)
- Neuroplasticity takes time (weeks) → explains delayed onset of all treatments
- Recovery = adaptive circuit remodeling, not just "restoring chemicals"

### Common Student Questions

**Q: "If exercise increases BDNF like antidepressants, why prescribe antidepressants?"**
- A: Both effective; choice depends on severity, patient preference, practical barriers. Combination may be optimal.

**Q: "How much exercise is needed to increase hippocampus volume?"**
- A: Erickson study: 40 min walking, 3x/week for 1 year. Dose-response not fully known. Module 3 will cover prescribing details.

**Q: "Can you 'over-plasticity' the brain?"**
- A: Interesting question. Plasticity usually adaptive, but maladaptive plasticity exists (e.g., chronic pain sensitization, addiction). Balance needed.
