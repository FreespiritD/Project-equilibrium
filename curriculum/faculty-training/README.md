# EQUILIBRIUM Faculty Training Program

## Overview

Faculty are the key to successful EQUILIBRIUM implementation. Many current faculty were trained in the traditional biomedical model and may lack expertise in:
- Evidence base for lifestyle and psychological interventions
- Comparative effectiveness (therapy vs. medication)
- Motivational interviewing and behavior change
- Social prescribing and community resources

The EQUILIBRIUM Faculty Training Program provides comprehensive preparation to teach this curriculum with confidence and competence.

## Training Philosophy

### Core Principles

1. **Evidence-first**: Faculty must understand the research base to teach it credibly
2. **Skills-focused**: Not just knowledge transfer, but teaching students HOW to deliver interventions
3. **Experiential**: Faculty experience mindfulness, MI techniques themselves
4. **Community of practice**: Ongoing support, not one-time training
5. **Scholarship**: Faculty contribute to curriculum research and improvement

### Goals

By completing training, faculty will be able to:
- **Teach** all EQUILIBRIUM curriculum content with accuracy and enthusiasm
- **Facilitate** skills workshops (CBT, MI, lifestyle prescription)
- **Assess** students using OSCEs, portfolios, and WBAs
- **Supervise** clinical students in holistic depression management
- **Model** evidence-based holistic care in their own clinical practice

## Training Structure

### Initial Training (20-25 hours total)

**Required for all faculty teaching EQUILIBRIUM content**

#### 1. Online Modules (20 hours self-paced)

**Access**: EQUILIBRIUM faculty platform (login provided)

**Modules**:

**Module 1: Evidence Base for Holistic Interventions** (5 hours)
- Exercise and depression (mechanisms, RCTs, meta-analyses)
- CBT vs. medication (efficacy, relapse prevention)
- Diet and mental health (SMILES trial, Mediterranean diet)
- Sleep interventions (CBT-I)
- Social prescribing
- **Assessment**: 20-question quiz (80% required to pass)

**Module 2: Comparative Effectiveness and Guideline-Concordant Prescribing** (4 hours)
- NICE guidelines (depression, anxiety)
- Inappropriate prescribing patterns (UK data)
- Shared decision-making frameworks
- When medication IS appropriate (moderate-severe depression)
- **Assessment**: Case-based scenarios (5 cases, prescribe appropriately)

**Module 3: Teaching Skills for Medical Education** (5 hours)
- Flipped classroom methods
- Facilitating case-based discussions
- Teaching psychomotor skills (demonstration, practice, feedback)
- Giving effective feedback
- **Assessment**: Video analysis exercise (critique teaching video)

**Module 4: Assessment and Standard Setting** (3 hours)
- OSCE examiner training (marking objectively)
- Portfolio assessment (using rubrics)
- Workplace-based assessments (Mini-CEX, CBD)
- **Assessment**: Calibration exercise (mark 5 OSCE videos, compare to gold standard)

**Module 5: Clinical Supervision of EQUILIBRIUM Students** (3 hours)
- Supervising holistic assessments
- Observing lifestyle prescription
- Feedback on biopsychosocial formulations
- Managing difficult scenarios (student wants to prescribe inappropriately)
- **Assessment**: Reflective essay on supervision philosophy

**Completion**:
- Pass all 5 module assessments
- **Certificate**: EQUILIBRIUM Teaching Faculty Level 1

#### 2. In-Person Workshop (3 days, 24 hours)

**When**: Intensive 3-day course (Friday-Sunday or spread over term)

**Location**: Hosted at pilot medical schools

**Format**: Interactive, skills-focused, small groups (12-15 faculty max)

**Day 1: Evidence and Mechanisms** (8 hours)

- **Morning**: Deep dive into evidence (guest lecture from EQUILIBRIUM research team)
  - Systematic reviews discussion
  - Mechanisms (neuroplasticity, inflammation, gut-brain axis)
  - Comparative effectiveness

- **Afternoon**: Teaching the evidence
  - How to explain neuroplasticity to Year 1 students
  - Facilitating journal clubs
  - Practice: Deliver 10-min mini-lecture (peer feedback)

**Day 2: Skills Teaching and Assessment** (8 hours)

- **Morning**: Teaching intervention skills
  - Demonstrate: How to teach motivational interviewing
  - Workshop: Faculty practice MI on each other (role-play)
  - Demonstrate: How to teach behavioral activation

- **Afternoon**: Assessment
  - OSCE examiner practice (mark live students, calibrate)
  - Portfolio assessment (use rubrics on example portfolios)
  - Feedback skills (give developmental feedback to simulated student)

**Day 3: Implementation and Community** (8 hours)

- **Morning**: Integrating into existing curriculum
  - Curriculum mapping exercise (identify where to embed EQUILIBRIUM)
  - Challenges and solutions (panel discussion)
  - Local adaptation strategies

- **Afternoon**: Community of practice launch
  - Introduction to ongoing support network
  - Scholarship opportunities (research on curriculum effectiveness)
  - Action planning: What will you do in first term?
  - Q&A with experienced EQUILIBRIUM faculty

**Completion**:
- Attend all 3 days
- Complete teaching observation (deliver 10-min segment)
- Pass OSCE examiner calibration exercise
- **Certificate**: EQUILIBRIUM Teaching Faculty Level 2 (fully certified)

### Ongoing Development (Continuous)

**Required for faculty to maintain certification**

#### Annual 1-Day Update (8 hours)

**When**: Each academic year (September)

**Format**: 1-day conference at participating school

**Content**:
- Updates to evidence base (new RCTs, meta-analyses)
- Curriculum refinements (based on evaluation data)
- Sharing best practices (faculty present innovations)
- Keynote from EQUILIBRIUM leadership
- Networking

**Requirement**: Attend at least 3 out of 5 years to maintain certification

#### Monthly Webinars (1 hour, optional but encouraged)

**Format**: Online, lunchtime (12-1pm)

**Topics**:
- "Teaching MI: Common student mistakes and how to correct them"
- "Facilitating difficult discussions (when students are skeptical of holistic approaches)"
- "Assessing portfolios efficiently"
- "New research update: Exercise and depression"
- Open Q&A sessions

**Archive**: Recorded for asynchronous viewing

#### Peer Observation (Annual)

**Process**:
1. Pair with another EQUILIBRIUM faculty member
2. Observe each other teach (1 session each)
3. Give structured feedback (using observation form)
4. Reflect on own teaching

**Benefit**: Continuous improvement, faculty connection

#### Scholarship and Research (Optional)

**Opportunities**:
- Contribute to EQUILIBRIUM evaluation research
- Publish teaching innovations
- Present at medical education conferences
- Develop new curriculum materials (paid consultancy)

**Support**:
- Research mentorship from EQUILIBRIUM team
- Access to evaluation data
- Co-authorship opportunities
- Conference travel grants

2. **Academic**:
   - EQUILIBRIUM Teaching Faculty certificate (recognized credential)
   - Publications and conference presentations
   - Promotion criteria (teaching innovation, medical education scholarship)

3. **Professional Development**:
   - Learn evidence-based approach to depression (improve own clinical practice)
   - Teaching skills enhancement (beneficial beyond EQUILIBRIUM)
   - Leadership opportunities (become regional EQUILIBRIUM lead)

4. **Intrinsic**:
   - Many faculty frustrated with over-prescribing, want to teach better approach
   - Student enthusiasm for holistic care is rewarding
   - Alignment with values (patient-centered, evidence-based care)

## Quality Assurance

### Faculty Evaluation

**Student feedback** (end of module):
- "Faculty were knowledgeable about evidence base" (Likert 1-5)
- "Faculty facilitated skills practice effectively"
- "Faculty modeled holistic approach in clinical teaching"

**Peer observation**:
- Teaching quality assessed by peer (structured form)
- Developmental feedback provided

**Student outcomes**:
- Exam pass rates (flagged if module has unusually low pass rate)
- OSCE performance (are students meeting competencies?)

**Response to poor performance**:
- Developmental feedback meeting
- Additional training/mentorship
- Observation by experienced faculty
- If persists, may not continue teaching EQUILIBRIUM

### Continuous Improvement

**Annual faculty survey**:
- What's working well?
- What needs improvement?
- What additional support needed?

**Incorporate feedback**:
- Update training materials
- Add new webinar topics
- Revise curriculum based on faculty input

## Certification Levels

### Level 1: EQUILIBRIUM Teaching Faculty (Online Only)
- Completed 20-hour online modules
- Passed all assessments
- **Can**: Co-teach lectures, facilitate small groups with supervision

### Level 2: EQUILIBRIUM Teaching Faculty (Fully Certified)
- Completed Level 1 + 3-day workshop
- Passed teaching observation and examiner calibration
- **Can**: Lead modules independently, examine OSCEs, supervise clinical students

### Level 3: EQUILIBRIUM Master Educator (Optional Advanced)
- 3+ years teaching EQUILIBRIUM
- Scholarly contributions (publications, presentations)
- Peer-nominated and reviewed
- **Recognition**: Lead regional faculty development, higher honoraria, invited speaker at annual conference

### For New Faculty

**Onboarding checklist**:
1. Complete online modules (20 hours, self-paced)
2. Attend 3-day workshop (next available date)
3. Shadow experienced EQUILIBRIUM faculty (1 module)
4. Co-teach with mentor (1 module, observed)
5. Independent teaching with mentor backup (ongoing)

**Timeline**: 3-6 months from start to independent teaching

## FAQs

**Q: I'm a basic scientist (not clinician). Can I teach EQUILIBRIUM?**
A: Yes! Modules 1-6 (Year 1) are basic science. Clinical context provided but neuroplasticity, nutrition, psychology are your expertise.

**Q: I don't have time for 3-day workshop.**
A: We understand. We offer multiple dates throughout the year, including weekends and term breaks. Online modules flexible (self-paced). We're exploring condensed 2-day intensive option.

**Q: I'm skeptical that exercise is as effective as antidepressants.**
A: Great! Critical thinking welcome. Online modules present evidence in detail (RCTs, meta-analyses, effect sizes). Day 1 workshop includes critical appraisal. If unconvinced after reviewing evidence, we can discuss further.

**Q: Will this replace my existing teaching?**
A: Ideally no. EQUILIBRIUM integrates into existing modules (e.g., Year 1 Neuroscience). You'd add neuroplasticity content, reduce outdated neuroanatomy. Net time neutral. Some faculty shift from pure lecturing to facilitating skills workshops (more rewarding).

**Q: What if my medical school doesn't adopt EQUILIBRIUM?**
A: Training still valuable for your own clinical practice and teaching. If your school later adopts, you're ahead. You can also advocate for adoption (we can help).
