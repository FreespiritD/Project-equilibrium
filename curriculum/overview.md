# EQUILIBRIUM Curriculum: Detailed Overview

## Executive Summary

The EQUILIBRIUM curriculum represents a transformative reform in medical education, integrating evidence-based holistic interventions throughout the standard 4-year medical school program. Rather than adding separate modules, EQUILIBRIUM content is **embedded into existing curriculum**, replacing outdated material and ensuring future doctors are equipped to prescribe exercise, diet, sleep interventions, psychotherapy, and social support alongside—and sometimes instead of—traditional pharmacological approaches.

This document provides a comprehensive narrative overview of the curriculum's design, implementation strategy, and expected outcomes.

---

## The Problem: Why This Curriculum Is Needed

### Current State of Medical Education

**Medical schools currently teach**:
- **Pharmacology**: Extensive (50-100 hours on psychopharmacology)
- **Psychotherapy**: Minimal (10-20 hours, primarily didactic)
- **Lifestyle medicine**: Almost none (1-5 hours total)
- **Social prescribing**: Rarely mentioned

**Result**: Doctors graduate with...
- Deep knowledge of antidepressant mechanisms, dosing, side effects
- Superficial awareness that "therapy works"
- Virtually no knowledge of exercise, diet, or sleep as treatments
- No skills in delivering brief psychological interventions
- No understanding of comparative effectiveness

### Current Prescribing Patterns

**UK data** (see `docs/01-evidence-base/prescribing-data/`):
- **23% of adults** on antidepressants (83 million prescriptions/year)
- **67% of prescriptions** don't follow NICE guidelines
- **Most common deviation**: Prescribing first-line for mild depression without trying alternatives
- **50%** on medication >2 years without review
- **Therapy-to-prescription ratio**: 1:55 (1.5M therapy referrals vs. 83M prescriptions)

**This is not malice—it's training**. Doctors prescribe what they were taught.

### The Evidence Gap

**What the evidence shows** (see `docs/01-evidence-base/systematic-reviews/`):
- **Exercise**: Equivalent to SSRIs for moderate depression (ES=0.62, NNT=5)
- **CBT**: Equivalent to medication acutely, BETTER for relapse prevention (50% vs. 80% relapse)
- **Diet**: SMILES trial showed Mediterranean diet superior to social support (remission 32% vs. 8%)
- **Sleep**: CBT-I treats insomnia and improves depression (dual benefit)
- **Combination**: Therapy + lifestyle often better than either alone

**What medical students learn**: Medication first, maybe refer to therapy if fails

**The disconnect**: Evidence doesn't match education

---

## The Solution: EQUILIBRIUM Curriculum

### Core Philosophy

**"Evidence-based holistic medicine as the DEFAULT, not the alternative"**

Key principles:
1. **Integration, not addition**: Embed into existing curriculum, don't create separate track
2. **Replace, don't add**: Remove outdated content to make space
3. **Skills-focused**: Not just "therapy works" but HOW to deliver brief interventions
4. **Comparative effectiveness**: Teach relative benefits/harms of all approaches
5. **Patient choice**: Empower doctors to offer genuine options
6. **Graduated difficulty**: Year 1 (mechanisms) → Year 2 (interventions) → Years 3-4 (practice)

### What Makes This Different

**Not "complementary medicine"** (unproven alternatives):
- EQUILIBRIUM teaches Grade A/B evidence interventions
- Comparable or superior to medication in many cases
- Recommended by NICE guidelines but underutilized

**Not "lifestyle advice"** (vague wellness tips):
- Specific, protocolized interventions
- Evidence-based dosing (e.g., 150 min/week moderate exercise)
- Taught with same rigor as pharmacotherapy

**Not "psychiatry only"** (siloed in mental health rotation):
- Integrated throughout 4 years
- Relevant to all specialties (depression common in cardiology, oncology, etc.)
- Builds progressively like pharmacology teaching

## Curriculum Architecture

### The Spiral Model

**Concept**: Same topics revisited at increasing depth/application

**Example - Exercise and Depression**:

**Year 1 (Foundations)**:
- Module 3: Movement as Medicine
- **Focus**: Mechanisms (BDNF, neurogenesis, HPA axis)
- **Level**: Understand the biology
- **Assessment**: Written exam on mechanisms

**Year 2 (Intervention)**:
- Module 7: Evidence-Based Psychotherapy (includes behavioral activation with exercise)
- **Focus**: How to prescribe (FITT principle, motivational interviewing)
- **Level**: Practice skills in simulated consultations
- **Assessment**: OSCE - prescribe exercise program

**Year 3-4 (Clinical Application)**:
- Depression Module: Real patients
- **Focus**: Apply in clinical context, troubleshoot barriers
- **Level**: Supervised practice with feedback
- **Assessment**: Workplace-based assessment, portfolio

**Graduation**: Competency in exercise prescription for depression

### Integration Strategy

**How to embed without adding curriculum time?**

#### 1. Replace Outdated Content

**Example - Year 1 Neuroscience**:
- **Remove**: Excessive neuroanatomy memorization (e.g., 6 hours on cranial nerve nuclei - not used clinically)
- **Add**: 6 hours on neuroplasticity, stress neurobiology, social neuroscience (clinically relevant, explains treatment mechanisms)

**Example - Year 2 Pharmacology**:
- **Remove**: Outdated drugs no longer used (e.g., MAOIs in detail)
- **Add**: Comparative effectiveness (SSRIs vs. CBT vs. exercise - helps prescribing decisions)

#### 2. Shift From Passive to Active Learning

**Example - Psychiatry Lectures**:
- **Current**: 20 lectures on descriptive psychopathology
- **EQUILIBRIUM**: 10 lectures (same content condensed), 10 skills workshops (CBT techniques, MI, lifestyle prescription)
- **Same time, more skills**

#### 3. Integrate Into Clinical Skills Teaching

**Example - Communication Skills OSCEs**:
- **Current**: Generic "breaking bad news" stations
- **EQUILIBRIUM**: Add stations on shared decision-making for depression, motivational interviewing for behavior change
- **Uses existing OSCE infrastructure**

## Year-by-Year Detailed Overview

### Year 1: Foundations (Pre-Clinical)

**40 weeks of content integrated into Year 1 curriculum**

#### Philosophy
**"Understand the biology of holistic interventions as deeply as drug mechanisms"**

Students learn the scientific basis for why exercise, diet, sleep, social connection, and psychological interventions affect brain function and mental health. This creates parity: pharmacology is taught alongside "behaviorology."

#### Modules

**Module 1: Neuroscience of Wellbeing (10 weeks)**
- **When**: Integrated into Year 1 Neuroscience block
- **Content**:
  - Neuroplasticity (LTP/LTD, BDNF, neurogenesis)
  - Social neuroscience (oxytocin, mirror neurons, loneliness effects)
  - Stress biology (HPA axis, cortisol, resilience)
  - Reward systems (dopamine, motivation, anhedonia)
- **Integration example**: Week 2 of neuroscience - instead of memorizing hippocampal subdivisions, learn how exercise increases hippocampal volume (clinically relevant neuroplasticity)
- **Assessment**: Written exam (15% of neuroscience exam)

**Module 2: Nutritional Medicine (6 weeks)**
- **When**: Integrated into Year 1 Biochemistry/Nutrition block
- **Content**:
  - Epidemiology (diet quality and depression rates)
  - Gut-brain axis (microbiome, vagus nerve, inflammation)
  - Nutritional deficiencies (B12, folate, vitamin D, omega-3)
  - Evidence from RCTs (SMILES, HELFIMED trials)
- **Integration example**: Instead of just biochemistry of vitamins, learn clinical implications for mental health
- **Assessment**: Written exam + dietary history OSCE

**Module 3: Movement as Medicine (6 weeks)**
- **When**: Integrated into Year 1 Physiology block
- **Content**:
  - Exercise physiology
  - Mechanisms (BDNF, neurogenesis, inflammation, HPA axis)
  - Epidemiology and RCT evidence
  - Comparative effectiveness (exercise vs. SSRIs)
- **Integration example**: Week on cardiovascular physiology expanded to include mental health benefits of exercise
- **Assessment**: Written exam + OSCE (explain exercise to patient)

**Module 4: Sleep and Circadian Biology (4 weeks)**
- **When**: Integrated into Year 1 Neuroscience/Physiology
- **Content**:
  - Sleep architecture and regulation
  - Circadian rhythms
  - Sleep and mental health (bidirectional relationship)
  - CBT-I principles
- **Integration example**: Sleep physiology lecture expanded to include psychiatric implications
- **Assessment**: Written exam + sleep history OSCE

**Module 5: Psychology of Human Experience (8 weeks)**
- **When**: Integrated into Year 1 Psychology/Psychiatry introduction
- **Content**:
  - Normal emotion regulation
  - Behavioral psychology (conditioning, learning)
  - Cognitive psychology (Beck's model)
  - Developmental psychology
- **Integration example**: Replaces purely descriptive psychopathology with mechanistic understanding
- **Assessment**: Written exam

**Module 6: Social Determinants of Health (6 weeks)**
- **When**: Integrated into Year 1 Public Health block
- **Content**:
  - Social epidemiology
  - Socioeconomic deprivation and mental health
  - Loneliness and social connection
  - Social prescribing
- **Integration example**: Public health module expanded to include mental health inequalities
- **Assessment**: Written exam + essay

#### Year 1 Outcomes
**By end of Year 1, students can**:
- Explain biological mechanisms of holistic interventions with same depth as drug mechanisms
- Describe the evidence base for non-pharmacological approaches
- Understand that depression has multiple causal pathways (not just "chemical imbalance")

#### Year 1 Challenges and Solutions

**Challenge**: "Too much content already"
- **Solution**: Replace outdated/low-yield material
- **Example**: Reduce excessive neuroanatomy (memorizing tract names), add clinically relevant neuroplasticity

**Challenge**: "Students want concrete facts, not 'soft' psychology"
- **Solution**: Teach psychology as rigorously as any basic science (mechanisms, RCTs, effect sizes)
- **Example**: BDNF upregulation from exercise taught like drug mechanisms

### Year 2: Mechanisms and Interventions (Pre-Clinical)

**24 weeks of content integrated into Year 2 curriculum**

#### Philosophy
**"Learn HOW to deliver evidence-based interventions, not just that they exist"**

Year 2 shifts from mechanisms to practical skills. Students practice taking holistic histories, delivering brief psychological interventions, prescribing lifestyle changes, and using motivational interviewing.

#### Modules

**Module 7: Evidence-Based Psychotherapy (10 weeks)**
- **When**: Integrated into Year 2 Psychiatry/Clinical Skills
- **Content**:
  - Evidence base (meta-analyses, comparative effectiveness)
  - CBT theory and techniques (thought records, behavioral activation)
  - IPT, BA, MBCT (overview)
  - Motivational interviewing
  - Shared decision-making
- **Integration example**: Psychiatry module includes 5 lectures (evidence) + 5 workshops (skills practice)
- **Skills taught**:
  - Conduct a CBT thought record with patient
  - Deliver behavioral activation (activity scheduling)
  - Use MI techniques (OARS - open questions, affirmations, reflections, summaries)
  - Present treatment options with balanced info on benefits/harms
- **Assessment**: OSCE (2 stations: brief BA intervention, shared decision-making)

**Module 8: Mind-Body Practices (6 weeks)**
- **When**: Integrated into Year 2 Psychiatry
- **Content**:
  - Mindfulness evidence base (MBCT for relapse prevention)
  - Mechanisms (attention regulation, meta-awareness, Default Mode Network)
  - Yoga and movement therapies
  - **Experiential**: Students practice mindfulness (personal wellbeing)
- **Integration example**: Week on anxiety/stress management includes mindfulness training
- **Assessment**: Reflective portfolio + OSCE (teach patient brief mindfulness)

**Module 9: Biopsychosocial Formulation (8 weeks)**
- **When**: Integrated into Year 2 Psychiatry/Clinical Skills
- **Content**:
  - Biopsychosocial model in depth
  - Holistic assessment (sleep, diet, activity, social, stress, substances)
  - PHQ-9, GAD-7 (use and limitations)
  - Diagnostic nuance (MDD vs. adjustment disorder vs. normal distress)
  - Treatment planning and stepped care
- **Skills taught**:
  - Comprehensive psychiatric history
  - Construct biopsychosocial formulation
  - Develop individualized treatment plan
  - Recognize when medication IS indicated vs. alternatives first
- **Assessment**: Portfolio case study + OSCE (holistic assessment)

#### Year 2 Outcomes
**By end of Year 2, students can**:
- Deliver brief psychological interventions (BA, thought challenging, MI)
- Prescribe lifestyle interventions (exercise, diet, sleep) with specific recommendations
- Use shared decision-making to present treatment options
- Formulate cases using biopsychosocial framework
- **Ready for clinical years**: Can assess and manage depression holistically under supervision

#### Year 2 Challenges and Solutions

**Challenge**: "Students aren't therapists, why teach psychotherapy?"
- **Solution**: Not training as therapists, but teaching:
  - When to refer to therapy (most cases)
  - Brief interventions GPs can deliver (BA, sleep hygiene)
  - How to explain therapy to patients (so they accept referral)

**Challenge**: "Not enough time to practice skills"
- **Solution**: Flipped classroom
  - Pre-class: Video lectures (theory, evidence)
  - In-class: Skills practice with simulated patients
  - Maximize active learning time

### Years 3-4: Clinical Application (Clinical Years)

**10+ weeks dedicated EQUILIBRIUM clinical experience + integration throughout all rotations**

#### Philosophy
**"Practice holistic care with real patients under supervision"**

Students apply Year 1-2 knowledge in clinical settings. EQUILIBRIUM is not confined to psychiatry rotation—integrated throughout all clinical placements (depression common in cardiology, oncology, etc.).

#### Clinical Modules

**Depression Module (4 weeks)**
- **Setting**: Primary care + psychiatric outpatient/inpatient
- **Structure**:
  - Week 1: Primary care (GP surgery) - see new depression presentations
  - Week 2-3: Psychiatric outpatient - see moderate-severe cases, therapy clinics
  - Week 4: Inpatient psychiatry - see severe, treatment-resistant depression
- **Clinical activities**:
  - **Assess** 15-20 patients with depression (varied severity)
  - **Practice**:
    - Holistic history and formulation
    - Shared decision-making consultations
    - Prescribe lifestyle interventions (supervised)
    - Deliver brief BA interventions
    - Prescribe antidepressants when indicated (with informed consent about alternatives)
  - **Observe**:
    - IAPT therapy sessions (CBT, BA)
    - Multidisciplinary team meetings
    - Social prescribing link worker consultations
- **Supervision**: Weekly supervision with GP/psychiatrist
- **Assessment**:
  - Mini-CEX (observed consultations)
  - Case-based discussions
  - Portfolio (3 case studies demonstrating holistic approach)

**Anxiety Module (4 weeks)**
- **Setting**: Primary care + specialist anxiety service
- **Structure**: Similar to depression module
- **Focus**:
  - Assessment of anxiety disorders (GAD, panic, social anxiety, OCD)
  - Exposure-based interventions (explain rationale, deliver brief exposure)
  - Avoid inappropriate benzodiazepine prescribing
  - Appropriate SSRI prescribing for anxiety
- **Assessment**: Same as depression module

**Trauma & PTSD Module (2 weeks)**
- **Setting**: Specialist trauma service
- **Focus**:
  - Trauma-informed care
  - Screening sensitively
  - Understand PTSD symptoms
  - Refer appropriately to trauma-focused therapy (EMDR, TF-CBT)
- **Assessment**: Observed consultation + reflective essay

**Integrated Clinical Cases (Throughout Years 3-4)**
- **All rotations**: Students identify and holistically manage depression/anxiety
- **Examples**:
  - **Cardiology**: Post-MI depression (prescribe cardiac rehab + exercise, address mood)
  - **Oncology**: Cancer-related depression (psycho-oncology referral, meaning-making)
  - **Surgery**: Pre-operative anxiety (teach brief relaxation, avoid benzos)
- **Portfolio requirement**: 5 cases across different specialties showing EQUILIBRIUM approach

#### Years 3-4 Outcomes
**By graduation, students can**:
- **Independently assess** depression/anxiety with holistic biopsychosocial formulation
- **Offer patients genuine choice** of therapy, medication, or lifestyle interventions with balanced evidence
- **Prescribe lifestyle interventions** with specific, evidence-based recommendations
- **Deliver brief psychological interventions** (BA, sleep hygiene, MI)
- **Prescribe antidepressants appropriately** (moderate-severe depression, informed consent, not first-line for mild)
- **Recognize inappropriate prescribing** and apply NICE guidelines
- **Work in MDT** (psychiatry, psychology, social work, social prescribing)

#### Years 3-4 Challenges and Solutions

**Challenge**: "Clinical placements too busy for teaching"
- **Solution**:
  - Dedicated EQUILIBRIUM modules (4+4+2 weeks)
  - Not additional teaching burden—replaces some traditional psychiatry time
  - Students still meet GMC requirements for psychiatry exposure

**Challenge**: "Supervisors not trained in EQUILIBRIUM approach"
- **Solution**: Faculty training program (see `faculty-training/`)
  - 20-hour online modules
  - 3-day in-person workshops
  - Certification required to supervise EQUILIBRIUM students

## Assessment Strategy

**Principle**: "What you assess is what students learn"

If EQUILIBRIUM is not assessed, students won't prioritize it. Assessment must be:
- **Summative** (counts toward grades)
- **Comprehensive** (knowledge, skills, attitudes)
- **Progressive** (across all 4 years)

### Assessment Methods

#### 1. Written Exams (Knowledge)

**Year 1-2**:
- **15% of exam content** = EQUILIBRIUM topics
- **Question types**:
  - Mechanisms (e.g., "Explain how exercise increases BDNF and promotes neurogenesis")
  - Evidence base (e.g., "Compare efficacy of CBT vs. sertraline in moderate depression")
  - Guidelines (e.g., "According to NICE, when should antidepressants be first-line?")

**Years 3-4**:
- Integrated into clinical exams (e.g., extended matching questions on depression management)

#### 2. OSCEs (Skills)

**Year 1**: 1-2 stations
- Example: "Explain to this patient why exercise might help their depression" (communication)
- Example: "Take a dietary history" (history-taking)

**Year 2**: 2 stations (required)
- Example: "This patient has low mood and inactivity. Deliver a brief behavioral activation intervention" (15 min station)
- Example: "This patient is considering antidepressants. Use shared decision-making to discuss options including therapy and lifestyle changes" (10 min station)

**Year 3**: 2 stations
- Example: "Assess this patient presenting with depression and develop a management plan" (20 min station)

**Year 4**: 2-3 stations (includes graduation competency stations)
- **Mandatory stations**:
  - Holistic assessment and formulation
  - Lifestyle prescription
  - Shared decision-making

**Passing EQUILIBRIUM OSCEs required to graduate**

#### 3. Portfolio (Attitudes + Reflective Practice)

**Components**:
1. **Personal wellbeing reflections** (Years 1-4)
   - "How do you manage stress?"
   - "Have you tried evidence-based practices?"
   - Promotes self-care, reduces burnout

2. **Case studies** (Years 2-4)
   - 3 cases demonstrating biopsychosocial formulation
   - Evidence of holistic management
   - Reflection on decision-making

3. **Prescribing log** (Years 3-4)
   - Log 10 lifestyle prescriptions (exercise, diet, sleep)
   - Log 5 antidepressant prescriptions (with justification)
   - Self-audit against NICE guidelines

4. **Feedback compilation** (Years 3-4)
   - Supervisor feedback on holistic approach
   - Patient feedback
   - Peer feedback

**Portfolio assessed**: Pass/Fail with rubric
- **Fail = cannot graduate**: Ensures engagement

#### 4. Workplace-Based Assessments (Years 3-4)

**Mini-CEX** (Mini-Clinical Evaluation Exercise):
- Observed clinical encounters
- Specific EQUILIBRIUM mini-CEX: Supervisor observes student assess depression and discuss treatment options
- Feedback on holistic approach, shared decision-making

**CBD** (Case-Based Discussion):
- Student presents case, supervisor probes reasoning
- Must include biopsychosocial formulation
- Discussion of why treatment choice made

**Minimum requirements**:
- 4 EQUILIBRIUM mini-CEX (Years 3-4)
- 4 EQUILIBRIUM CBD (Years 3-4)

### Competency Requirements for Graduation

**Cannot graduate without demonstrating**:
1. ✅ Holistic assessment (biological, psychological, social)
2. ✅ Lifestyle prescription skills (exercise, diet, sleep)
3. ✅ Brief psychological intervention (BA or MI)
4. ✅ Shared decision-making for depression treatment
5. ✅ Guideline-concordant prescribing

**Method**: Graduation OSCE must include stations testing these competencies

## Pedagogical Innovations

### 1. Flipped Classroom

**Traditional**: Lecture in class, homework alone
**EQUILIBRIUM**: Video lectures before class, skills practice in class

**Example - Week 3, Module 7 (CBT Thought Records)**:
- **Pre-class (30 min video)**: Theory of automatic thoughts, cognitive distortions, thought record structure
- **In-class (2 hours)**:
  - Demonstrate thought record with simulated patient (15 min)
  - Students practice in pairs (60 min)
  - Debrief and feedback (30 min)
  - Quiz (15 min)

**Benefits**:
- Maximizes active learning time
- Students can re-watch videos at own pace
- Class time for skill development (can't do alone)

### 2. Deliberate Practice

**Concept**: Practice with immediate feedback improves skills faster

**EQUILIBRIUM approach**:
- **Simulated patients**: Trained actors for OSCE practice
- **Microteaching**: Practice 5-min segments, get feedback, repeat
- **Peer practice**: Students practice on each other (BA, MI)

**Example**: Students practice exercise prescription 5 times with feedback before assessed OSCE

### 3. Interleaving

**Concept**: Mix topics rather than blocking (improves retention)

**Traditional**: All neuroplasticity week 1, all HPA axis week 2, etc.
**EQUILIBRIUM**: Revisit each topic multiple times across years

**Example - HPA Axis**:
- **Year 1, Week 3**: Basic HPA physiology
- **Year 1, Week 6**: HPA axis in depression
- **Year 1, Week 8**: How exercise modulates HPA axis
- **Year 2, Module 8**: Mindfulness and HPA axis
- **Year 3, Depression module**: Apply to patient assessment

### 4. Case-Based Learning

**Concept**: Learn through realistic clinical scenarios

**EQUILIBRIUM cases** (integrated throughout):
- **Year 1**: Written cases (e.g., "Why does Jane, who exercises regularly, have lower depression risk than Susan, who is sedentary?")
- **Year 2**: Interactive cases (online branching scenarios - choose management, see outcomes)
- **Years 3-4**: Real patients

**Benefits**:
- Contextual learning (not abstract facts)
- Develops clinical reasoning
- Memorable (stories stick)

### 5. Interprofessional Education

**Concept**: Learn with students from other professions

**EQUILIBRIUM opportunities**:
- **Joint workshops** with nursing, psychology, social work students
- **Shared lectures** on evidence base
- **MDT simulation**: Medical, nursing, psychology students manage case together

**Benefits**:
- Understand other professions' roles
- Practice teamwork
- Reduces professional silos

## Addressing Common Concerns

### "Medical school is already overloaded"

**Response**:
- EQUILIBRIUM **replaces** outdated content, doesn't add
- **Examples of removed content**:
  - Excessive neuroanatomy (memorizing details not used clinically)
  - Outdated drugs (MAOIs in excessive detail)
  - Purely descriptive psychopathology (shift to mechanistic)
- Net: Same curriculum hours, better content

**Curriculum mapping**: Schools provide current curriculum, EQUILIBRIUM team identifies replacement opportunities

### "Students can't be therapists in 4 years"

**Response**:
- **Agree!** Not training as therapists
- **Goal**:
  - Deliver brief interventions GPs use (BA, sleep hygiene)
  - Know when to refer to specialist therapy (most cases)
  - Explain therapy to patients (increase acceptance of referrals)
- **Analogy**: GPs prescribe SSRIs but refer complex cases to psychiatrists. Same model for therapy.

### "The evidence isn't strong enough"

**Response**:
- **Exercise, CBT, diet**: Grade A evidence (Cochrane reviews, large RCTs, meta-analyses)
- **Often equivalent or superior** to medication
- **NICE guidelines recommend** these approaches (implementation gap, not evidence gap)
- **Higher bar than many medications**: E.g., antidepressants have modest effect sizes (0.3) vs. exercise (0.62)

### "This is anti-medication"

**Response**:
- **No—it's anti-INAPPROPRIATE-medication**
- **EQUILIBRIUM teaches**:
  - Medication appropriate for moderate-severe depression
  - Combination (medication + therapy + lifestyle) often best
  - Medication not appropriate first-line for mild depression (NICE)
- **Goal**: Right treatment for right patient at right time

### "Pharmaceutical industry will oppose"

**Response**:
- **Possible** (though SSRIs mostly generic now, less industry profit)
- **Counter-strategy**:
  - Emphasize guideline concordance (NICE, GMC)
  - Frame as improving prescribing quality
  - Protect academic freedom (medical schools resist industry influence)
  - Public support (patients want options)

## Sustainability Plan

**After pilot funding ends, how do schools maintain EQUILIBRIUM?**

### Built-In Sustainability

1. **Embedded curriculum**: Not a separate program requiring special funding
2. **Faculty trained**: Once trained, faculty can continue teaching (minimal ongoing cost)
3. **Materials repository**: Online platform (open-access) for lectures, OSCEs, exam questions
4. **Standard practice**: GMC adoption makes it required (schools must maintain)

### Ongoing Support (Years 6+)

1. **EQUILIBRIUM Teaching Faculty network**:
   - Self-sustaining community of practice
   - Annual conference (cost-neutral, registration fees)
   - Online forum (free)

2. **Content updates**:
   - Small central team (3 FTE) maintains materials
   - Evidence review every 2 years
   - Schools access updated content (subscription model, £5K/year covers costs)

3. **Scholarship**:
   - Faculty research on curriculum effectiveness
   - Publications generate academic credit (promotion)
   - Sustains interest and continuous improvement

### Financial Model (Long-term)

**Year 1-5 (Pilot/Rollout)**: Funded by grants (£5-10M total)
**Year 6+**:
- Medical schools (£5K/year subscription for updated materials)
- Academic publications (open-access with APC funding)
- Conferences (cost-neutral)
- **Estimate**: £500K/year operating costs, covered by subscriptions

## Conclusion

The EQUILIBRIUM curriculum is a comprehensive, evidence-based reform that integrates holistic interventions throughout medical education. By embedding this content into existing curriculum (rather than adding to it), training faculty, assessing competencies, and evaluating outcomes, EQUILIBRIUM aims to transform medical education and, ultimately, prescribing practice.

**The problem is clear**: Over-prescribing, under-utilization of alternatives, training gap

**The evidence is strong**: Exercise, CBT, diet, sleep are effective, often comparable to medication

**The solution is feasible**: Integration (not addition), pilot → national rollout, faculty training

**The time is now**: Rising prescribing rates, growing public concern, NICE guidelines not implemented

**EQUILIBRIUM is the path forward**: From education to practice to patient care
