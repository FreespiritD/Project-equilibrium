## Recognition Principles

### Attribution Standards

We follow these principles:
- **Substantial contribution**: Listed contributors have made significant, identifiable contributions
- **Consent required**: All individuals consent to being listed
- **Appropriate credit**: Credit matches level and type of contribution
- **Collective recognition**: Some contributions recognized collectively (e.g., anonymous patient stories)
- **Institutional acknowledgment**: Institutional affiliations listed with permission

### Privacy

- Patient and public contributors listed only with explicit consent
- Option for anonymous contribution always available
- Affiliations listed only with permission
- Contact information not included without consent

### Types of Recognition

**Named contributors**:
- Steering committee and leadership
- Working group chairs
- Significant content developers
- Research collaborators
- Patient advisors (with consent)

**Collective acknowledgment**:
- Working group members
- Anonymous patient contributors
- Workshop participants
- Survey respondents
- Pilot site staff

**Institutional partners**:
- Pilot schools and practices
- Research institutions
- Supporting organizations
- Funding bodies

## Special Acknowledgments

### Inspirational Work

This project builds on pioneering work in:
- **Lifestyle medicine**: Evidence base for exercise, nutrition, and lifestyle interventions
- **Collaborative care**: Integration of mental health in primary care
- **Medical education reform**: Innovations in competency-based education
- **Patient-centered care**: Shared decision-making and patient empowerment
- **Implementation science**: Systematic approaches to healthcare transformation

We acknowledge the thousands of researchers, clinicians, educators, and patient advocates whose work makes EQUILIBRIUM possible.

### Historical Context

Special recognition to:
- Early researchers documenting effectiveness of holistic interventions
- Clinicians who persisted in integrative approaches despite system barriers
- Medical educators who advocated for curriculum reform
- Patients who shared their experiences and advocated for choice
- Policy makers who supported innovation and evidence-based change
