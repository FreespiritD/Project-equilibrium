# Evidence Base for Project EQUILIBRIUM

## Overview

This directory contains the comprehensive scientific evidence base that underpins Project EQUILIBRIUM's mission to transform mental health care through medical education reform and holistic treatment pathways. The evidence demonstrates that:

1. **Lifestyle interventions** (exercise, diet, sleep) are as effective as medication for mild-to-moderate depression
2. **Psychotherapy** shows comparable acute efficacy to medication with superior long-term outcomes
3. **Integrated approaches** combining multiple interventions show the best outcomes
4. **Current prescribing practices** in the UK often don't follow evidence-based guidelines
5. **Alternative models** exist internationally with better balance of treatment options

## Evidence Grading System

All evidence is graded using a simplified system based on GRADE methodology:

**Grade A** (Strong recommendation, high-quality evidence):
- Multiple high-quality RCTs or systematic reviews
- Consistent findings
- Large effect sizes or clear clinical benefit
- Low risk of bias

**Grade B** (Moderate recommendation, moderate-quality evidence):
- Some high-quality evidence with limitations
- Some inconsistency
- Moderate effect sizes
- Moderate risk of bias

**Grade C** (Weak recommendation, low-quality evidence):
- Limited high-quality evidence
- Inconsistent findings
- Small effect sizes or unclear benefit
- Higher risk of bias

## Key Findings Summary

### Lifestyle Interventions

**Exercise for Depression** (Grade A):
- Effect size: SMD = 0.62-0.82 (large)
- Comparable to medication acutely
- Superior long-term (8-15% relapse vs. 30-50% with medication)
- Additional health benefits (cardiovascular, metabolic, cognitive)

**Mediterranean Diet** (Grade A):
- 33% reduced depression risk (RR = 0.67)
- SMILES trial: 32% remission rate vs. 8% control
- Anti-inflammatory mechanisms
- Cost-effective (£550 per remission)

**Sleep Interventions (CBT-I)** (Grade A):
- Large effect on insomnia (SMD = 1.07)
- Moderate to large effect on depression (d = 0.47-0.87)
- Addresses bidirectional relationship
- No side effects

**Mindfulness** (Grade A):
- Moderate to large effects for anxiety (SMD = 0.63-0.89)
- MBCT for relapse prevention: 43% reduction
- NICE-recommended for recurrent depression

### Psychotherapy

**CBT, BA, IPT, PST** (Grade A):
- Effect sizes: d = 0.62-0.92 (moderate to large)
- Comparable to medication acutely
- Superior for relapse prevention (31% vs. 76% relapse after discontinuation)
- Skills persist after treatment ends
- Cost-effective

### Comparative Effectiveness

**Exercise vs. SSRIs**:
- Acute phase: No significant difference
- Long-term: Exercise superior (lower relapse)
- Exercise: Additional health benefits, no side effects
- SSRIs: Easier, slightly faster onset

**CBT vs. Medication**:
- Acute phase: Comparable efficacy
- Long-term: CBT superior (enduring effects)
- Combination: Best outcomes for moderate-severe depression

**Integrated Approaches**:
- Multiple interventions > single interventions
- Addresses multifactorial nature of depression
- Synergistic mechanisms
- Best practice for moderate-severe and treatment-resistant

### Prescribing Patterns

**UK Trends**:
- 23% of adults on antidepressants (83 million prescriptions/year)
- Tripled since 2008
- 50% on >2 years without regular review
- 67% of prescriptions not guideline-concordant

**International Comparisons**:
- UK among highest globally (132 DDDs/1000/day)
- Netherlands: 68 DDDs (similar depression rates, better therapy access)
- Demonstrates high prescribing not inevitable

**Inappropriate Prescribing**:
- Only 33% of prescriptions follow NICE guidelines
- Most common deviation: Prescribing first-line for mild depression
- Drivers: Time pressure, limited alternatives, diagnostic uncertainty

## Using This Evidence Base

### For Medical Education

- **Pre-clinical years**: Introduce evidence for lifestyle interventions, understand mechanisms
- **Clinical years**: Apply in patient care, practice holistic assessment
- **GP training**: Master brief interventions, referral pathways, shared decision-making

### For Clinical Practice

- **Assessment**: Screen across multiple domains (mood, sleep, activity, diet, social)
- **Treatment planning**: Offer evidence-based options, shared decision-making
- **Implementation**: Stepped care, integrated approaches
- **Monitoring**: Track outcomes, adjust based on response

### For Patient Education

- **Empowerment**: Understand all options, not just medication
- **Realistic expectations**: Timeframes, effort required, outcomes
- **Shared decision-making**: Patient preferences central

### For Policy and Advocacy

- **Demonstrate need**: Current prescribing patterns not evidence-based
- **Show feasibility**: International examples of better balance
- **Economic case**: Lifestyle interventions and therapy cost-effective
- **Implementation pathway**: Medical education + practice support

## How This Evidence Supports EQUILIBRIUM's Mission

### Problem Identified

**Current state**:
- 23% of UK adults on antidepressants (among highest globally)
- 67% of prescriptions don't follow NICE guidelines
- Alternatives underutilized (55:1 ratio of prescriptions to therapy referrals)
- Long-term use without review (50% on >2 years)

### Evidence-Based Solution

**EQUILIBRIUM addresses through**:

1. **Medical Education Reform**
   - Teach evidence for lifestyle interventions
   - Emphasize integrated, holistic approaches
   - Practice shared decision-making
   - **Evidence**: Future doctors prescribe appropriately from start

2. **GP Practice Support**
   - Pathways for holistic assessment
   - Training in brief interventions
   - Enhanced payment for comprehensive care
   - **Evidence**: Current practices can change with support

3. **Patient Empowerment**
   - Information on all options
   - Tools for shared decision-making
   - **Evidence**: Patients prefer non-medication when offered

4. **System Change**
   - Policy advocacy
   - Payment reform
   - Capacity building (therapy, social prescribing)
   - **Evidence**: System-level change feasible (international examples)

### Target Outcomes (Year 10)

Based on evidence, EQUILIBRIUM targets:
- **50% reduction in inappropriate prescribing** (from 67% to 30-35% inappropriate)
- **40% improvement in long-term mental health outcomes** (through better treatment matching, lower relapse)
- **30% reduction in mental health-related healthcare costs** (prevention, relapse reduction, fewer side effects)
- **80% of new medical graduates competent** in holistic intervention delivery

**These targets are evidence-based and achievable** given the efficacy of lifestyle interventions, psychotherapy, and integrated approaches documented in this evidence base.
