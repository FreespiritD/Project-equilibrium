# International Antidepressant Prescribing Comparisons

## Summary

Antidepressant prescribing rates vary dramatically across countries, from **4-5% in some Asian and Eastern European countries to 23-26% in UK, Iceland, and Australia**. These variations reflect differences in healthcare systems, cultural attitudes, diagnostic practices, and - critically - availability of alternative treatments. International comparisons demonstrate that high prescribing rates are not inevitable.

## OECD Data (2023)

### Highest Prescribing Countries

**Defined Daily Doses (DDDs) per 1000 population per day**:

1. **Iceland**: 153 DDDs (highest globally)
2. **Australia**: 145 DDDs
3. **UK**: 132 DDDs
4. **Portugal**: 128 DDDs
5. **Sweden**: 123 DDDs
6. **Belgium**: 118 DDDs
7. **Spain**: 115 DDDs
8. **Canada**: 110 DDDs
9. **Denmark**: 108 DDDs
10. **USA**: 105 DDDs

**Translation to population prevalence**: ~18-26% of adults on antidepressants

### Moderate Prescribing Countries

- **Germany**: 75 DDDs (~12-14%)
- **France**: 70 DDDs (~11-13%)
- **Italy**: 55 DDDs (~8-10%)
- **Netherlands**: 68 DDDs (~11%)

### Lower Prescribing Countries

- **Slovakia**: 47 DDDs (~7%)
- **Estonia**: 42 DDDs (~6-7%)
- **Latvia**: 40 DDDs (~6%)
- **Hungary**: 38 DDDs (~5-6%)
- **Poland**: 35 DDDs (~5%)
- **South Korea**: 28 DDDs (~4%)
- **China**: 22 DDDs (~3-4%)*

*Note: Data quality varies; some countries underreport

## What Explains Variation?

### Healthcare System Factors

**Universal healthcare with GP gatekeeping** (UK, Nordic countries):
- **Higher prescribing**: GPs prescribe readily
- Limited specialist referral
- Time-pressured consultations
- **Easy access** to prescriptions

**Insurance-based systems** (USA, France, Germany):
- **More variable**: Depends on insurance coverage
- Specialist access easier (psychiatrists may use therapy more)
- Cost barriers to therapy

**Out-of-pocket dominant** (Eastern Europe, Asia):
- **Lower prescribing**: Cost barrier to antidepressants
- Also lower diagnosis rates
- Stigma barriers

### Availability of Psychotherapy

**Countries with robust therapy systems** (Netherlands, Germany):
- **Lower prescribing** relative to depression prevalence
- **Therapy reimbursed**: Patients can access
- **Cultural acceptance**: Therapy normalized

**Countries with limited therapy access** (UK, Portugal):
- **Higher prescribing**: Therapy wait lists
- **Default to medication**: Easier to provide

**Example - Netherlands**:
- **Lower prescribing** than UK (68 vs. 132 DDDs)
- **Similar depression rates** (~10-12%)
- **Difference**: Therapy widely available, reimbursed, utilized

### Cultural Attitudes

**Anglo-Saxon countries** (UK, Australia, USA, Canada):
- **Medicalization of distress**: Medical solutions preferred
- **"Chemical imbalance"** narrative widespread
- **Less stigma** about mental illness → more help-seeking

**Mediterranean countries** (Italy, Greece):
- **Lower diagnosis**: Stigma higher
- **Family support**: Traditional coping mechanisms
- **Later adoption**: Prescribing rising but from lower baseline

**Asian countries**:
- **Stigma very high**: Mental illness highly stigmatized
- **Underdiagnosis and undertreatment**: Major issue
- **Low prescribing** ≠ good care (often untreated)

**Nordic countries** (high prescribing):
- **Low stigma**: Help-seeking encouraged
- **Preventive approach**: Screen and treat
- **But**: May over-medicalize

### Diagnostic Practices

**Screening culture** (UK, Iceland):
- **PHQ-9 screening** common in primary care
- Identifies more cases (including mild)
- **Threshold for treatment**: Lower (may medicate mild)

**Symptom-based diagnosis** (varying internationally):
- Different thresholds for diagnosis
- Cultural variations in symptom expression

**Specialist diagnosis** (some European countries):
- Psychiatrist confirms diagnosis
- **Higher bar** for treatment

### Pharmaceutical Industry Influence

**Direct-to-consumer advertising** (USA, New Zealand only):
- **Increases demand**: Patients request medication
- **Controversial**: Banned in most countries

**Marketing to physicians** (globally):
- Historically very influential (1990s-2000s)
- **Now restricted** in Europe more than USA
- **Legacy effect**: Prescribing culture established

### Social Safety Nets

**Strong social support systems** (Nordic countries, some Western Europe):
- **Paradox**: High prescribing despite good welfare
- **Possible**: More help-seeking due to less stigma

**Weak safety nets** (USA, UK austerity):
- **Prescribing for social problems**: Poverty, unemployment treated with medication
- **Medicalization of inequality**

## Trends Over Time (OECD Average)

**1990-2000**: Dramatic increase (SSRI introduction)
- **SSRIs marketed** as safe, effective
- 2-3x increase in prescribing
- **Watershed moment**: Prozac (fluoxetine) 1988

**2000-2010**: Continued increase, slower
- Growing recognition of limitations
- But momentum continued

**2010-2023**: Plateauing in some countries, continued growth in others
- **UK, Australia, Iceland**: Continued increase
- **Germany, France**: Plateau or slight decline
- **Eastern Europe, Asia**: Increase from low base

**COVID-19 impact (2020-2023)**:
- **10-20% increase** in many countries (pandemic mental health crisis)
- May persist beyond pandemic

## Country Deep-Dives

### United States

**Prescribing**: ~105 DDDs (18-20% of adults)

**Unique features**:
- **Direct-to-consumer advertising**: "Ask your doctor about..."
- **Insurance-driven**: Many plans cover medication but not therapy (or limited sessions)
- **Specialist access**: Easier than many countries, but unequal

**Trends**:
- Prescribing increased steadily 1990-2015
- Plateaued recently
- **Opioid crisis response**: Increased monitoring, some spillover to antidepressants

**Comparison to UK**: Similar rates, different drivers

### Germany

**Prescribing**: ~75 DDDs (12-14% of adults)

**Lower than UK/Australia/USA**

**Reasons**:
- **Therapy reimbursed**: Patients entitled to psychotherapy
- **"Psychotherapy culture"**: Normalized, preferred by many
- **Specialist gatekeeping**: Psychiatrists involved in diagnosis

**Lessons for EQUILIBRIUM**: Therapy access reduces prescribing

### Netherlands

**Prescribing**: ~68 DDDs (11% of adults)

**Lower than UK despite universal healthcare**

**Reasons**:
- **Robust therapy system**: Well-developed, reimbursed
- **Stepped care**: Clear pathways from self-help → therapy → medication
- **GP training**: Emphasis on psychosocial interventions

**Model system**: Balance of approaches

### France

**Prescribing**: ~70 DDDs (11-13% of adults)

**Unique features**:
- **Benzodiazepines**: Higher use than other countries (historical preference)
- **Psychoanalysis tradition**: Therapy common (though changing to CBT)
- **Medication + therapy**: Often combined

### Iceland

**Prescribing**: 153 DDDs (**highest globally, ~26-28% of adults**)

**Why so high?**
- **Long winters**: Seasonal affective disorder (SAD) high
- **Small population**: Cultural factors amplified
- **Universal screening**: Very proactive
- **Low stigma**: Help-seeking encouraged
- **Easy access**: Small country, quick prescriptions

**Concern**: May be over-prescribing

### South Korea

**Prescribing**: ~28 DDDs (4% of adults)

**Very low compared to Western countries**

**Reasons**:
- **High stigma**: Mental illness highly stigmatized
- **Underdiagnosis**: Major issue
- **Family shame**: Reluctant to seek help
- **Traditional medicine**: Some use alternatives

**Not a model**: Low prescribing reflects poor access, not good alternatives

### Japan

**Prescribing**: ~40 DDDs (6-7% of adults)

**Lower than Western countries**

**Reasons**:
- **Stigma**: High, though decreasing
- **Diagnostic practices**: Conservative
- **"Adjustment disorder"**: Often diagnosed instead of depression (less severe label)
- **Prescribing rising**: Catching up to Western countries

## Lessons for EQUILIBRIUM

### High Prescribing ≠ Inevitable

**Netherlands and Germany** show that comparable healthcare systems can maintain lower prescribing through:
1. **Robust therapy access**
2. **Stepped care pathways**
3. **Cultural acceptance of therapy**

**EQUILIBRIUM goal**: Shift UK towards this model

### Therapy Access Is Key

**Countries with good therapy access** prescribe less
- Not because depression rates lower
- But because **alternatives available**

**EQUILIBRIUM strategy**: Expand and normalize alternatives

### Cultural Change Possible

**Prescribing is partly cultural**:
- Medicalization of distress
- "Pill for every ill" mentality
- Can be shifted through education

**EQUILIBRIUM approach**: Medical education + public awareness

### Screening Without Alternatives = Over-Prescribing

**Iceland, UK**: High screening + limited alternatives = high prescribing

**Better model**: Screening + diverse treatment options

### Balance Needed

**Too high** (Iceland, UK, Australia): Over-medicalization
**Too low** (South Korea, Eastern Europe): Undertreatment, stigma
**Goldilocks** (Netherlands, Germany): ~10-12% treated, diverse approaches

**EQUILIBRIUM target**: Reduce UK from 23% to 12-15% (appropriate level)

## Data Limitations

**Comparing prescribing data internationally**:
- Different data collection methods
- Prescription ≠ consumption
- Private prescriptions captured variably
- Indication not always known (antidepressants used for pain, anxiety, etc.)
- Depression prevalence estimates vary (diagnostic criteria, stigma)

**Despite limitations**: Clear patterns emerge (high vs. low prescribing countries)

## Conclusion

International comparisons reveal that **UK's high prescribing rates (23% of adults) are not inevitable**. Countries like Netherlands and Germany maintain rates around 11-12% while providing excellent mental health care through **robust therapy systems and stepped care**. EQUILIBRIUM aims to shift the UK towards this balanced model, reducing inappropriate prescribing while ensuring those who need treatment receive evidence-based care.
