# Project EQUILIBRIUM Evidence Brief

**15-minute read**

## The Evidence Problem

Despite 23% of the UK population being prescribed antidepressants:
- Disability from depression is **increasing**
- Only 33% of prescriptions follow **guidelines**
- 50% of patients **discontinue** medication prematurely
- GPs report having "**no alternative**" to prescribing

Meanwhile, robust evidence exists for lifestyle and psychological interventions that are:
- ✓ As effective as medication for mild-moderate presentations
- ✓ More durable in long-term outcomes
- ✓ Preferred by many patients
- ✓ Addressing root causes, not just symptoms

**Yet these interventions remain dramatically underutilized.**

## What the Evidence Shows

### 1. Exercise is as Effective as Medication

**91 meta-analyses** examining exercise for depression and anxiety demonstrate:
- **Effect size comparable to antidepressants** (SMD = 0.62-0.82)
- **Works for**: Depression, anxiety, stress
- **Dose**: 150 minutes/week moderate activity optimal
- **Additional benefits**: Physical health, social connection, self-efficacy
- **Side effects**: All positive

**Key Evidence:**
- Schuch et al. (2016): Meta-analysis of 25 RCTs, N=1,487 - large effect size
- Cooney et al. (2013): Cochrane review confirming effectiveness
- Stonerock et al. (2015): Effect comparable to psychotherapy and medication

### 2. Psychological Interventions Work

**Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT):**
- **Hundreds of RCTs** demonstrate effectiveness
- **Effect sizes**: Large for both depression (d=0.67) and anxiety (d=0.84-1.14)
- **Long-term**: More durable than medication (lower relapse rates)
- **Deliverable**: In primary care via IAPT, online, or brief protocols

**Behavioral Activation:**
- **As effective as full CBT** (d=0.78)
- **Advantages**: Simpler, deliverable by GPs with training
- **Primary care RCT**: Ekers et al. (2014) - BA effective in routine practice

**Mindfulness:**
- **Prevents relapse** in recurrent depression (RR=0.66)
- **Comparable to maintenance medication** for relapse prevention
- **Stress and anxiety**: Moderate to large effects

### 3. Lifestyle Factors are Crucial

**Nutrition:**
- **Mediterranean diet**: 30% reduced depression risk
- **RCT evidence**: SMILES trial - dietary intervention improved depression (d=1.16)
- **Ultra-processed foods**: Increase depression risk (RR=1.22)

**Sleep:**
- **Sleep problems predict** depression and anxiety
- **Treating insomnia improves** mental health (d=0.47)
- **CBT for insomnia (CBT-I)**: Effective and improves comorbid conditions

**Social Connection:**
- **Loneliness predicts depression** (OR=2.5)
- **Social prescribing**: Improves mental health and wellbeing
- **Group activities**: Enhance adherence and add social benefit

### 4. Integrated Care is Superior

**Collaborative Care:**
- **Meta-analysis of 79 RCTs**: Superior to usual care (d=0.32)
- **Cost-effective**: £5000/QALY in UK studies
- **Components**: Care coordination, stepped care, specialist consultation

**Stratified Care:**
- **Matching intensity to need** improves outcomes
- **More efficient** resource utilization

## Comparative Effectiveness: Intervention vs. Medication

| Factor | Psychological/Lifestyle | Antidepressants |
|--------|------------------------|-----------------|
| **Acute effectiveness (mild-moderate)** | Equivalent | Equivalent |
| **Long-term outcomes** | More durable | Higher relapse rates |
| **Side effects** | Beneficial | 30-50% adverse |
| **Addresses root causes** | Yes | No |
| **Patient preference** | 75% prefer if equally effective | 25% |
| **Physical health** | Improves | Neutral/negative |
| **Cost** | Low-moderate | Low for medication, high for consequences |

**Key Point**: For mild-to-moderate depression and anxiety, non-pharmaceutical interventions are as effective acutely and superior long-term.

**For severe presentations**: Medication has important role; combination treatment often optimal.

## The Implementation Gap

Despite this evidence, lifestyle and psychological interventions are underutilized because:

### 1. Medical Education Gap
- **Current training** emphasizes pharmaceutical approaches
- **Limited education** in lifestyle medicine and psychological techniques
- **No supervised practice** in delivering these interventions
- **Culture created**: Prescribing-oriented

**Result**: Doctors graduate without competency in evidence-based alternatives.

### 2. System Barriers
- **10-minute appointments**: Insufficient for holistic assessment
- **Payment models**: Favor quick prescribing over comprehensive care
- **Limited access**: To psychological services and lifestyle programs
- **No guidelines integration**: Evidence not incorporated into routine pathways

**Result**: GPs feel they have no alternative to prescribing.

### 3. Evidence-Practice Gap
- **Knowledge isn't enough**: Knowing evidence doesn't change practice
- **Skills required**: Delivering interventions requires training
- **System support needed**: Time, resources, reimbursement
- **Culture change**: Requires comprehensive approach

**Result**: Evidence-based interventions remain underutilized despite known effectiveness.

## The EQUILIBRIUM Solution

**Comprehensive approach addressing root causes:**

### 1. Transform Medical Education
**What**: Integrate holistic approaches throughout medical curriculum
- Year 1: Understanding biopsychosocial model
- Year 2: Skills in psychological and lifestyle interventions
- Years 3-4: Supervised clinical practice

**Evidence**: Educational interventions change practice patterns; experiential learning essential for skill acquisition.

### 2. Redesign Clinical Pathways
**What**: Evidence-based algorithms for common presentations
- Holistic biopsychosocial assessment
- Full range of treatment options
- Shared decision-making tools
- Monitoring and adjustment protocols

**Evidence**: Clinical decision support improves guideline adherence; shared decision-making enhances outcomes and satisfaction.

### 3. Reform System Support
**What**: Enable holistic care through system change
- Enhanced payments for comprehensive assessments
- Access to psychological and lifestyle services
- Quality metrics emphasizing appropriate prescribing
- Ongoing practitioner training and support

**Evidence**: Payment reform changes practice; implementation support increases adoption; quality metrics drive improvement.
