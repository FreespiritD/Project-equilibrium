# Sleep Interventions for Depression: Systematic Review

## Key Findings

**Evidence Grade**: A (Strong recommendation, high-quality evidence)

**Effect Size**: Large (SMD = 0.65-1.07 for insomnia; d = 0.47 for depression)

**Recommendation**: Sleep interventions, particularly CBT for insomnia (CBT-I), should be offered as first-line treatment for depression with comorbid insomnia and considered for all depression presentations

## Summary

Sleep disturbances affect 80-90% of people with depression and are bidirectionally related (poor sleep worsens depression; depression worsens sleep). Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I) demonstrates large effects on both sleep and depression, with benefits sustained long-term. Sleep hygiene, light therapy, and circadian rhythm interventions also show promise.

## Major Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses

### Gee et al. (2019)

**Title**: The efficacy of sleep hygiene in the treatment of insomnia: A meta-analysis

**Journal**: *Sleep Medicine Reviews*, 44, 93-106

**Study Design**: Meta-analysis of sleep interventions

**Sample**:
- 17 studies
- N = 1,586 participants
- Focus on non-pharmacological sleep interventions

**Key Findings**:
- **CBT-I**: Large effect (SMD = 0.92 for sleep quality)
- **Sleep hygiene alone**: Small to moderate effect (SMD = 0.39)
- **Multicomponent better** than single-component interventions
- **Effects sustained** at follow-up (3-12 months)

**Conclusion**: CBT-I is highly effective; sleep hygiene useful as component

### Cunningham & Shapiro (2018)

**Title**: Cognitive Behavioural Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I) to treat depression: A systematic review

**Journal**: *Journal of Psychosomatic Research*, 106, 1-12

**Study Design**: Systematic review

**Sample**:
- 17 studies
- N = 1,465 participants with depression and insomnia
- RCTs of CBT-I

**Key Findings**:
- **Depression improvement**: Medium to large effect (d = 0.47-0.87)
- **Sleep improvement**: Large effect (d = 0.92)
- **Dual benefits**: Improves both sleep and mood
- **Mechanism**: Sleep improvement mediates depression improvement
- **Comparable to**: Antidepressants for comorbid insomnia-depression

**Conclusion**: CBT-I should be first-line for depression with insomnia

### Mitchell et al. (2019)

**Title**: Comparative effectiveness of cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia: A systematic review

**Journal**: *BMC Family Practice*, 20, 65

**Study Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis

**Sample**:
- 37 RCTs
- Various populations and formats

**Key Findings**:
- **CBT-I vs. control**: Large effect (SMD = 1.07)
- **Brief CBT-I (4 sessions)**: Effective (SMD = 0.87)
- **Digital CBT-I**: Effective (SMD = 0.68)
- **Group format**: Comparable to individual
- **Primary care delivery**: Feasible and effective

**Conclusion**: CBT-I effective in various formats, including brief and digital

### Gebara et al. (2018)

**Title**: Effect of insomnia treatments on depression: A systematic review and meta-analysis

**Journal**: *Depression and Anxiety*, 35(4), 717-731

**Study Design**: Meta-analysis

**Sample**:
- 15 RCTs
- N = 1,013 participants
- CBT-I for people with depression + insomnia

**Key Findings**:
- **Depression reduction**: Moderate effect (d = 0.39)
- **Insomnia reduction**: Large effect (d = 1.03)
- **Sleep → mood**: Improvement in sleep predicts improvement in depression
- **Not just sleep**: CBT-I has direct effects on depression beyond sleep improvement

**Conclusion**: Treating insomnia improves depression outcomes

## Sleep and Depression: Bidirectional Relationship

### Prevalence

**Insomnia in depression**:
- 80-90% of people with depression have sleep problems
- Most common: Difficulty falling asleep, early morning awakening, non-restorative sleep
- Severity correlates with depression severity

**Depression in insomnia**:
- 40% of people with chronic insomnia have depression
- Insomnia increases depression risk 2-4x
- Untreated insomnia predicts depression onset

### Mechanisms: Sleep → Depression

**1. Emotion dysregulation**
- Poor sleep impairs prefrontal cortex function
- Increases amygdala reactivity
- Reduces ability to regulate emotions

**2. Cognitive impairment**
- Concentration and memory affected
- Increases negative thinking
- Impairs problem-solving

**3. Neurobiological changes**
- Disrupts neurotransmitter systems (serotonin, dopamine)
- Increases inflammation (IL-6, TNF-α, CRP)
- Reduces BDNF (neuroplasticity)
- HPA axis dysregulation (elevated cortisol)

**4. Behavioral effects**
- Reduced activity and social engagement
- Increased isolation
- Negative cycle: fatigue → avoidance → low mood

### Mechanisms: Depression → Sleep

**1. Rumination**
- Worry and negative thinking interfere with sleep onset
- Early morning awakening with rumination

**2. Neurobiological changes**
- Altered circadian rhythms
- REM sleep abnormalities (reduced latency, increased REM)
- Sleep architecture disruption

**3. Behavioral factors**
- Daytime napping (disrupts nighttime sleep)
- Irregular sleep schedule
- Reduced activity (less sleep drive)

## Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I)

### Components

**1. Sleep Restriction (Paradoxical)**
- **Rationale**: Consolidate sleep by limiting time in bed
- **Method**:
  - Calculate average sleep time (from sleep diary)
  - Set time in bed = average sleep time (minimum 5 hours)
  - No napping
  - Gradually increase as sleep efficiency improves (≥85%)
- **Mechanism**: Increases sleep drive, consolidates sleep

**2. Stimulus Control**
- **Rationale**: Strengthen bed-sleep association
- **Instructions**:
  - Go to bed only when sleepy
  - Use bed only for sleep (and sex)
  - If not asleep in 15-20 minutes, get up
  - Return to bed when sleepy
  - Same wake time daily (regardless of sleep)
- **Mechanism**: Classical conditioning

**3. Sleep Hygiene Education**
- **Rationale**: Optimize environment and behaviors for sleep
- **Key elements**:
  - Consistent sleep schedule
  - Comfortable sleep environment (cool, dark, quiet)
  - Avoid caffeine (after 2pm), alcohol (within 3 hours of bed)
  - Regular exercise (not within 2 hours of bed)
  - Light exposure: Morning light, dim evening
  - Wind-down routine (30-60 minutes)

**4. Cognitive Therapy**
- **Rationale**: Address unhelpful beliefs about sleep
- **Common beliefs**:
  - "I need 8 hours or I'll be useless" (unrealistic expectations)
  - "Insomnia is ruining my health" (catastrophizing)
  - "I must sleep now" (performance anxiety)
- **Intervention**: Identify and challenge beliefs, realistic expectations

**5. Relaxation Training**
- **Rationale**: Reduce physiological and cognitive arousal
- **Techniques**:
  - Progressive muscle relaxation
  - Diaphragmatic breathing
  - Guided imagery
  - Mindfulness meditation
- **Application**: Pre-sleep routine, when can't sleep

### Format and Delivery

**Traditional CBT-I**:
- **Duration**: 4-8 sessions
- **Frequency**: Weekly
- **Format**: Individual or group
- **Setting**: Clinic, primary care, digital

**Brief CBT-I** (4 sessions):
- Session 1: Assessment, psychoeducation, sleep diary
- Session 2: Sleep restriction and stimulus control
- Session 3: Sleep hygiene and cognitive therapy
- Session 4: Relapse prevention

**Digital CBT-I**:
- **Programs**: Sleepio (NHS-approved), SHUTi, CBT-I Coach
- **Evidence**: Effective (SMD = 0.68)
- **Advantages**: Accessible, scalable, cost-effective
- **Limitations**: Lower completion rates, less personalized

### Evidence for CBT-I

**Insomnia outcomes**:
- **Remission**: 40-60% achieve insomnia remission
- **Sleep efficiency**: Increases from ~70% to ~85%+
- **Sleep quality**: Large improvements
- **Sustained**: Benefits maintained at 1-2 years

**Depression outcomes**:
- **Comorbid depression**: Moderate to large effects (d = 0.47-0.87)
- **Prevents depression**: Reduces new onset in people with insomnia
- **Comparable to medication**: Similar or better outcomes

**Other benefits**:
- Reduced anxiety
- Improved daytime functioning
- Reduced pain (in chronic pain)
- Improved quality of life

## Other Sleep Interventions

### Sleep Hygiene Education

**Components**:
- Consistent sleep-wake schedule
- Bedroom environment optimization
- Caffeine and alcohol guidelines
- Exercise timing
- Light exposure optimization
- Pre-sleep routine

**Evidence**:
- **Alone**: Small to moderate effect (SMD = 0.39)
- **As component**: Enhances CBT-I
- **Accessible**: Can be delivered in single GP visit
- **Foundation**: Good starting point

**Limitations**:
- Less effective than CBT-I
- Many people already know but don't apply
- May need more intensive intervention

### Light Therapy

**Rationale**: Regulates circadian rhythm, increases alertness

**Protocol**:
- **Timing**: Morning (within 1 hour of waking)
- **Intensity**: 10,000 lux
- **Duration**: 20-30 minutes
- **Device**: Light box (specific wavelength)

**Evidence**:
- **Depression**: Moderate to large effect (d = 0.53-0.84)
- **Particularly effective**: Seasonal depression, atypical depression
- **Sleep**: Improves circadian rhythm disorders
- **Combined**: Often combined with CBT-I

**Safety**:
- Generally safe
- Side effects rare (headache, eye strain)
- Contraindicated: Retinal conditions, photosensitizing medications

### Circadian Rhythm Interventions

**Sleep Phase Delay** (common in depression):
- **Chronotherapy**: Gradually shift sleep time
- **Morning light**: Advance circadian rhythm
- **Evening light restriction**: Reduce blue light

**Sleep Phase Advance** (early awakening):
- **Evening light**: Delay circadian rhythm
- **Delay sleep time**: Gradually later bedtime

### Exercise for Sleep

**Evidence**:
- **Improves sleep quality**: Moderate effect (d = 0.45)
- **Reduces sleep latency**: Falls asleep faster
- **Increases sleep duration**: Modest increase
- **Timing matters**: Morning or afternoon best (not within 2 hours of bed)

**Mechanism**:
- Increases sleep drive
- Regulates circadian rhythm
- Reduces anxiety and depression

### Mindfulness for Sleep

**Evidence**:
- **Improves sleep quality**: Moderate effect (d = 0.55)
- **Reduces sleep latency**: Quiets rumination
- **Particularly helpful**: For rumination-related insomnia

**Application**:
- Body scan at bedtime
- Mindful breathing when can't sleep
- Acceptance of wakefulness (reduces performance anxiety)

## Pharmacological Considerations

### Risks of Sleep Medications

**Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs** (zolpidem, zopiclone):
- **Tolerance**: Effectiveness decreases over weeks
- **Dependence**: Physical and psychological
- **Withdrawal**: Rebound insomnia (worse than original)
- **Cognitive effects**: Memory impairment, falls (especially older adults)
- **Not curative**: Only treats symptoms

**Antidepressants for sleep** (mirtazapine, trazodone):
- **Sedating**: Can help sleep short-term
- **Tolerance**: Sedation often diminishes
- **Weight gain**: Significant with mirtazapine
- **Hangover effect**: Morning grogginess

### CBT-I vs. Medication

**Acute phase** (first 2-4 weeks):
- **Medication**: Faster onset
- **CBT-I**: Requires 2-4 weeks

**Medium-term** (2-6 months):
- **Comparable efficacy**

**Long-term** (6+ months):
- **CBT-I superior**: Benefits sustained or increase
- **Medication**: Tolerance, dependence concerns

**NICE guidelines (UK)**:
- CBT-I first-line
- Medication only for short-term acute insomnia (max 2-4 weeks)

### Combined Treatment

**CBT-I + Medication**:
- Can be combined short-term
- Goal: Taper medication as CBT-I takes effect
- **Evidence**: Combined not better than CBT-I alone long-term

## Implementation in Primary Care

### Screening

**All depression patients**:
- **Ask**: "How are you sleeping?"
- **Screen**:
  - Difficulty falling asleep?
  - Difficulty staying asleep?
  - Early morning awakening?
  - Non-restorative sleep?
  - Daytime impairment?

**Tools**:
- **Insomnia Severity Index (ISI)**: 7 items, quick
- **Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI)**: More detailed

### Brief Sleep Intervention (15-20 minutes)

**Can be delivered in GP consultation**:

**1. Assessment** (5 minutes)
- Sleep pattern (sleep diary helpful)
- Sleep hygiene practices
- Caffeine, alcohol, medications
- Daytime naps
- Bedroom environment

**2. Psychoeducation** (5 minutes)
- Sleep-depression connection (bidirectional)
- Sleep drive and circadian rhythm
- Importance of consistency

**3. Core behavioral advice** (5 minutes)
- **Consistent wake time**: Most important
- **Limit time in bed**: Match to actual sleep time
- **Get up if not sleeping**: Break bed-worry association
- **No daytime napping**

**4. Sleep hygiene** (5 minutes)
- Review and optimize
- Written handout

**5. Follow-up**
- 2-4 weeks
- Review sleep diary
- Adjust recommendations

### Referral to Sleep Service or CBT-I

**When to refer**:
- Moderate to severe chronic insomnia
- Failed brief intervention
- Patient preference for structured program
- Comorbid sleep disorder (sleep apnea, restless legs)

**What's available**:
- **NHS sleep services**: Some CCGs have
- **IAPT services**: Some offer CBT-I
- **Digital CBT-I**: Sleepio (free via NHS in some areas), SHUTi
- **Private**: Psychologists, sleep specialists

### Sleep Diary

**Essential tool**:
- Record for 1-2 weeks
- Daily tracking: Bedtime, sleep time, wake time, time out of bed
- Calculate sleep efficiency: (Total sleep time / Time in bed) x 100%
- Identify patterns

**Digital options**:
- Apps available (many free)
- Can share with clinician

## Special Populations

### Older Adults

**Sleep changes**:
- More fragmented sleep (normal with aging)
- Earlier circadian phase (sleepy earlier)
- Reduced deep sleep

**Considerations**:
- **Adjust expectations**: 5-7 hours may be adequate
- **Safety**: Falls risk with sleep medications, sleep deprivation
- **Medical conditions**: Pain, nocturia, medications affect sleep
- **CBT-I effective**: Same or better than younger adults

### Adolescents and Young Adults

**Sleep phase delay**:
- **Natural**: Circadian rhythm shifts later in adolescence
- **Social jet lag**: School/work schedules conflict with biology

**Interventions**:
- **Morning light**: Advance rhythm
- **Evening light restriction**: Avoid screens 1 hour before bed
- **Consistent schedule**: Even on weekends (within 1 hour)
- **School start times**: Later better for adolescent health

### Shift Workers

**Challenges**:
- **Irregular schedules**: Disrupt circadian rhythm
- **Sleep difficulties**: Common
- **Depression risk**: Increased

**Strategies**:
- **Light management**: Bright light during shifts, dark during day sleep
- **Consistent schedule**: As much as possible
- **Strategic naps**: Short (20 min) or longer (90 min - full cycle)
- **Sleep environment**: Optimize for daytime sleep (dark, quiet)

### Pregnancy and Postpartum

**Sleep disruption**:
- **Common**: Especially 3rd trimester and postpartum
- **Depression risk**: Sleep loss increases postpartum depression risk

**Interventions**:
- **Sleep hygiene**: Especially important
- **Naps**: Can be helpful during pregnancy
- **Sleep restriction**: Not appropriate postpartum (safety)
- **Support**: Night-time infant care support

## Mechanisms: How Sleep Interventions Improve Depression

### Direct Pathways

**1. Neurobiological restoration**
- Sleep deprivation increases inflammation → depression
- Adequate sleep reduces inflammatory markers
- Sleep supports neuroplasticity (BDNF)

**2. Emotion regulation**
- Sleep deprivation → amygdala hyperreactivity
- Adequate sleep → prefrontal control restored
- Better emotional regulation during day

**3. Cognitive function**
- Sleep improves concentration, memory, problem-solving
- Better daytime function → mood improvement
- Reduces cognitive symptoms of depression

### Indirect Pathways

**1. Behavioral activation**
- Better sleep → more energy
- Increased activity and engagement
- Breaks depression-inactivity cycle

**2. Self-efficacy**
- Successfully improving sleep → sense of control
- Mastery experience → improves mood
- Generalization to other areas

**3. Reduced rumination**
- CBT-I reduces pre-sleep rumination
- Less time in bed awake (reduced rumination time)
- Sleep restriction → too tired to ruminate

## Economic Considerations

### Cost-Effectiveness

**CBT-I vs. medication** (Holbrook et al., 2019):
- **Cost per QALY**: £3,850 for CBT-I vs. £8,420 for medication
- **CBT-I more cost-effective**
- **No ongoing costs**: Unlike medication

**Group CBT-I**:
- 8-12 participants per therapist
- Cost per person: £150-300 for full course
- Highly cost-effective

**Digital CBT-I**:
- Very low cost per user (£30-50)
- Scalable to population level
- Cost-effective for mild-moderate insomnia

### Economic Impact of Untreated Insomnia

**Healthcare costs**:
- Increased GP visits, medication use
- Increased risk of accidents
- Increased sick leave
- Development of chronic conditions

**Treating insomnia**:
- Reduces these costs
- Prevents depression (major cost driver)
- Improves work productivity

## Safety Considerations

### Sleep Restriction Safety

**Cautions**:
- **Driving**: Increased risk when sleep-deprived
- **Occupational hazards**: Safety-sensitive jobs
- **Seizure disorders**: Sleep deprivation lowers threshold
- **Bipolar disorder**: Can trigger mania (use cautiously)

**Monitoring**:
- Start conservatively (minimum 5-6 hours in bed)
- Monitor daytime function
- Adjust if excessive sleepiness

### Digital Interventions

**Self-help**:
- Appropriate for mild-moderate insomnia
- Face-to-face better for severe or complex cases
- Medical screening important (rule out sleep apnea)

## Recommendations for EQUILIBRIUM

### Medical Education

**Students should learn**:
1. Sleep-depression bidirectional relationship
2. Assessment of sleep (history, sleep diary, ISI)
3. Sleep hygiene principles
4. Core CBT-I components (especially stimulus control, sleep restriction)
5. How to deliver brief sleep intervention (15-20 min)
6. When to refer for specialist CBT-I
7. Risks of sleep medications (especially long-term)

**Curriculum integration**:
- **Year 1-2**: Sleep physiology, sleep hygiene
- **Psychiatry**: Sleep-mood relationship, CBT-I
- **GP placement**: Brief sleep intervention in consultations
- **Clinical skills**: Motivational interviewing for behavior change

### GP Practice

**Standard approach for all depression patients**:
1. **Screen for sleep problems** (100% of depression presentations)
2. **Sleep diary** (1-2 weeks)
3. **Deliver brief sleep intervention** (15-20 min):
   - Consistent wake time
   - Limit time in bed to actual sleep time
   - Stimulus control basics
   - Sleep hygiene optimization
4. **Follow up** (2-4 weeks): Review sleep diary, adjust plan
5. **Refer if needed**: To CBT-I specialist or digital program

**Enhanced pathway**:
- Commission CBT-I services (group or individual)
- Digital CBT-I available (Sleepio, SHUTi)
- Training for practice nurses or HCAs to deliver brief CBT-I
- Integration with IAPT services

**Avoid**:
- Long-term sleep medications as first-line
- Treating depression without addressing sleep
- Assuming sleep will improve when depression improves (may need direct intervention)
