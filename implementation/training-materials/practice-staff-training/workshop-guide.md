# EQUILIBRIUM Practice Staff Training Workshop

**Half-day training for reception, administrative staff, and nurses**

---

## Workshop Overview

This half-day workshop equips practice staff (reception, administrators, nurses) with the knowledge and skills to support EQUILIBRIUM implementation. While GPs deliver the clinical assessment and treatment planning, practice staff play a crucial role in:

1. Patient preparation (PHQ-9 administration before consultation)
2. Signposting to lifestyle resources
3. Appointment scheduling and follow-up coordination
4. Creating a supportive practice environment

**Format:** Interactive workshop with brief presentations, discussions, and practical exercises

**Duration:** Half day (3.5 hours, typically 9am-12:30pm or 1pm-4:30pm)

**Participants:** Reception staff, administrators, practice nurses, healthcare assistants

**Group size:** 8-15 optimal, 25 maximum

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this workshop, participants will be able to:

1. **Explain** the EQUILIBRIUM approach in simple terms
2. **Administer** PHQ-9 questionnaires correctly and score them
3. **Signpost** patients to lifestyle resources (exercise programs, nutrition support, sleep apps, social prescribing)
4. **Schedule** appropriate follow-up appointments based on EQUILIBRIUM protocols
5. **Respond** sensitively to patients with mental health concerns
6. **Identify** when to alert GP immediately (suicide risk, crisis)

---

### Session 2: Introduction to EQUILIBRIUM (0:10-0:40, 30 min)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand what EQUILIBRIUM is and why the practice is implementing it
- Recognize the evidence base for lifestyle interventions
- Appreciate the importance of holistic approaches to depression

**Key Content:**

**Slide 1-3: What is EQUILIBRIUM? (5 min)**

**EQUILIBRIUM stands for:**
*Evidence-based, Holistic approach to depression management*

**Core principles:**
1. **Depression has multiple causes:** Biological (sleep, physical health), psychological (thoughts, coping), and social (relationships, work stress, isolation)
2. **Lifestyle interventions work:** Exercise, nutrition, and sleep improvements are as effective as medication for mild-moderate depression
3. **Patient choice matters:** We offer options (lifestyle interventions, therapy, medication) and patients decide what fits their life

**Why our practice is implementing EQUILIBRIUM:**
- Better outcomes for patients (evidence-based)
- Aligns with NICE guidelines (medication should NOT be first-line for mild depression)
- Addresses root causes, not just symptoms
- Many patients prefer non-medication approaches

**Slide 4-7: Evidence in Brief (10 min)**

*(Facilitator: Keep this simple - staff don't need detailed research, just key takeaways)*

**Exercise:**
- As effective as antidepressants for mild-moderate depression
- Example: 30 minutes of brisk walking, 5 days/week
- Benefits seen within 2-4 weeks

**Nutrition (Mediterranean diet):**
- Vegetables, fruits, fish, whole grains
- Research shows 32% of patients achieved remission with dietary changes alone

**Sleep:**
- 80% of depressed patients have poor sleep
- Improving sleep improves depression
- Simple: Regular sleep schedule, reduce screen time before bed, online sleep programs (e.g., Sleepio)

**Social connection:**
- Isolation worsens depression
- Connecting patients to community activities (exercise groups, volunteering, hobbies) helps

**Key message for staff:** "These aren't just vague suggestions - they're evidence-based treatments"

**Slide 8-10: What EQUILIBRIUM Means in Practice (10 min)**

**How EQUILIBRIUM changes our practice:**

**Before EQUILIBRIUM:**
- Patient presents with low mood → GP often prescribes antidepressant
- May discuss "eat better, exercise" briefly but not systematically

**With EQUILIBRIUM:**
1. **Assessment:** GP explores biological, psychological, and social factors (what's contributing to depression?)
2. **Options:** GP offers lifestyle interventions, therapy, or medication - patient chooses
3. **Support:** Practice staff help patient access resources (you!)
4. **Monitoring:** Regular follow-up with depression questionnaire (PHQ-9) to track progress

**Your role:**
- Administer PHQ-9 before consultation (so GP has baseline data)
- Signpost patients to resources (exercise programs, nutrition apps, sleep apps)
- Schedule follow-up appointments (2 weeks, 4 weeks, 8 weeks)
- Create welcoming, supportive environment for patients with mental health concerns

**Q&A (5 min)**

**Common questions:**
- **Q: "Will we stop prescribing antidepressants?"**
  - A: No. EQUILIBRIUM offers alternatives first for mild depression, but medication is still appropriate for some patients (moderate-severe depression, patient preference, if lifestyle approaches insufficient).

- **Q: "Won't this mean longer appointments?"**
  - A: Initially yes (GPs may book 15-20 min for depression assessments). But systematic monitoring actually saves time in the long run (less "I'm still not better" appointments).

- **Q: "What if patients don't want to do lifestyle changes?"**
  - A: That's their choice. GP will discuss options and patient decides. Some will choose medication, and that's OK.

### Session 3: PHQ-9 Administration and Scoring (0:40-1:10, 30 min)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Correctly administer PHQ-9 questionnaire
- Score PHQ-9 and interpret severity levels
- Recognize when to alert GP immediately (suicide risk)

**Materials Needed:**
- Blank PHQ-9 forms (1 per participant)
- Completed PHQ-9 examples (for scoring practice)
- PHQ-9 scoring cards (laminated, 1 per participant to keep)
- Slides

**Key Content:**

**Slide 11-13: What is PHQ-9? (5 min)**

**PHQ-9 = Patient Health Questionnaire-9**
- Brief questionnaire (9 questions) that screens for depression
- Patients complete it themselves (not staff interview)
- Takes 2-3 minutes to complete
- Score tells GP how severe depression is

**9 questions ask about past 2 weeks:**
1. Little interest or pleasure in doing things?
2. Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless?
3. Trouble falling/staying asleep, or sleeping too much?
4. Feeling tired or having little energy?
5. Poor appetite or overeating?
6. Feeling bad about yourself?
7. Trouble concentrating?
8. Moving/speaking slowly or being fidgety/restless?
9. Thoughts of being better off dead or hurting yourself?

**Each question scored:**
- 0 = Not at all
- 1 = Several days
- 2 = More than half the days
- 3 = Nearly every day

**Total score: 0-27**

**Slide 14-16: How to Administer PHQ-9 (10 min)**

**Step-by-step:**

**1. When to give PHQ-9:**
- **New depression consultations:** Give to patient before they see GP
- **Depression follow-ups:** Give at every follow-up appointment (2 weeks, 4 weeks, etc.)
- **Timing:** Ideally when patient checks in at reception, or in waiting room

**2. What to say:**
> "The doctor has asked me to give you a short questionnaire about your mood over the past 2 weeks. It helps us understand how you're feeling. Please answer all 9 questions honestly - there are no right or wrong answers. It takes about 2-3 minutes. I'll collect it when you're done and score it."

**3. Provide:**
- PHQ-9 form
- Pen
- Clipboard (if filling out in waiting room)
- Private space if possible (or at least quiet corner)

**4. Patient completes form** (2-3 min)

**5. Collect form and score immediately** (30 seconds)
- Add up all 9 responses (each 0-3)
- Total = PHQ-9 score
- Write total at bottom of form
- Attach to patient notes for GP

**6. Check question 9** (IMPORTANT - SAFETY):
- **If patient answered >0 on question 9** (thoughts of self-harm/suicide):
  - **Alert GP immediately** (before patient goes in, discreetly)
  - GP will do safety assessment
  - DO NOT dismiss or ignore, even if score is low (e.g., "several days")

**Slide 17-19: Scoring Practice (15 min)**

**Facilitator distributes completed PHQ-9 examples**

**Example 1:** (Show on screen)
- Responses: 2, 2, 1, 3, 1, 2, 1, 0, 0
- **Calculate:** 2+2+1+3+1+2+1+0+0 = **12**
- **Interpretation:** Moderate depression
- **Question 9:** 0 (no suicidal thoughts - routine appointment)

**Example 2:**
- Responses: 3, 3, 2, 3, 2, 2, 1, 0, 1
- **Calculate:** 3+3+2+3+2+2+1+0+1 = **17**
- **Interpretation:** Moderately severe depression
- **Question 9:** 1 (answered "several days" to suicide question - **ALERT GP immediately**)

**Participants practice:**
- Distribute 2-3 completed PHQ-9 forms to each participant
- Participants score them (2 min)
- Facilitator reviews correct answers

**Severity interpretation (show on slide and laminated card):**
- **0-4:** Minimal/none
- **5-9:** Mild depression
- **10-14:** Moderate depression
- **15-19:** Moderately severe depression
- **20-27:** Severe depression

**Key points:**
- ✅ **Do:** Score accurately, check question 9, alert GP if >0 on question 9
- ❌ **Don't:** Interpret score for patient ("You're moderately depressed"), share score with patient (GP will discuss), judge patient based on score

### Session 4: Patient Resources and Signposting (1:25-2:10, 45 min)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Identify local and national resources for exercise, nutrition, sleep, social connection
- Signpost patients confidently to appropriate resources
- Provide printed materials and online resources

**Materials Needed:**

- Local resource directory (compiled by practice manager before workshop)
- Laptops/tablets (optional, for exploring online resources)

**Key Content:**

**Slide 20-22: What is Signposting? (5 min)**

**Signposting** = Directing patients to helpful resources

**Why it matters:**
- GP may recommend "exercise 5 times/week" or "improve your diet" - but patient may not know where to start
- Practice staff can provide specific, actionable resources
- Helps patients take first step

**When to signpost:**
- After depression consultation (GP may say "Please give Mr. Smith the exercise and sleep resources")
- When patient asks "How do I get started with exercise?" or "Where can I find a support group?"
- At follow-up appointments (reinforcement)

**Slide 23-28: Exercise Resources (10 min)**

**1. NHS Couch to 5K app** (Free)
- Beginner-friendly running program (9 weeks)
- Audio coaching

**2. NHS Active 10 app** (Free)
- Tracks brisk walking (10-minute chunks)
- Good for: Very sedentary patients, elderly, those with mobility limitations

**3. NHS Exercise Prescription**
- Some practices can refer to local leisure centers (subsidized/free gym access)
- Check if your practice has arrangement with local facilities

**4. Walking groups:**
- Ramblers UK (www.ramblers.org.uk) - local walking groups nationwide
- Walking for Health (www.walkingforhealth.org.uk) - organized walks

**Local resources (Practice-Specific):**
*(Facilitator: Provide list specific to your area - examples below)*

- [Local Swimming Pool]: Discounted swimming (show patient letter from GP)

**How to signpost:**
> "The doctor mentioned exercise would be helpful for your mood. Here are some resources to get started. The Couch to 5K app is popular - it's free and designed for complete beginners. Or if you prefer walking, this leaflet lists local walking groups that meet weekly. Would any of these interest you?"

**Slide 29-33: Nutrition Resources (10 min)**

**National resources:**

**1. NHS Eat Well Guide** (Free online)

**2. Mediterranean Diet Information**

**Slide 34-38: Sleep Resources (10 min)**

**National resources:**

**1. Sleepio** (Free on NHS in some areas)

- 6-week course, personalized
- Clinically proven to improve sleep and depression

**How to signpost:**

There's also an online program called Sleepio that's like a sleep course - many of our patients have found it really helpful."

### Session 5: Supporting Patients and Communication Skills (2:10-2:40, 30 min)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Communicate sensitively with patients experiencing mental health difficulties
- Recognize distress and respond appropriately
- Know when to escalate to GP or manager

**Key Content:**

**Slide 44-47: Creating a Supportive Environment (10 min)**

**Mental health stigma:**
- Many patients feel ashamed or embarrassed about depression
- Staff attitude matters enormously

**How to create supportive practice culture:**

**✅ DO:**
- **Normalize:** "Depression is really common - you're not alone. Many of our patients experience this."
- **Validate:** "It sounds like you're going through a difficult time"
- **Respect privacy:** Discuss mental health concerns discreetly (not at busy reception desk with other patients nearby)
- **Show empathy:** Warm tone, eye contact, patient approach
- **Be encouraging:** "It's great that you're seeking help. The doctor will discuss options with you."

**❌ DON'T:**
- **Minimize:** "Just cheer up" or "Everyone gets sad sometimes"
- **Judge:** "You don't look depressed" or facial expressions showing surprise/judgment
- **Gossip:** Never discuss patient's mental health with colleagues outside of professional context
- **Rush:** If patient is tearful or distressed, allow moment to compose themselves

**Slide 48-51: Responding to Distress (10 min)**

**Scenarios and responses:**

**Scenario 1: Patient becomes tearful while filling out PHQ-9**

**Poor response:** (Avoid this)
> "Don't cry, you're OK. Just finish the form."

**Good response:**
> "Take your time. I can see this is difficult. Would you like a tissue? Would you like a few minutes, or would you prefer to complete the form with the doctor?"

---

**Scenario 2: Patient says "I don't see the point anymore" while checking in**

**Response:**
> "I'm sorry you're feeling that way. It's really important we talk about this today. I'm going to let the doctor know you're here and that it's urgent. Please take a seat - we'll get you in as soon as possible."

*(Then: Alert GP or manager immediately - patient may be expressing suicidal thoughts)*

---

**Scenario 3: Patient angry: "I've been on antidepressants for 2 years and I'm still not better. Nobody's helping me."**

**Poor response:**
> "Well, you need to give the medication time" or "That's not our fault"

**Good response:**
> "I'm really sorry to hear you're still struggling. That must be frustrating. The doctor will want to review your treatment plan today and discuss other options that might help. Let's make sure you get a proper appointment time to go through this thoroughly."

---

**Scenario 4: Patient embarrassed: "I feel silly coming in for this. Other people have real problems."**

**Response:**
> "Depression is a real medical condition, just like diabetes or high blood pressure. You're absolutely right to seek help. The doctor will discuss ways we can support you."

**Slide 52-54: When to Escalate (10 min)**

**Alert GP/manager immediately if:**
- Patient mentions suicide, self-harm, or "I'd be better off dead"
- Patient scores >0 on PHQ-9 question 9 (suicide item)
- Patient appears very distressed, agitated, or unable to function
- Patient expresses intent to harm someone else
- Safeguarding concern (child at risk, domestic violence, vulnerable adult)

**How to escalate:**
- Speak to GP directly (in person, discreetly) before patient's appointment
- If GP unavailable: Tell practice manager or duty doctor
- Document briefly in notes: "Patient expressed [concern], GP alerted"

**If patient in crisis and GP not available:**
- Stay with patient (don't leave them alone)
- Contact duty doctor, 999 (if immediate danger), or crisis team (if your practice has one)

### Session 6: Practice Workflows and Appointment Scheduling (2:40-3:10, 30 min)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand EQUILIBRIUM workflow from initial presentation to follow-up
- Schedule appropriate appointment types and intervals
- Coordinate between GP, nurse, and link worker

**Materials Needed:**
- Workflow diagram (printed or on slides)
- Appointment scheduling guide

**Key Content:**

**Slide 55-58: EQUILIBRIUM Workflow (15 min)**

**Patient journey:**

**1. Initial Presentation (Week 0)**
- Patient books appointment for "low mood" or depression
- **Reception:** Book standard GP appointment (10-15 min, or longer if practice policy)
- **Day of appointment:** Give PHQ-9 to complete before seeing GP (see Session 3)
- **GP:** Conducts biopsychosocial assessment, offers treatment options (lifestyle/therapy/medication), patient decides
- **After appointment:** GP may ask reception to:
  - Provide patient resource pack (exercise, nutrition, sleep handouts)
  - Book follow-up in 2 weeks
  - Refer to link worker (if available)

**2. Early Follow-Up (Week 2)**
- **Purpose:** Check patient has started treatment plan, address barriers, ensure safety
- **Reception:** Book 10-min GP appointment or nurse appointment (if nurse trained in EQUILIBRIUM)
- **Give PHQ-9** before appointment
- **GP/Nurse:** Briefly review progress, troubleshoot (e.g., "I couldn't start exercise because..." → problem-solve)

**3. Progress Review (Week 4, 8, 12)**
- **Purpose:** Monitor PHQ-9 (is patient responding?), adjust treatment if needed
- **Reception:** Book 10-15 min GP appointment
- **Give PHQ-9** before each appointment
- **GP:** Compare PHQ-9 to baseline
  - If improving: Continue plan, encourage patient
  - If not improving enough: Discuss intensifying treatment (add medication, refer to therapy)

**4. Maintenance (if remission achieved)**
- **Follow-up every 3-6 months** (brief check-in, PHQ-9)
- Can often be nurse-led if patient stable

**Workflow diagram (show on slide):**

```
Initial Presentation → PHQ-9 → GP Assessment → Treatment Plan
    ↓
Week 2 Follow-Up → PHQ-9 → Quick check-in (GP or Nurse)
    ↓
Week 4 Follow-Up → PHQ-9 → Progress review (GP)
    ↓
Week 8 Follow-Up → PHQ-9 → Decision (continue or intensify)
    ↓
Week 12 Outcome → PHQ-9 → Remission? → Maintenance plan
```

**Slide 59-62: Appointment Scheduling Guide (15 min)**

**Quick reference for reception:**

| Appointment Type | Who | Duration | When | Notes |
|------------------|-----|----------|------|-------|
| **Initial depression assessment** | GP | 15-20 min | Patient presents with low mood | Book longer slot if possible; give PHQ-9 before |
| **Week 2 follow-up** | GP or Nurse | 10 min | 2 weeks after initial | Give PHQ-9; safety and engagement check |
| **Week 4, 8, 12 follow-ups** | GP | 10-15 min | Ongoing monitoring | Give PHQ-9; compare to baseline |
| **Link worker** | Link worker | 30-45 min | After GP refers | Social prescribing, community connections |
| **Medication review** (if prescribed) | GP | 10 min | 2-4 weeks after starting medication | Side effects, tolerability |

**Tips for scheduling:**
- **Use appointment notes:** Add "Depression F/U, give PHQ-9" so staff know to administer PHQ-9
- **Continuity:** Try to book with same GP for all follow-ups (patient preference, GP knows case)
- **Buffer time:** Don't overbook - depression appointments sometimes run long
- **Flexibility:** If patient struggling to attend frequent appointments (work, childcare), can do some follow-ups by phone

**Practice exercise (10 min):**

**Scenario 1:**
> Patient comes to desk: "I saw Dr. Smith 2 weeks ago for depression. She told me to book a follow-up today."

**Task:** What do you do?
- **Answer:** Book 10-min appointment with Dr. Smith (continuity). Note in appointment: "Depression F/U, give PHQ-9". When patient arrives for appointment, give them PHQ-9 to complete.

**Scenario 2:**
> GP calls to reception: "I've just seen Mrs. Jones for depression. Can you give her the EQUILIBRIUM resource pack and book follow-up in 2 weeks?"

**Task:** What do you do?
- **Answer:** (1) Give Mrs. Jones the patient resource folder (exercise, nutrition, sleep handouts) and explain: "Here are some resources the doctor wanted you to have. They've got information on exercise programs and sleep tips." (2) Book 10-min follow-up appointment in 2 weeks, note "Depression F/U, give PHQ-9".

### Session 7: Q&A, Troubleshooting, and Wrap-Up (3:10-3:30, 20 min)

**Facilitator:** "You've learned a lot today! Let's address any remaining questions and troubleshoot common challenges."

**Open Q&A (15 min)**

**Common questions:**

**Q: "What if patient refuses to complete PHQ-9?"**
- A: That's OK - it's voluntary. Explain: "It's just to help the doctor understand how you're feeling, but it's up to you." Let GP know patient declined. GP can still assess verbally.

**Q: "What if patient asks me to interpret their PHQ-9 score?"**
- A: Redirect to GP: "The doctor will go through the results with you and explain what they mean." (Don't interpret yourself - that's GP's role.)

**Q: "What if I'm not confident signposting patients?"**
- A: Start simple - you don't need to memorize everything. Keep patient resource folder at reception desk (refer to it). Say: "Let me grab some information for you" (look it up together). Confidence will grow with practice.

**Q: "What if patient is crying and I don't know what to do?"**
- A: It's OK to feel uncertain. Simple responses work: Offer tissue, validate ("I can see you're upset, that's completely understandable"), give them time, offer to speak somewhere more private. You don't need to "fix" it - empathy is enough.

**Q: "What do we do if GP isn't available and patient in crisis?"**
- A: Don't leave patient alone. Contact duty doctor or practice manager. If immediate danger (suicidal intent, psychosis), call 999 or crisis team. You won't be expected to handle this alone.

**Facilitator:**

- ✅ How to communicate sensitively with patients experiencing mental health difficulties
- ✅ EQUILIBRIUM workflows and appointment scheduling

**Your role is vital.** GPs can prescribe lifestyle interventions, but YOU connect patients to practical resources and create a welcoming environment. Together, we're improving outcomes for patients with depression.

## Troubleshooting Common Challenges

**Challenge 1: Staff forget to give PHQ-9**
- **Solution:**
  - Use appointment flags/alerts in EHR ("Depression F/U - give PHQ-9")
  - Keep PHQ-9 forms in visible location at reception (physical reminder)
  - Debrief at staff meetings (reinforce importance)

**Challenge 2: Patients confused by PHQ-9 questions**
- **Solution:**
  - Staff can clarify questions (e.g., "This question asks about sleep - trouble falling asleep, staying asleep, or sleeping too much")
  - Offer to read questions aloud if patient has literacy difficulties
  - If patient unable to complete (cognitive impairment, language barrier), inform GP (GP can assess verbally)

**Challenge 3: Staff feel overwhelmed by mental health conversations**
- **Solution:**
  - Normalize discomfort ("It's normal to feel unsure at first")
  - Emphasize: You're not counseling, just signposting and showing empathy
  - Provide examples/scripts for common scenarios (see Session 5)
  - Ensure staff know how to escalate (GP, manager, crisis team)

**Challenge 4: Patient resource packs running out**
- **Solution:**
  - Designate staff member to restock monthly (Practice Manager or admin lead)
  - Resources available digitally (practice can email PDFs if handouts run out)

## Adaptation for Different Contexts

### Virtual Workshop (Online Delivery)
- **Platform:** Zoom or MS Teams
- **Duration:** Shorten to 2.5-3 hours (screen fatigue)
- **Adjustments:**
  - Mail participant handbooks and PHQ-9 forms in advance
  - Use breakout rooms for practice activities
  - Interactive polls for engagement (e.g., "Have you ever administered a mental health questionnaire?")
- **Pros:** Accessible, no travel
- **Cons:** Less hands-on practice, harder to build team cohesion

### Abbreviated Workshop (2 Hours)
- **For practices with extreme time constraints:**
  - Focus on essentials: PHQ-9 administration (30 min), signposting (30 min), workflows (20 min), Q&A (20 min)
  - Skip: Detailed EQUILIBRIUM evidence, extensive communication skills practice
  - **Trade-off:** Less depth, may need follow-up training

### Large Group (>20 Staff)
- **Adjustments:**
  - Use auditorium/large room
  - Microphone for facilitator
  - Breakout small groups for practice activities (PHQ-9 scoring, scenario discussions)
  - Multiple facilitators (1 per 10-15 participants) to support small group work
