# Case Study: University of Edinburgh Medical School
## Modular Integration of Project EQUILIBRIUM

**Implementation Model:** Modular pilot ’ Selective expansion
**Timeline:** January 2021 - August 2022 (18 months)
**Student Cohort Size:** 250 students per year
**Setting:** Established medical school with traditional curriculum structure

---

## Executive Summary

University of Edinburgh Medical School implemented Project EQUILIBRIUM using a **modular pilot approach**, integrating holistic mental health content into their existing Year 3 Psychiatry module. After demonstrating success, they selectively expanded to Year 2 (lifestyle medicine) and Year 4 (advanced practice).

This case study demonstrates how EQUILIBRIUM can be implemented **without full curriculum overhaul** - ideal for schools with moderate readiness, curriculum constraints, or preference for incremental change.

**Key Outcomes (August 2022):**
- **Year 3 students:** **82% pass rate** on biopsychosocial assessment OSCE (vs 61% baseline)
- **Knowledge of NICE guidelines:** **71%** correctly identified first-line treatment for mild depression (vs 19% baseline)
- **Student satisfaction:** **9.1/10** for psychiatry module (vs 7.2/10 pre-EQUILIBRIUM)
- **Successful expansion:** Based on pilot success, expanded to Year 2 and Year 4 modules
- **Faculty engagement:** **100%** of psychiatry and GP faculty requested ongoing EQUILIBRIUM training

---

## Context and Motivation

### Why Edinburgh Chose EQUILIBRIUM

**The Catalyst (2020):**

A **student petition** in December 2020 highlighted gaps in mental health teaching:
- Students felt teaching was "too focused on diagnosis and medication"
- 120 students (48% of Year 3) signed petition requesting "more training in therapy and lifestyle interventions"
- Student Medical Society organized panel discussion featuring GP who practiced holistic mental health care

**Curriculum Committee Response (January 2021):**

Dr. James MacLeod, Psychiatry Module Lead, was tasked with reviewing and improving mental health teaching. After researching evidence-based approaches, he discovered Project EQUILIBRIUM.

**Decision:**
- Pilot EQUILIBRIUM within **Year 3 Psychiatry module** (6 weeks, existing teaching block)
- If successful after 6 months ’ consider expansion
- **Rationale:** Modular approach allows testing without major curriculum restructuring

---

## Why Modular Approach?

Edinburgh chose modular pilot rather than full integration for several reasons:

1. **Curriculum constraints:** 5-year curriculum with limited flexibility for major overhaul
2. **Governance:** Curriculum changes require extensive committee approval (slow process)
3. **Moderate readiness:** Readiness score 132/200 - good faculty capacity but resource constraints
4. **Risk mitigation:** Pilot allows "proof of concept" before committing resources to full integration
5. **Student demand:** Immediate response to student petition (pilot feasible within 6 months)

## Implementation Journey

### Phase 1: Planning and Preparation (January - May 2021)

**Readiness Score:** 132/200 (Moderate Readiness)

**Strengths:**
- Supportive psychiatry and GP faculty
- Student enthusiasm (petition demonstrated demand)
- Good assessment infrastructure

**Gaps:**
- Limited resources (no dedicated educational innovation funding)
- Curriculum already overcrowded
- No existing lifestyle medicine teaching
- Traditional curriculum structure (less flexible than problem-based learning)

**Planning Process:**

1. **Approval** (January 2021):
   - Dr. MacLeod presented pilot proposal to Curriculum Committee
   - Approved as "quality improvement" (no major curriculum change approval needed)
   - Budget: £5,000 (departmental discretionary fund)

2. **Working Group** (February 2021):
   - Small group: Dr. MacLeod (psychiatrist), Dr. Fiona Murray (GP), Dr. Robert Chen (public health), 2 Year 3 student reps
   - Goal: Integrate 12-15 hours of EQUILIBRIUM content into 6-week psychiatry module

3. **Content Selection** (March 2021):
   - Reviewed full EQUILIBRIUM curriculum, selected content relevant to Year 3
   - Focus: Depression and anxiety management (most common presentations in primary care)
   - Content:
     - Biopsychosocial assessment (4 hours)
     - Evidence base for lifestyle interventions (3 hours)
     - Shared decision-making (3 hours)
     - Treatment planning integrating holistic approaches (4 hours)
   - **Total: 14 hours** within existing 60-hour psychiatry module

4. **What Content Was Reduced:**
   - Reduced psychopharmacology detail (8 hours ’ 4 hours)
   - Removed one lecture on history of psychiatry (2 hours)
   - **Total removed: 6 hours**
   - **Net addition: 8 hours** (accommodated by extending some teaching days)

5. **Faculty Development** (April-May):
   - 3 faculty attended 2-day EQUILIBRIUM training in Manchester
   - Shared learning with 6 other Edinburgh faculty via 1-day local workshop
   - **Total: 9 faculty trained**

---

### Phase 2: Pilot Implementation (June - August 2021)

**Pilot Cohort:** Year 3 students (N=248)

**Module Structure:**

| Week | Topic | EQUILIBRIUM Content | Hours |
|------|-------|-------------------|-------|
| 1 | Depression fundamentals | NICE guidelines, biopsychosocial model | 2 |
| 2 | Assessment skills | **Biopsychosocial assessment practical** | 4 |
| 3 | Evidence base | **Lifestyle interventions as treatment** | 3 |
| 4 | Treatment approaches | **Shared decision-making, treatment planning** | 3 |
| 5 | Anxiety & insomnia | **Holistic management of anxiety, CBT-I** | 2 |
| 6 | Integration week | Case-based learning, OSCE practice | - |

**Teaching Methods:**
- Lectures: 6 hours (evidence base, NICE guidelines)
- Small group workshops: 8 hours (skills practice)
- Case-based learning: Weekly throughout module

**EQUILIBRIUM-Specific Innovations:**

1. **Biopsychosocial Assessment Workshop (Week 2):**
   - 4-hour workshop in groups of 8
   - Students practiced comprehensive assessment (sleep, exercise, nutrition, psychosocial factors)
   - Used standardized patients
   - Feedback from GP faculty

2. **Shared Decision-Making Role-Play (Week 4):**
   - Students practiced discussing treatment options (therapy vs medication vs lifestyle vs combination)
   - Peer feedback
   - Video recording for self-reflection

3. **Case-Based Integration (Throughout):**
   - Every week: Real GP cases with holistic management plans
   - Students developed treatment plans integrating lifestyle interventions

### Phase 4: Decision to Expand (September 2021)

**Results Presentation to Curriculum Committee (September 2021):**

Dr. MacLeod presented pilot outcomes. Key data:
- Student satisfaction: 9.1/10 (highest-rated module in Year 3)
- OSCE pass rates improved
- Student petition signatories surveyed: 92% satisfied with changes
- Faculty enthusiasm

**Committee Decision:**
- **Approved expansion** to Year 2 (lifestyle medicine focus) and Year 4 (advanced practice)
- Allocated £8,000 for expansion
- Timeline: Implement Year 2 content January 2022, Year 4 content September 2022

---

### Phase 5: Expansion (January - August 2022)

**Year 2 Expansion (January 2022):**

Integrated into **"Health and Society" module** (4-week module in Year 2):
- Focus: Lifestyle medicine fundamentals
- Content:
  - Exercise prescription for mental and physical health (4 hours)
  - Mediterranean diet and mental health (2 hours)
  - Sleep medicine (CBT-I basics) (2 hours)
  - Motivational interviewing for behavior change (4 hours)
- **Total: 12 hours**
- Replaced: 8 hours of epidemiology lectures (deemed lower priority)

**Outcomes:**
- Student satisfaction: 8.6/10
- Students appreciated early introduction to lifestyle medicine
- 81% felt confident prescribing exercise after module

**Year 4 Expansion (September 2022):**

Integrated into **"Preparation for Practice"** module:
- Focus: Advanced skills for F1
- Content:
  - Deprescribing antidepressants safely (3 hours)
  - Managing complex cases (comorbidities, treatment resistance) (3 hours)
  - Relapse prevention strategies (2 hours)
- **Total: 8 hours**

**Outcomes:**
- Student feedback: "Feel much better prepared for F1 mental health consultations"
- 87% felt confident discussing stopping antidepressants with patients

## Advantages of Modular Approach

### What Edinburgh Learned About Modular Implementation

**Advantages:**

1. **Faster approval:** Quality improvement to existing module, not major curriculum change
2. **Lower resource requirements:** £5,000 pilot (vs £40,000+ for full integration)
3. **Proof of concept:** Demonstrated success before committing to larger change
4. **Manageable scope:** 9 faculty trained (vs 30+ for full integration)
5. **Student-responsive:** Direct response to petition within 6 months
6. **Risk mitigation:** If pilot failed, limited impact on overall curriculum
7. **Faculty buy-in:** Success recruited skeptical faculty organically

**Disadvantages:**

1. **Less integrated:** Students receive concentrated doses rather than vertical integration
2. **Inconsistent placement experience:** Year 4 students in placements didn't receive Year 1-2 foundation
3. **Partial reach:** 2019-2020 cohorts graduated without EQUILIBRIUM content
4. **Faculty continuity:** Relies on module leads maintaining EQUILIBRIUM integration

---

## Key Success Factors

### What Made Edinburgh's Modular Pilot Successful?

1. **Student demand** - Petition provided mandate and urgency

2. **Supportive module lead** - Dr. MacLeod championed within his scope of control

3. **Targeted content selection** - Chose most relevant EQUILIBRIUM content for Year 3, didn't try to include everything

4. **Faculty development** - Even small pilot required faculty training

5. **Robust evaluation** - Collected comprehensive data to make case for expansion

6. **Starting where we were** - Worked within existing curriculum structure, didn't wait for perfect conditions

7. **Student involvement** - Petition signatories provided feedback, ensuring content matched demand

8. **Quick wins** - High student satisfaction in pilot built momentum for expansion

## Challenges and Lessons Learned

### Challenge 1: Limited Reach

**Issue:** Only Year 3 students in pilot cohort received EQUILIBRIUM content. Year 4-5 students (2019-2020 entry) graduated without it.

**Impact:** Some F1 doctors from Edinburgh still lack holistic mental health training.

**Learning:** Modular approach has trade-off - faster to implement, but slower to achieve full cohort coverage.

**Future Plan:** All students entering 2021 onwards receive EQUILIBRIUM in Years 2, 3, and 4.

---

### Challenge 2: Curriculum "Patching" vs Integration

**Issue:** EQUILIBRIUM content concentrated in 3 modules rather than vertically integrated.

**Impact:** Students receive intensive bursts rather than continuous thread across years.

**Student Feedback:** Some students noted "felt like a lot of new content in Year 3, would have been easier if introduced in Year 1."

**Learning:** Modular approach works but lacks elegance of full vertical integration.

**Potential Solution:** If future curriculum review occurs, advocate for full vertical integration.

---

### Challenge 3: Placement Inconsistency

**Issue:** Year 4 students in clinical placements observed variable practice - some supervisors modeled holistic approach, others didn't.

**Impact:** Mixed messages about "real world" practice.

**Resolution:**
- Used reflective practice: students reflected on gaps between what they learned and observed
- Selected 3 GP practices known for holistic approach for interested students
- Assessment via OSCE (standardized) rather than workplace-based

**Learning:** Cannot control all placements. Use reflection to learn from inconsistency.

---

### Challenge 4: Faculty Continuity

**Issue:** Modular integration depends on module lead maintaining EQUILIBRIUM content. What if Dr. MacLeod leaves or new module lead isn't supportive?

**Mitigation:**
- EQUILIBRIUM now in module learning objectives (formally approved by curriculum committee)
- OSCE stations integrated into standard Year 3 assessments (ensures content taught)
- Multiple faculty trained (not dependent on single person)
- Created teaching resources repository

**Learning:** Embed in formal curriculum documentation and assessment to ensure sustainability.

### Edinburgh (Modular) vs Manchester (Full Integration)

## Advice for Schools Considering Modular Approach

### When is Modular Approach Right for You?

**Consider modular approach if:**
-  Moderate readiness (120-159 readiness score)
-  Limited resources (time, funding)
-  Curriculum difficult to restructure (traditional, inflexible)
-  Governance processes slow (curriculum changes take years)
-  Want to demonstrate success before major commitment
-  Immediate demand (e.g., student petition, external pressure)

**Consider full integration if:**
-  High readiness (160-200 score)
-  Adequate resources available
-  Flexible curriculum structure
-  Leadership strong support for major change
-  Can wait 2-3 years for full implementation

**Both approaches are valid.** Edinburgh's modular approach achieved significant impact with modest resources.
