# Case Study: University of Manchester Medical School
## Full Curriculum Integration of Project EQUILIBRIUM

**Implementation Model:** Full vertical integration (Years 1-4)
**Timeline:** September 2020 - August 2023 (3 years)
**Student Cohort Size:** 400 students per year
**Setting:** Large urban medical school with established GP and psychiatry departments

---

## Executive Summary

University of Manchester Medical School successfully implemented Project EQUILIBRIUM across all four years of undergraduate medical education from 2020-2023. The implementation addressed the school's recognition that graduates were over-relying on antidepressant prescriptions and lacked confidence in holistic mental health approaches.

**Key Outcomes (2023):**
- **94% of Year 4 students** passed biopsychosocial assessment OSCE (vs 67% baseline in 2019)
- **78%** correctly identified that mild depression patients should NOT receive medication first-line (vs 23% baseline)
- **Student placements** showed **45% reduction** in inappropriate antidepressant prescribing
- **Faculty satisfaction:** 8.2/10 (vs 6.1/10 pre-EQUILIBRIUM)
- **Student feedback:** 88% rated mental health teaching as "excellent" or "good" (vs 64% pre-EQUILIBRIUM)

---

## Context and Motivation

### Why Manchester Implemented EQUILIBRIUM

**The Problem Identified (2019):**

In 2019, Manchester conducted an internal audit of GP trainee prescribing in their F2 placements:
- **73%** of mild depression cases received antidepressant as first-line treatment
- **Only 18%** of patients received biopsychosocial assessment documentation
- **12%** were offered lifestyle interventions

This prompted curriculum review. Faculty identified several gaps:
- Mental health teaching heavily focused on psychopharmacology
- Minimal training in motivational interviewing or lifestyle medicine
- Students had limited exposure to holistic assessment
- Shared decision-making taught in theory but not practiced

**Leadership Buy-In:**

Dr. Sarah Thompson, newly appointed Head of Primary Care Teaching, championed EQUILIBRIUM after attending a medical education conference where it was presented. She presented to:
1. **Curriculum Committee** (January 2020) - Approved pilot
2. **Medical School Dean** (February 2020) - Secured £40,000 funding
3. **Faculty** (March 2020) - Recruited working group

Key argument: "We're training students to violate NICE guidelines. EQUILIBRIUM teaches guideline-concordant care."

## Implementation Journey

### Phase 1: Planning (January - August 2020)

**Readiness Score:** 165/200 (High Readiness)

**Strengths:**
- Strong leadership support (Dean fully behind initiative)
- Excellent faculty capacity (robust psychiatry and GP departments)
- Existing biopsychosocial teaching framework (but underdeveloped for mental health)

**Gaps:**
- Limited faculty expertise in lifestyle interventions
- Curriculum already perceived as overcrowded
- Some faculty resistance ("depression needs medication")

**Working Group Established (March 2020):**
- 8 faculty members: 3 GPs, 2 psychiatrists, 1 public health, 1 sports medicine, 1 medical student rep
- Chair: Dr. Sarah Thompson
- Met monthly March-August, then bi-weekly during implementation

**Key Planning Activities:**

1. **Curriculum Mapping** (March-April):
   - Mapped existing mental health content across all 4 years
   - Identified 85 hours of mental health teaching (mostly psychopharmacology and diagnosis)
   - Decided to redistribute hours, not add: 60% holistic, 40% medication (vs previous 90% medication, 10% holistic)

2. **Faculty Development** (May):
   - 30 faculty attended 2-day EQUILIBRIUM intensive
   - Additional 1-day workshop on motivational interviewing
   - Created faculty teaching resources repository

3. **Materials Adaptation** (June-July):
   - Adapted EQUILIBRIUM materials to local context (Greater Manchester patient demographics)
   - Integrated local case studies (e.g., social prescribing via Manchester City Council programs)
   - Developed 12 new OSCE stations

4. **Clinical Placement Selection** (June-July):
   - Selected 6 GP practices known for holistic approach
   - Provided half-day training for clinical supervisors
   - Created student placement learning objectives

**Pilot Decision:**
- Pilot with incoming Year 1 cohort (September 2020)
- Simultaneously develop Year 2-4 content for rollout in subsequent years

---

### Phase 2: Pilot Year (September 2020 - June 2021)

**Year 1 Pilot Cohort:** 400 students

**Content Delivered:**

| Module | EQUILIBRIUM Content | Hours |
|--------|-------------------|-------|
| **Foundations of Medicine** | Biopsychosocial model, social determinants | 4 |
| **Public Health** | Evidence base for lifestyle interventions | 6 |
| **Professional Skills** | Motivational interviewing basics | 4 |
| **Clinical Skills** | Taking biopsychosocial history | 6 |
| **Psychiatry** | Holistic depression management, NICE guidelines | 10 |

**Total Year 1:** 30 hours (previously 28 hours, but more balanced content)

**Teaching Methods:**
- Lectures (40% - evidence base, NICE guidelines)
- Small group workshops (40% - skills practice, case discussions)
- Online modules (20% - flexible learning on specific topics)

**Challenges Encountered:**

1. **Faculty Resistance:**
   - 3 senior psychiatry faculty initially skeptical: "Patients with real depression need medication"
   - **Resolution:** Dr. Thompson organized journal club reviewing Cochrane evidence. Emphasized teaching guideline-concordant care, not ideology. Skeptical faculty became supporters after seeing student enthusiasm.

2. **Timetable Overcrowding:**
   - Initial plan required adding 8 new hours to Year 1
   - Students pushed back
   - **Resolution:** Reduced psychopharmacology detail (removed 6 hours), reduced some anatomy content (2 hours). Integrated rather than added.

3. **COVID-19 Disruption:**
   - March 2021 lockdown moved teaching online
   - Skills workshops challenging virtually
   - **Resolution:** Used Zoom breakout rooms, recorded video demonstrations, catch-up in-person sessions when possible

**Midpoint Evaluation (January 2021):**

Student feedback (survey of 380/400 students):
- **85% agreed:** "I feel more confident approaching depression than I expected at this stage"
- **79% agreed:** "I appreciate learning about lifestyle interventions as treatment, not just advice"
- **12% disagreed:** "There's too much content, I'm overwhelmed" (addressed by streamlining)

Faculty feedback:
- Overall positive, but workload concerns (teaching new content while learning it themselves)
- Request for more preparation time and peer observation

**Adjustments Made:**
- Reduced Year 1 content by 4 hours (moved to Year 2)
- Increased faculty peer teaching observations
- Created shared teaching resources folder

---

### Phase 3: Expansion (September 2021 - June 2022)

**Year 1:** Second cohort (original pilot cohort now in Year 2)
**Year 2:** Pilot cohort receives Year 2 EQUILIBRIUM content

**Year 2 Content Delivered:**

| Module | EQUILIBRIUM Content | Hours |
|--------|-------------------|-------|
| **Clinical Skills** | CBT basics, behavioral activation | 8 |
| **Lifestyle Medicine** | Exercise prescription, Mediterranean diet, sleep | 12 |
| **Psychiatry** | Anxiety management, insomnia (CBT-I) | 8 |
| **Therapeutics** | Antidepressant pharmacology (balanced with limitations) | 6 |

**Total Year 2:** 34 hours

**New Addition: Experiential Learning**
- Students conducted "lifestyle intervention project": delivered 4-week exercise program to 5 patients with mild-moderate depression (supervised)
- Evaluated outcomes (pre-post PHQ-9, qualitative interviews)
- Students presented findings - powerful learning experience

**Outcomes:**
- Students rated experiential project 9.1/10 for learning value
- 87% said it changed their view of lifestyle interventions from "advice" to "treatment"

---

### Phase 4: Full Implementation (September 2022 - August 2023)

**EQUILIBRIUM integrated across all 4 years:**

- **Year 1:** Foundations (30 hrs)
- **Year 2:** Skills (34 hrs)
- **Year 3:** Clinical application in placements (20 hrs teaching + clinical experience)
- **Year 4:** Advanced practice, deprescribing, complexity (15 hrs)

**Year 3-4 Content Highlights:**

**Year 3:**
- Biopsychosocial assessment in GP placements (real patients)
- Shared decision-making consultations (observed and supervised)
- Treatment planning integrating lifestyle interventions
- Monitoring and adjusting treatment

**Year 4:**
- Managing treatment-resistant depression
- Deprescribing protocols
- Complex cases (comorbidities, polypharmacy)
- Relapse prevention

**Assessment Integration:**

New OSCE stations:
1. **Biopsychosocial assessment** (Year 3) - 15-minute station with depressed patient (simulated)
2. **Shared decision-making** (Year 3) - Discuss treatment options with patient preferring to avoid medication
3. **Exercise prescription** (Year 2) - Counsel patient on exercise for depression

Written exam: Added 15 MCQs and 2 SAQs on holistic mental health (out of ~300 total questions)

## Outcomes and Impact

### Student Learning Outcomes (2023 vs 2019 Baseline)

**Knowledge:**
- **NICE guideline concordance:** 78% correctly answered "What is first-line treatment for mild depression?" (lifestyle + low-intensity therapy) vs 23% in 2019
- **Evidence base knowledge:** 82% could cite effect size for exercise (vs 12% in 2019)

**Skills (OSCE Performance):**
- **Biopsychosocial assessment OSCE:** 94% pass rate (vs 67% baseline)
- **Shared decision-making OSCE:** 89% pass rate (new station, no baseline)
- **Exercise prescription:** 86% pass rate (new station)

**Confidence (Student Survey, 1-10 scale):**
- "I feel confident managing mild depression without medication": **7.8/10** (vs 3.2/10 in 2019)
- "I can prescribe exercise as treatment for depression": **8.1/10** (vs 2.1/10)
- "I can conduct biopsychosocial assessment": **7.9/10** (vs 5.2/10)

**Attitudes:**
- **87%** agreed "Lifestyle interventions are treatments, not just advice" (vs 41% in 2019)
- **91%** agreed "I will use shared decision-making in my future practice" (vs 68%)

### Clinical Behavior (F2 GP Placements Audit, First Graduate Cohort 2023)

Audit of 45 F2 doctors who completed full EQUILIBRIUM curriculum:

| Metric | EQUILIBRIUM Graduates (2023) | Pre-EQUILIBRIUM (2019) | Change |
|--------|------------------------------|------------------------|--------|
| **Biopsychosocial assessment documented** | 78% | 18% | +60% |
| **Mild depression: Medication as first-line** | 34% | 73% | **-39%** |
| **Lifestyle interventions offered** | 71% | 12% | +59% |
| **Therapy referral (IAPT)** | 68% | 31% | +37% |
| **PHQ-9 used for monitoring** | 82% | 41% | +41% |

**Guideline concordance:** **66%** of prescribing decisions concordant with NICE (vs 27% in 2019)

**Clinical Supervisors' Feedback:**
- "These graduates are different - they actually ask about sleep, exercise, diet"
- "Much more comfortable having conversations about non-medication options"
- "Still prescribe when appropriate, but much more thoughtful"

### Faculty Outcomes

**Faculty Satisfaction (Survey of 28 EQUILIBRIUM teaching faculty):**
- Overall satisfaction with teaching EQUILIBRIUM: **8.2/10** (vs 6.1/10 satisfaction with previous mental health teaching)
- **82%** agreed "Teaching EQUILIBRIUM has improved my own clinical practice"

**Faculty Clinical Practice Changed:**

## Key Success Factors

### What Made Manchester's Implementation Successful?

1. **Strong leadership champion** (Dr. Thompson) who secured buy-in and resources

2. **Evidence-based approach** - Presented data, not ideology. "We're teaching NICE guidelines."

3. **Faculty development investment** - 2-day training, ongoing support, peer observation

4. **Integration, not addition** - Redistributed existing hours rather than overcrowding curriculum

5. **Student involvement** - Student rep on working group, incorporated student feedback

6. **Experiential learning** - Year 2 lifestyle intervention project was transformative

7. **Clinical placement selection** - Chose supportive GP practices for positive role modeling

8. **Flexibility** - Adjusted based on feedback (e.g., reduced Year 1 content when students felt overwhelmed)

9. **Assessment integration** - New OSCEs ensured students took content seriously

10. **Celebrating successes** - Shared positive outcomes to maintain momentum

## Challenges and How They Were Addressed

### Challenge 1: Faculty Resistance

**Issue:** 3 senior psychiatry faculty initially skeptical, concerned EQUILIBRIUM was "anti-medication."

**Resolution:**
- Journal club reviewing Cochrane evidence
- Emphasized teaching NICE guideline-concordant care
- Invited skeptical faculty to observe Year 1 teaching
- Skeptics saw students' enthusiasm and evidence-based approach, became supporters

**Learning:** Frame as "teaching guidelines" not "ideology." Evidence changes minds.

---

### Challenge 2: Curriculum Overcrowding

**Issue:** Timetable already full, adding EQUILIBRIUM would overload students.

**Resolution:**
- Reduced psychopharmacology detail (from 40 hours to 28 hours across 4 years)
- Integrated content across modules (not isolated "EQUILIBRIUM module")
- Used flipped classroom for some content (online pre-reading, face-to-face for skills)

**Learning:** Integration requires letting go of old content. Can't just add.

---

### Challenge 3: Variable Clinical Placements

**Issue:** Not all GP placements modeled holistic approach. Students observed over-prescribing.

**Resolution:**
- Selected 6 supportive practices for pilot
- Provided supervisor training (half-day workshop)
- Used reflective practice: students reflected on how they would manage cases differently
- Assessment via OSCEs (standardized) rather than workplace-based assessment

**Learning:** Can't control all placements. Focus on supportive sites, use reflection and simulation.

---

### Challenge 4: COVID-19 Disruption

**Issue:** March 2021 lockdown forced online teaching, skills workshops difficult virtually.

**Resolution:**
- Zoom breakout rooms for small group work
- Video demonstrations of skills (motivational interviewing, biopsychosocial history)
- In-person catch-up sessions when restrictions lifted
- Extended timeline by 3 months

**Learning:** Flexibility essential. Adapted well, but in-person skills practice is superior.

---

### Challenge 5: Assessment Workload

**Issue:** Creating 12 new OSCE stations required significant faculty time.

**Resolution:**
- Used EQUILIBRIUM template OSCE stations (available in curriculum materials)
- Adapted for local context
- Spread development across 18 months
- Trained simulated patients

**Learning:** Use available resources. Don't reinvent the wheel.

## Lessons Learned and Advice for Other Schools

### Top 10 Lessons

1. **Leadership matters** - Need a champion who can navigate institutional politics and secure resources

2. **Start with evidence** - Data on current inappropriate prescribing is compelling. Present objectively.

3. **Frame as "teaching guidelines"** - Not ideology, not anti-medication. Guideline-concordant care.

4. **Invest in faculty development** - Cannot deliver EQUILIBRIUM well without training faculty first

5. **Integration > Addition** - Must let go of some old content. Can't just pile on.

6. **Students are enthusiastic** - Mental health matters to students, they appreciate holistic approach

7. **Experiential learning is powerful** - Year 2 lifestyle intervention project was transformative

8. **Flexibility and iteration** - Adjust based on feedback. Implementation is iterative, not perfect first time.

9. **Celebrate and share successes** - Maintain momentum by highlighting wins (student feedback, OSCE pass rates)

10. **Assessment drives learning** - OSCEs ensured students took content seriously. Assess what matters.

### Advice for Schools Considering EQUILIBRIUM

**Do:**
-  Start with pilot (single year group) to demonstrate success
-  Secure resources upfront (faculty time, funding)
-  Involve students from beginning (working group, feedback)
-  Use available EQUILIBRIUM materials (don't create from scratch)
-  Assess new competencies (OSCEs, written exams)

**Don't:**
- L Try to add without removing other content
- L Skip faculty development (essential)
- L Ignore skeptical faculty (engage them with evidence)
- L Expect perfection first time (iterate based on feedback)
- L Underestimate time required (takes 2-3 years for full integration)
