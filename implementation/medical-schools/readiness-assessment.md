# EQUILIBRIUM Curriculum Readiness Assessment for Medical Schools

**Self-assessment tool to evaluate your institution's readiness to implement Project EQUILIBRIUM**

## Purpose

This assessment helps medical schools determine:
- Current readiness level for EQUILIBRIUM implementation
- Strengths to leverage
- Gaps to address before implementation
- Timeline and resources needed
- Implementation approach (full vs modular pilot)

**Instructions:** Rate each item on a scale of 1-5, then calculate your total score.

---

## Scoring Guide

**5 = Strongly agree / Excellent capacity**
**4 = Agree / Good capacity**
**3 = Neutral / Moderate capacity**
**2 = Disagree / Limited capacity**
**1 = Strongly disagree / No capacity**

## Interpreting Your Score

### 160-200 points: HIGH READINESS

**Interpretation:** Your institution is well-positioned to implement EQUILIBRIUM curriculum.

**Recommended approach:**
- **Full curriculum integration** (all years, vertical integration)
- Timeline: 2-3 years for complete implementation
- Start with pilot in one year group, then spread

**Next steps:**
1. Secure formal commitment from leadership
2. Establish curriculum development working group
3. Complete pilot application
4. Develop detailed implementation timeline
5. Begin faculty development program

**Strengths to leverage:** Identify your highest-scoring sections and use these as foundations for implementation.

---

### 120-159 points: MODERATE READINESS

**Interpretation:** Your institution has good capacity but some gaps need addressing.

**Recommended approach:**
- **Modular pilot** first (e.g., integrate into Year 2 module or psychiatry teaching)
- OR **Focus on specific years** initially (e.g., start with Years 3-4 clinical teaching)
- Timeline: 6-12 months for pilot, 2-3 years for full integration

**Next steps:**
1. Review sections scoring <15 points - these need strengthening
2. Develop plan to address gaps (e.g., faculty development, secure resources)
3. Secure leadership commitment
4. Choose pilot approach (single module or single year group)
5. Complete pilot application

**Common gaps to address:**
- **Faculty expertise:** Arrange training for faculty (use EQUILIBRIUM training materials)
- **Resources:** Apply for educational innovation funding
- **Clinical placements:** Identify 2-3 supportive sites for pilot

---

### 80-119 points: DEVELOPING READINESS

**Interpretation:** Your institution has interest but significant preparatory work needed before full implementation.

**Recommended approach:**
- **Preparatory phase** (6-12 months) to build capacity
- Then **small-scale pilot** (single module or workshop series)
- Timeline: 1 year preparation, then 1-2 year pilot

**Next steps:**
1. Focus on building foundations first:
   - Secure leadership buy-in (present evidence, audit data, case studies)
   - Develop faculty champions (identify 2-3 interested faculty)
   - Secure resources (even small amounts for pilot)
2. Start small:
   - Single workshop series (e.g., 4 × 2-hour sessions for one year group)
   - OR integrate into existing module (e.g., add 4-6 hours to psychiatry block)
3. Demonstrate success, then expand

**Priority areas to strengthen:**
- **Leadership support:** Present case studies, evidence base, and audit data showing current prescribing patterns
- **Faculty champions:** Identify 1-2 enthusiastic faculty members who can advocate
- **Resources:** Start with minimal resource pilot (use existing teaching time)

---

### Below 80 points: LOW READINESS

**Interpretation:** Significant barriers exist. EQUILIBRIUM implementation is not currently feasible without major changes.

**Recommended approach:**
- **Not recommended to proceed** with implementation at this time
- Focus on building readiness over 1-2 years
- Revisit this assessment in 12 months

**Next steps:**
1. Identify the major barriers (sections scoring <10 points)
2. Develop a 1-2 year plan to address these:
   - **If leadership barrier:** Build case for change (present data, evidence, visit schools that have implemented)
   - **If faculty barrier:** Invest in faculty development, recruit new faculty with relevant expertise
   - **If resource barrier:** Explore funding opportunities, partnerships
3. Start with awareness-raising:
   - Grand rounds on over-prescribing and holistic interventions
   - Journal club on EQUILIBRIUM evidence base
   - Student interest group on lifestyle medicine
4. Reassess readiness in 12 months

## Addressing Common Gaps

### Gap: Limited Faculty Expertise in Lifestyle Interventions

**Solutions:**
1. Use EQUILIBRIUM faculty training materials (2-day intensive course available)
2. Partner with faculty from sports medicine, nutrition, or preventive medicine departments
3. Invite external guest speakers initially
4. Faculty self-study using EQUILIBRIUM online modules
5. Send 2-3 faculty to external training, who then train others (train-the-trainer)

**Timeline:** 3-6 months for faculty development

---

### Gap: Curriculum Already Overcrowded

**Solutions:**
1. EQUILIBRIUM is integrated, not added - it replaces some current content
2. Identify low-value content that can be reduced (e.g., excessive psychopharmacology detail)
3. Use flipped classroom (online modules for knowledge, face-to-face for skills)
4. Integrate across multiple disciplines (not just psychiatry - also in GP, public health, etc.)
5. Use clinical placements for experiential learning (not all classroom-based)

**Key principle:** EQUILIBRIUM doesn't add 40 hours - it redistributes existing mental health teaching hours to be more balanced.

---

### Gap: Limited Resources (Time and Funding)

**Solutions:**
1. Start small (pilot with single year group or module)
2. Use existing teaching slots (integrate, don't add)
3. Use EQUILIBRIUM ready-made materials (reduces development time)
4. Apply for medical education innovation grants
5. Partner with local NHS organizations (may provide funding/faculty)
6. Demonstrate ROI (improved student outcomes, better GMC alignment, reduced future healthcare costs)

**Minimal viable pilot:** Can be delivered with <£5,000 and 0.2 FTE faculty time for development.

---

### Gap: Resistant Faculty ("Depression needs medication, this is unrealistic")

**Solutions:**
1. Present evidence base (Cochrane reviews, RCTs) - data changes minds
2. Present NICE guidelines - emphasize we're teaching guideline-concordant care
3. Share audit data showing current prescribing is 67% non-concordant
4. Emphasize EQUILIBRIUM doesn't ban medication - it teaches balanced approach
5. Start with faculty champions (early adopters) - let success spread
6. Involve GP faculty who see consequences of inappropriate prescribing
7. Share patient testimonials (many patients prefer non-medication approaches)

**Key message:** "We're teaching what NICE guidelines say, not ideology."

---

### Gap: Clinical Placements Don't Model Holistic Approach

**Solutions:**
1. Select 2-3 supportive GP practices for pilot student placements
2. Provide training for clinical supervisors (half-day workshop)
3. Create student-led activities (students can teach patients lifestyle interventions)
4. Use reflective practice (students reflect on how they would approach cases differently)
5. Use simulated patients/OSCEs for assessment (if placements inconsistent)

**Doesn't require all placements to change** - start with supportive sites.

## Reassessment

We recommend reassessing readiness:
- **Every 6 months** if in preparation phase
- **Annually** once implementation begun
- **After major changes** (new leadership, curriculum review, etc.)

Track your progress over time - readiness should increase as you build capacity.

---

## Need Help?

If your assessment reveals significant gaps and you're not sure how to proceed:

1. **Review case studies** - see how other schools addressed similar barriers
2. **Connect with schools that have implemented** - learn from their experience
3. **Start smaller** - consider workshop series or single module pilot
4. **Seek external support** - educational consultants, EQUILIBRIUM team

**Remember:** High readiness is ideal, but moderate readiness is sufficient to start. The key is honest assessment of gaps and a plan to address them.
