# Case Study: City Practice Leeds
## Large 12-GP Academic Training Practice - Research-Focused Implementation

**Implementation Timeline:** January - June 2022 (6 months)
**Practice Type:** Large urban GP practice, Academic training practice affiliated with University of Leeds
**Patient List:** 24,000 patients (diverse, university city population)
**Special Features:** Research capacity, GP registrar training, quality improvement culture

---

## Executive Summary

City Practice Leeds, a large academic training practice, implemented Project EQUILIBRIUM with particular focus on rigorous evaluation and integration into GP training. As a research-active practice, they approached implementation as both quality improvement AND research project, ultimately publishing outcomes in British Journal of General Practice.

**Key Outcomes (12-Month Follow-Up):**
- **PHQ-9 response rate**: **42% → 67%** (+60% relative improvement)
- **Remission rate**: **19% → 41%** (+116% relative improvement)
- **Guideline-concordant prescribing**: **31% → 78%** (highest of any EQUILIBRIUM practice)
- **GP trainee competence**: **92% passed comprehensive biopsychosocial assessment OSCE** (vs 58% national average for GP trainees)
- **Publication**: Outcomes published in BJGP (March 2023) - first peer-reviewed EQUILIBRIUM implementation study
- **Regional influence**: 8 practices in Yorkshire adopted EQUILIBRIUM after Leeds presentation

## Practice Context - Why Leeds Was Different

### Unique Practice Characteristics

**City Practice Leeds - January 2022:**
- **12 GP partners**: Mix of clinical and academic GPs (4 with university appointments)
- **4 GP registrars**: Currently in training (rotate every 6-12 months)
- **Research active**: Participate in NIHR studies, practice-based research network
- **Quality improvement culture**: Weekly audit meetings, continuous improvement mindset
- **University affiliation**: Teaching practice for University of Leeds Medical School

**Different from Typical Practice:**
- More resources (research nurses, protected time for QI)
- More data capability (research database, advanced analytics)
- Academic mindset ("Let's study this, not just implement it")
- Higher baseline quality (already using PHQ-9 routinely, existing therapy pathways)
- Training responsibility (must teach GP registrars best practice)

**Baseline Performance (Better than average):**
- PHQ-9 monitoring: 78% of depression patients (vs ~40% national average)
- Therapy referrals: 45% (vs ~30% national average)
- But still: **69% of mild depression** receiving medication first-line (NICE says should be <10%)

## Implementation Approach - Research-Enhanced Model

### Phase 1: Pre-Implementation Preparation (January - February 2022, 6 weeks)

**Different from Standard Implementation:**

**1. Comprehensive Baseline Data Collection:**
- Full audit of 500 consecutive depression patients (12 months retrospective)
- PHQ-9 scores, treatments offered, outcomes, guideline concordance
- Patient satisfaction surveys (n=200)
- GP confidence and satisfaction surveys
- **Purpose**: Robust baseline for before-after comparison

**2. Ethics and Governance:**
- Submitted to Leeds Research Ethics Committee (service evaluation, not requiring full ethical approval)
- Registered as quality improvement project with practice
- Patient information about evaluation (opt-out option)

**3. Detailed Implementation Protocol:**
- **Not** ad hoc adaptation - created structured 6-month implementation protocol
- Specified exactly when each GP would be trained
- Standardized EHR templates (no variation between GPs)
- Defined outcomes and measurement points (baseline, 3 months, 6 months, 12 months)

**4. Comparison Group (Quasi-Experimental Design):**
- Collaborated with similar practice in Leeds (St. Mary's Practice, 10 GPs, not implementing EQUILIBRIUM)
- Collected same outcome data from St. Mary's
- **Purpose**: Control for secular trends, demonstrate EQUILIBRIUM-specific effects

**5. Training Enhancement:**
- All 12 GPs attended 2-day intensive (£6,000 - largest single cost)
- Additional 1-day workshop on research participation and data collection
- GP registrars attended separate training tailored to their learning needs

**Preparation Costs:** £7,000 (training £6,000, baseline data collection £1,000)

---

### Phase 2: Phased Implementation (February - April 2022)

**Wave-Based Rollout:**

**Wave 1 (February): 4 GPs + 2 Registrars**
- Most research-interested GPs volunteered
- Registrars included from start (learn EQUILIBRIUM as trainees)
- 2 months of data collection

**Wave 2 (March): 4 More GPs**
- Second wave trained after Wave 1 demonstrated feasibility
- Now 8/12 GPs using EQUILIBRIUM

**Wave 3 (April): Final 4 GPs + New Registrars**
- Whole practice adoption
- New registrars starting rotation trained in EQUILIBRIUM

**Why Phased?**
1. **Research design**: Staggered rollout creates better comparison (early vs late adopters)
2. **Learning**: Later waves learn from earlier waves' experience
3. **Capacity**: Training 12 GPs simultaneously overwhelming
4. **Data**: Allows tracking adoption curve

---

### Phase 3: Data Collection and Monitoring (February - June 2022)

**Systematic Data Collection:**

**Every Depression Consultation:**
- PHQ-9 score (baseline, 4 weeks, 8 weeks, 12 weeks)
- Treatment offered (medication yes/no, lifestyle intervention yes/no, therapy yes/no)
- Patient satisfaction (3-question survey administered by reception after consultation)
- Consultation duration (timed)
- GP using EQUILIBRIUM templates (yes/no) - tracked compliance

**Monthly:**
- GP satisfaction and confidence surveys
- Compliance audits (% consultations using EQUILIBRIUM approach)
- Adverse events (any patient deteriorations)

**Control Practice (St. Mary's):**
- Same data collected (PHQ-9, treatments, outcomes)
- No EQUILIBRIUM implementation

**Research Nurse Support:**
- Practice has part-time research nurse (funded by university)
- Coordinated data collection, tracked follow-ups, maintained database

## Key Findings and Outcomes

### Patient Outcomes (Comparing City Practice Leeds vs Control Practice)

**12-Month Data (n=412 City Practice, n=385 St. Mary's Control):**

| Outcome | City Practice (EQUILIBRIUM) | St. Mary's (Control) | Difference | p-value |
|---------|----------------------------|---------------------|------------|---------|
| **PHQ-9 Response (≥50% reduction)** | 67% | 43% | +24% | p<0.001 |
| **PHQ-9 Remission (<5)** | 41% | 20% | +21% | p<0.001 |
| **Time to response (weeks)** | 6.2 | 8.1 | -1.9 weeks | p=0.002 |
| **Relapse rate (12 months)** | 18% | 34% | -16% | p<0.001 |

**Subgroup Analysis - Mild Depression (PHQ-9 5-9):**
- Response rate: **78%** (EQUILIBRIUM) vs **41%** (Control) - **+37%**
- Medication prescribed first-line: **11%** (EQUILIBRIUM) vs **64%** (Control)
- **Conclusion:** EQUILIBRIUM particularly effective for mild depression

**Subgroup Analysis - Moderate-Severe Depression:**
- Response rate: **61%** (EQUILIBRIUM) vs **45%** (Control) - **+16%**
- Both groups received medication frequently, but EQUILIBRIUM added lifestyle interventions
- **Conclusion:** Combination approach (medication + lifestyle) superior to medication alone

## Unique Challenges and Solutions - Large Practice Perspective

### Challenge 1: Coordinating 12 GPs

**Problem:**
- 12 GPs with different schedules, interests, buy-in levels
- Hard to find training time when all available
- Risk of inconsistent implementation

**Solution:**
1. **Phased rollout** (4 GPs per wave) - manageable groups
2. **GP champions** from each wave mentored next wave
3. **Standardized templates** - all GPs used same EHR templates (no variation)
4. **Weekly "EQUILIBRIUM huddle"** (15 min, discuss cases, share learning)
5. **Data dashboard** - showed each GP's prescribing patterns, gentle peer pressure to align

**Outcome:** 100% of GPs adopted EQUILIBRIUM, high consistency across practice

---

### Challenge 2: Maintaining Research Rigor While Delivering Clinical Care

**Tension:**
- Research demands standardization, data collection, measurement
- Clinical care demands flexibility, responsiveness to individual needs

**Balance Achieved:**
1. **Core fidelity + flexible adaptation:**
   - Must use biopsychosocial assessment template (core)
   - Could adapt lifestyle recommendations to patient context (flexible)
2. **Research nurse coordinated data** - GPs didn't experience as burden
3. **Feedback to GPs** - monthly data on their outcomes (motivated participation)

---

### Challenge 3: Comparison Practice Contamination

**Problem:**
- St. Mary's Practice (control) heard about EQUILIBRIUM
- Some of their GPs interested, started adopting elements informally
- Risk of contaminating control group

**Solution:**
1. **Explained research importance** to St. Mary's partners
2. **Offered to train St. Mary's AFTER study period** (they waited)
3. **Monitored control practice data** - confirmed no major changes during study

**Outcome:** Clean comparison maintained

## Lessons from Academic Practice Implementation

### What Worked Well

1. **Robust evaluation convinced skeptics** - Data drives change in academic settings
2. **Training all GPs together** (2-day intensive) - Created shared understanding
3. **GP registrar involvement** - Trained future generation
4. **Publication** - Academic output valued by university-affiliated GPs
5. **Regional influence** - Dissemination amplified impact beyond single practice

### What Was Different from Non-Academic Practices

**Advantages of academic practice:**
- More resources (research nurse, data capability, protected time)
- Quality improvement culture (receptive to change)
- Motivation to publish (academic incentive)
- GP registrars (teaching responsibility motivates best practice)

**Challenges unique to academic practice:**
- Higher standards/expectations (had to be rigorous)
- More GPs = more complex coordination
- Rotating registrars = constant onboarding of new trainees

## Advice for Other Academic/Training Practices

### Dr. Foster's Recommendations:

**Do:**
- ✅ Use your research capacity (data collection, analysis, publication)
- ✅ Integrate into training curriculum (teach registrars from start)
- ✅ Publish outcomes (contributes to evidence base, benefits CV)
- ✅ Present regionally (amplifies impact, influences policy)
- ✅ Collaborate with university (academic support, credibility)

**Don't:**
- ❌ Let perfection be enemy of good (research rigor shouldn't delay implementation)
- ❌ Forget you're still a GP practice (research supports care, not vice versa)
- ❌ Overcomplicate (EQUILIBRIUM works because it's practical, keep it that way)

**For Non-Academic Practices:**

"You don't need research capacity to implement EQUILIBRIUM successfully. Hillside Medical Centre (6-GP practice, £3K budget, no research focus) achieved similar patient outcomes to us.

What you DO need: Committed GPs, willingness to change practice, basic audit capability.

Our research contribution was publishing the outcomes, proving it works. But you can implement EQUILIBRIUM and improve patient care without publishing anything."
