# Case Study: Hillside Medical Centre
## Urban 6-GP Practice Successfully Implements Holistic Depression Management

**Implementation Timeline:** September 2021 - January 2022 (4 months)
**Practice Type:** Urban 6-GP practice, standard NHS contract
**Patient List:** 12,000 patients (diverse, deprived urban area)

---

## Executive Summary

Hillside Medical Centre, a typical urban GP practice in Manchester, successfully implemented Project EQUILIBRIUM's holistic approach to depression management over 4 months. Starting with 2 pilot GPs, the practice expanded to whole-practice adoption, demonstrating significant improvements in patient outcomes, prescribing quality, and GP satisfaction.

**Key Outcomes (12-Month Follow-Up):**
- **PHQ-9 response rate** (≥50% reduction): **44% → 63%** (+43% relative improvement)
- **Antidepressant prescribing for mild depression** (first-line): **61% → 18%** (-70% relative reduction)
- **Patient satisfaction** with depression care: **72% → 89%**
- **GP satisfaction**: "Finally feel like I'm treating the whole person, not just writing prescriptions"
- **Guideline-concordant prescribing**: **29% → 71%** of depression management aligned with NICE guidelines

## Practice Context and Motivation

### Practice Profile

**Hillside Medical Centre - September 2021:**
- **6 GP partners:** Mix of ages and experience (2 partners nearing retirement, 2 mid-career, 2 recently qualified)
- **Patient demographics:** Urban, ethnically diverse, 40% from most deprived quintile (IMD)
- **Depression prevalence:** ~800 patients on depression register (6.7% of list)
- **Antidepressant prescribing:** High volume, many long-term prescriptions (5+ years)

**Practice Culture:**
- Committed to high-quality care but feeling overwhelmed
- Traditional approach to mental health ("refer to psychiatry or prescribe")
- Some GPs frustrated with limited options
- Practice manager proactive about quality improvement

---

### Why They Implemented EQUILIBRIUM

**The Catalyst (August 2021):**

Dr. Emma Chen, a mid-career GP partner, attended a regional primary care conference where EQUILIBRIUM was presented. She was struck by:
1. **Audit data:** 67% of UK antidepressant prescribing non-guideline-concordant (thought: "That's probably us")
2. **Evidence base:** Exercise as effective as medication (effect size 0.62) - "I had no idea"
3. **Patient preferences:** Many patients prefer non-medication approaches - "Matches what I hear in consultations"

**Internal Audit (August 2021):**

Dr. Chen proposed practice audit of depression management. Results shocked partners:
- **61% of mild depression** patients received antidepressant as first-line (NICE guideline: should be <10%)
- **Only 18%** of depression patients had biopsychosocial assessment documented
- **12%** were offered lifestyle interventions
- **44%** achieved PHQ-9 response at 12 weeks (national average ~45%, below EQUILIBRIUM target of 60%)

**Partner Meeting (September 2021):**

Dr. Chen presented EQUILIBRIUM proposal:
- "We're violating NICE guidelines daily"
- "Our patients aren't getting better at the rate they should"
- "I want more tools than just prescription pad"

**Decision:** Pilot EQUILIBRIUM, starting with 2 interested GPs (Dr. Chen and Dr. James Walsh, recently qualified GP). Budget approved: £3,000 from practice quality improvement fund.

## Implementation Journey

### Phase 1: Rapid Preparation (September 2021 - 2 weeks)

**Readiness Score:** 42/50 (High Readiness)

**Strengths:**
- 2 enthusiastic GP champions (Dr. Chen and Dr. Walsh)
- Supportive practice manager (Sarah Thompson)
- Partners approved resources
- EHR system (EMIS) with templates capability

**Gaps:**
- Limited budget (£3,000 vs ideal £5,000)
- Other 4 GPs skeptical/uncertain
- No existing IAPT relationship (waiting lists 6+ months)

**Week 1 Activities:**

**Day 1-2:**
- Pilot GPs (Chen and Walsh) completed EQUILIBRIUM online training modules (8 hours total)
- Practice manager set up pilot tracking spreadsheet
- Ordered patient education materials (250 copies across 5 guides)

**Day 3:**
- Practice manager customized EQUILIBRIUM EHR templates for EMIS (4 hours)
- Added local resources (Manchester social prescribing, local exercise referral scheme)
- Tested templates with dummy patients

**Day 4:**
- Half-day staff training workshop (receptionists, admin, 2 practice nurses)
- Staff trained on PHQ-9 administration, signposting resources
- Quick reference cards distributed (IAPT contact, crisis numbers, local social prescribing)

**Day 5:**
- Pilot GPs practice consultations with each other (role-play)
- Identified 10 patients each for pilot week
- Set up daily 15-min debrief slots

**Cost:** £1,500 (online training £200, materials £300, EHR customization £500, staff training £500)

### Phase 3: Midpoint Evaluation and Expansion Decision (November 2021)

**Pilot Data Review (November 2021):**

After 1 month pilot:
- **40 patients** managed using EQUILIBRIUM approach
- **PHQ-9 baseline scores:** Mean 14.2 (moderate depression)
- **Treatments offered:**
  - Lifestyle interventions: 83% (exercise 78%, sleep hygiene 85%, diet 45%)
  - IAPT referral: 68%
  - Medication: 38% (mostly moderate-severe depression, some patient preference)
  - Combination (lifestyle + medication): 25%
- **Patient satisfaction:** 38/40 (95%) rated consultation "excellent" or "good"
- **GP satisfaction:** Both pilot GPs want to continue, feel more effective

**Partner Meeting Decision (November 2021):**

Pilot GPs presented outcomes. Partners impressed by:
- Patient satisfaction (95%)
- High treatment adherence (patients actually following through with lifestyle recommendations)
- GP enthusiasm

**Skeptical GP (Dr. Michael Roberts) question:** "This is fine for young motivated patients, but what about my complex patients with comorbidities?"

**Dr. Chen response:** "That's exactly who benefited most. Mr. J (diabetes, depression, obese) - we addressed diet for both diabetes and depression. He's lost 8kg, PHQ-9 from 16 to 9. Holistic approach works for complex patients."

**Decision:** Expand pilot. Train 2 more GPs (Dr. Roberts - the skeptic, and Dr. Sarah Johnson). Continue for 2 more months, then whole practice.

## Challenges and Solutions

### Challenge 1: "I Don't Have Time for 20-Minute Assessments"

**Initial Concern:**
- GPs worried biopsychosocial assessment would take too long
- 15-minute appointments standard at Hillside

**Solution:**
1. **Book initial assessments as 15 min** (not 20) - created urgency to be efficient
2. **Practice with template** - By week 2, GPs fluent, assessment took 12-15 min
3. **Staged assessment** for complex patients - First visit: core assessment + sleep. Follow-up: diet and exercise details
4. **Reframed value:** "15 min upfront thorough assessment saves 3× 10-min repeat appointments later"

**Outcome:** By month 2, average biopsychosocial assessment time: **14 minutes**

---

### Challenge 2: IAPT Waiting Lists (6 Months)

**Problem:**
- IAPT waiting lists 6 months
- Patients couldn't access therapy quickly
- GPs worried recommending therapy that's not available

**Solutions:**
1. **Low-intensity options:** Offered online CBT (Beating the Blues - free via NHS), guided self-help books
2. **Lifestyle interventions don't require IAPT:** Exercise, sleep, diet - GPs can prescribe directly
3. **Social prescribing:** Manchester has social prescribing link workers - referred patients for group activities, reducing isolation
4. **Rebuilt IAPT relationship:** Practice manager contacted IAPT, negotiated faster pathway for Hillside patients (reduced to 8-10 weeks)

**Outcome:** Only 30% of patients needed IAPT. Others managed with lifestyle interventions or online CBT.

---

### Challenge 3: Skeptical GPs → Champions

**Dr. Roberts (The Skeptic):**

**Initial position:** "Depression is a chemical imbalance. Medication is the treatment. This lifestyle stuff is nice but not real medicine."

**What Changed His Mind:**
1. **Saw patient outcomes:** Mrs. P's dramatic improvement with sleep intervention alone
2. **Read Cochrane reviews:** Exercise effect size 0.62 vs medication 0.30 - evidence convinced him
3. **Patient feedback:** "Doc, this is the first time someone's treated me like a whole person, not just a diagnosis"
4. **Less prescribing felt better:** "I was burned out from feeling like a prescription machine. This is why I became a doctor."

**Dr. Roberts became strongest EQUILIBRIUM advocate** by month 3, presenting at local PCN meeting.

---

### Challenge 4: Patient Expectations ("Just Give Me Pills")

**Scenario:**
Some patients came expecting antidepressant prescription, resistant to "just exercise."

**Dr. Walsh's Approach:**
1. **Validate:** "I hear you're feeling hopeless. That's the depression talking."
2. **Shared decision-making:** "Let me share what the research shows, then we decide together."
3. **Show evidence:** "Exercise is as effective as medication - effect size 0.62 vs 0.30. But it takes effort. Medication is easier, but higher relapse rate."
4. **Offer choice:** "We could try exercise for 6 weeks, see if helps. If not, we add medication. Or start both. What feels right to you?"

**Most patients** (80%) willing to try lifestyle-first when presented with evidence and choice.

**For patients insisting on medication:** Prescribed, but also discussed lifestyle interventions. "Medication plus exercise works better than either alone."

## Key Success Factors

### What Made Hillside's Implementation Successful?

1. **Champion GP (Dr. Chen)** - Passionate advocate who drove implementation

2. **Evidence-based approach** - Presented audit data and research, not ideology

3. **Pilot before whole-practice** - 2 GPs proved concept before asking all 6 to change

4. **Supportive practice manager** - Sarah Thompson coordinated logistics, solved problems

5. **Converting the skeptic** - Dr. Roberts' conversion was powerful for other uncertain GPs

6. **Patient feedback** - High satisfaction scores convinced partners this was worthwhile

7. **Rapid implementation** - 4 months from decision to whole-practice adoption maintained momentum

8. **Flexibility** - Adapted to local context (IAPT waiting lists, used alternative resources)

9. **Peer support** - GPs learned from each other, shared successes

10. **Celebration** - Recognized milestones, maintained positivity

## Lessons Learned and Advice

### Dr. Chen's Advice for Practices Considering EQUILIBRIUM

**Do:**
- ✅ Start with audit (data makes the case for change)
- ✅ Pilot with 1-2 enthusiastic GPs (don't try to change everyone at once)
- ✅ Invest in training (online modules sufficient, we did that vs. 2-day course to save money)
- ✅ Support pilot GPs (daily debriefs first week were crucial)
- ✅ Share patient successes (convinced skeptical GPs)

**Don't:**
- ❌ Expect perfection immediately (learning curve first 2 weeks)
- ❌ Skip the pilot (whole-practice rollout would have failed without proof of concept)
- ❌ Underestimate patient enthusiasm (they want holistic care)
- ❌ Forget staff training (reception and admin staff crucial for workflow)
