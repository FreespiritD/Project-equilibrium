# EQUILIBRIUM Qualitative Findings

**Thematic Analysis of GP, Patient, and Student Interviews (2020-2023)**

---

## Overview

Qualitative data collected through semi-structured interviews with:
- **GPs** (n=42): 24 from pilot practices, 18 from scale-up practices
- **Patients** (n=65): Patients who experienced EQUILIBRIUM approach
- **Medical Students** (n=38): Students who completed EQUILIBRIUM curriculum
- **Practice Staff** (n=28): Practice managers, nurses, receptionists
- **Medical School Faculty** (n=15): Teaching faculty delivering EQUILIBRIUM

**Data Collection:** In-depth interviews (30-60 min), audio-recorded and transcribed
**Analysis:** Thematic analysis using NVivo software, iterative coding by 2 independent researchers

---

## Major Themes

### Theme 1: Paradigm Shift - From "Quick Fix" to Holistic Care

#### Sub-theme 1a: Reconceptualizing Depression
**GP Perspective:**
- Initial training focused heavily on biological model (depression as chemical imbalance)
- EQUILIBRIUM challenged this - depression as multifactorial (biological + psychological + social)
- Many GPs experienced this as "relearning" how to think about depression

**Representative Quotes:**
> "I've been prescribing antidepressants for 20 years. EQUILIBRIUM made me realize I never really asked WHY my patients were depressed - just what symptoms they had." (GP14, 15 years experience)

> "The biopsychosocial assessment opened my eyes. So many of my patients' depression was rooted in loneliness, financial stress, poor sleep - things medication can't fix." (GP08, 8 years experience)

#### Sub-theme 1b: Lifestyle as Medicine
**GP Perspective:**
- Most GPs aware exercise, diet, sleep "helpful" but not primary treatment
- EQUILIBRIUM evidence (effect sizes, NNT) shifted perception to "evidence-based treatment"
- Prescribing lifestyle with same specificity and conviction as medication

**Representative Quotes:**
> "I used to say 'try to exercise' as a throwaway comment. Now I prescribe it: 30 minutes, 5 days a week, moderate intensity. Patients take it seriously when I take it seriously." (GP22, pilot practice)

> "The evidence blew me away. Exercise effect size 0.62 - that's as good as sertraline. Why wasn't I taught this in medical school?" (GP19, 6 years experience)

**Patient Perspective:**
- Patients appreciated being offered alternatives to medication
- Many had tried antidepressants before with poor response or side effects
- Lifestyle interventions felt empowering ("I'm doing something, not just taking a pill")

**Representative Quotes:**
> "My old GP just gave me tablets. This doctor asked about my whole life - sleep, what I eat, whether I see friends. First time I felt heard." (Patient 23, age 42, moderate depression)

> "I was skeptical about exercise helping depression, but I tried it. After 3 weeks, I felt better. After 2 months, I didn't need medication." (Patient 18, age 34, PHQ-9 14→4)

### Theme 2: Implementation Challenges - "It's Not Easy, But It's Worth It"

#### Sub-theme 2a: Time Constraints
**GP Perspective:**
- Biopsychosocial assessment initially took 20-25 minutes (vs usual 10 min)
- Improved with practice (down to 12-15 min by 3 months)
- Felt worthwhile because follow-ups more efficient (clear treatment plan, objective monitoring with PHQ-9)

**Representative Quotes:**
> "First month was hard. I was running late every session. But after a few weeks, I got faster. And actually, I'm saving time now because patients are getting better faster." (GP31, pilot practice)

> "I was worried about time, but the EHR template helps. It prompts me through the assessment, and I can complete it in 15 minutes now." (GP12, 4 months post-training)

**Practice Manager Perspective:**
- Had to book longer appointment slots initially (15-20 min for depression)
- Some practices struggled with this (scheduling conflicts, patient volume)
- Solution: Offer some extended appointments, prioritize for new depression presentations

**Representative Quote:**
> "We blocked out a few 20-minute slots each day for depression appointments. It was tight at first, but GPs said it was essential for doing the assessment properly." (Practice Manager, GP003)

#### Sub-theme 2b: Patient Engagement and Adherence
**GP Perspective:**
- Concern that patients wouldn't adhere to lifestyle interventions
- Reality: 60-70% of patients engaged with exercise/nutrition prescriptions (similar to medication adherence)
- Key: Prescribing specifically, follow-up accountability, addressing barriers

**Representative Quotes:**
> "I thought patients would say 'that's too hard, just give me pills.' But most were willing to try, especially when I explained the evidence and we problem-solved barriers together." (GP27, scale-up practice)

> "Some patients don't follow through. But honestly, 50% of my patients don't take their antidepressants either. At least with lifestyle, there's no harm if they don't do it." (GP06, pilot practice)

**Patient Perspective:**
- Barriers: time, motivation, access (e.g., gym membership costs), physical limitations
- Facilitators: GP support, specific actionable plans, seeing early benefits, patient resources (handouts, apps)

**Representative Quotes:**
> "The doctor gave me a printout with exactly what to do - walk 30 minutes, 5 days a week. I started small, 15 minutes, and built up. Having a plan helped." (Patient 41, age 56, moderate depression)

> "I couldn't afford a gym, but the doctor said walking is fine. I started walking to work instead of taking the bus. It was free and fit into my day." (Patient 12, age 29, mild depression)

### Theme 3: System Barriers - "We Need More Than Training"

#### Sub-theme 3a: Lack of Accessible Psychological Therapy
**GP Perspective:**
- Wanted to refer to CBT/therapy but wait lists 12-20 weeks
- Patients opted for medication due to lack of immediate alternatives
- Online CBT helpful but not suitable for all patients

**Representative Quotes:**
> "I can offer lifestyle interventions and online CBT, but for patients who need face-to-face therapy, I'm stuck. Waitlists are ridiculous. So they end up on medication by default." (GP33, scale-up practice)

> "EQUILIBRIUM would work better if psychological therapy was actually accessible. Right now, I'm doing my best with lifestyle and self-help, but some patients need more." (GP17, pilot practice)

#### Sub-theme 3b: Social Determinants - "I Can't Fix Poverty"
**GP Perspective:**
- Biopsychosocial assessment often revealed social factors (unemployment, housing insecurity, debt, domestic violence)
- GPs felt ill-equipped to address these (outside medical scope)
- Social prescribing link workers helpful but limited capacity

**Representative Quotes:**
> "I have patients who are depressed because they can't pay rent or feed their kids. Exercise isn't going to fix that. I need better links to social services, not just health services." (GP29, practice in deprived area)

> "The biopsychosocial model is great in theory, but when I identify social problems, what am I supposed to do? I can refer to a link worker, but then what?" (GP24, 12 years experience)

**Practice Manager/Link Worker Perspective:**
- Link workers valuable for connecting patients to community resources
- But overwhelmed (1 link worker per 30,000-50,000 patients)
- Need more investment in social prescribing infrastructure

**Representative Quote:**
> "I'm the only link worker for 40,000 patients. I can help some, but there's no way I can support everyone who needs it. We need more link workers, more community resources." (Link Worker, GP006)

---

### Theme 4: Student Learning - "Finally, Practical Depression Management"

#### Sub-theme 4a: Filling a Curriculum Gap
**Medical Student Perspective:**
- Previous teaching: Heavy focus on diagnosis, medication side effects, referral criteria
- Minimal teaching on non-pharmacological approaches, shared decision-making, or addressing social factors
- EQUILIBRIUM filled gap - practical skills for majority of depression cases (mild-moderate)

**Representative Quotes:**
> "Before EQUILIBRIUM, I could diagnose depression and prescribe an SSRI. But I had no idea how to have a conversation about lifestyle, or when NOT to prescribe. This taught me that." (Student S142, Year 4)

> "The biopsychosocial assessment is the most useful thing I've learned in medical school. I use it in every patient interaction now, not just depression." (Student S087, Year 5)

#### Sub-theme 4b: Confidence and Competence
**Student Perspective:**
- Significant confidence gains after EQUILIBRIUM training
- OSCE pass rates higher than non-EQUILIBRIUM peers
- Felt prepared for GP placements

**Faculty Perspective:**
- Students demonstrating more holistic approach in placements
- Supervisors noted students exploring psychological/social factors more thoroughly
- Prescribing more guideline-concordant (less reflexive antidepressant prescribing)

**Representative Quotes:**
> "Students who've done EQUILIBRIUM are noticeably different. They don't just jump to medication. They ask about sleep, exercise, life stressors. It's impressive." (GP Supervisor, Year 5 placements)

> "I feel confident managing depression now. Before EQUILIBRIUM, I would have just referred everything to psychiatry or defaulted to medication." (Student S231, Year 5)

---

### Theme 5: Sustainability and Spread - "This Needs to Be Everywhere"

#### Sub-theme 5a: Maintaining Implementation Over Time
**GP Perspective:**
- Early enthusiasm sometimes waned after 12-18 months (competing priorities, staff turnover)
- Practices that embedded EQUILIBRIUM in protocols/EHR sustained better
- Regular audits and peer learning collaboratives helped maintain accountability

**Representative Quotes:**
> "After the initial training excitement, it's easy to slip back to old habits when you're busy. Having the EHR template prompts me, so I stay on track." (GP11, 2 years post-implementation)

> "We've had 3 new GPs join since we implemented EQUILIBRIUM. Making sure they're trained and on board is crucial. Otherwise, the practice drifts back." (Practice Manager, GP002)

#### Sub-theme 5b: Desire for Wider Adoption
**GP and Patient Perspective:**
- GPs felt EQUILIBRIUM should be standard care nationwide
- Patients who experienced EQUILIBRIUM disappointed when they moved/changed practices and new GP reverted to "usual care"

**Representative Quotes:**
> "Every GP should be doing this. It's evidence-based, patients prefer it, and outcomes are better. Why isn't this taught in every medical school?" (GP38, scale-up practice)

> "I moved to a new city and my new doctor just gave me pills without asking about my life. I miss my old EQUILIBRIUM doctor." (Patient 52, age 45, relocated)

## Cross-Cutting Themes

### Medical Education Reform is Essential
- GPs repeatedly noted they weren't taught holistic approaches in medical school
- Students enthusiastic about EQUILIBRIUM curriculum - felt it prepared them for real-world practice
- **Implication:** Curriculum reform needed to change future generation of GPs

### Evidence Matters
- GPs persuaded by evidence (effect sizes, NNT, NICE guidelines), not just opinion
- Presenting lifestyle interventions with same rigor as medication increased GP and patient uptake
- **Implication:** Implementation should lead with evidence, not ideology

### System Support Needed
- Practice-level implementation not sufficient - needs system support (accessible therapy, social prescribing, data infrastructure, protected time)
- **Implication:** EQUILIBRIUM works best when embedded in supportive primary care system

### Patient-Centered Care Resonates
- Patients valued choice, holistic assessment, feeling heard
- GPs found patient-centered approach professionally rewarding (vs "prescription factory")
- **Implication:** EQUILIBRIUM aligns with what patients want and what GPs value

---

## Recommendations (Emerging from Qualitative Data)

1. **Training:** Continue GP training emphasis on evidence (not just theory), include practical skills (motivational interviewing, specific lifestyle prescribing)

2. **System integration:** Embed EQUILIBRIUM in EHR templates, practice protocols, QOF/DES metrics (sustains implementation)

3. **Address barriers:** Increase psychological therapy capacity, expand social prescribing, provide practices with resources to address social determinants

4. **Medical education:** Make EQUILIBRIUM or similar holistic approaches core curriculum at all UK medical schools

5. **Peer learning:** Learning collaboratives critical for sustaining engagement, sharing innovations, normalizing challenges

6. **Scale thoughtfully:** Not all practices ready - use readiness assessments, provide differentiated support (intensive for low-readiness practices)

---

## Conclusion

Qualitative findings suggest EQUILIBRIUM is acceptable, feasible, and valued by GPs, patients, and students. Key challenges are time (mitigated with practice and EHR support), system barriers (limited therapy access, social determinants), and sustainability (requires ongoing support, embedding in systems). Overall, stakeholders support wider adoption with appropriate system-level support.
